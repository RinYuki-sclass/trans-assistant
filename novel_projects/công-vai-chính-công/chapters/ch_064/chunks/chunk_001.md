Truth be told, Ji Chenxi could’ve just taken all the points in Lu Jianhan’s hands and completely cut off any chance of him turning things around.
Crippling the other guy, snapping his wings so he’d lose any chance to shine in this trial, would’ve been way easier than working together. But the moment Ji Chenxi laid eyes on Lu Jianhan, a different answer had already formed in his mind.
First off, they were all new students who’d just stepped into a new campus—there was no need to go that hard. Second, those points that looked like a big deal right now were nothing compared to building a good relationship with Lu Jianhan. This guy might only seem a bit gifted at the moment, but in the future, he’d become the Empire’s legendary commoner war god. Ji Chenxi didn’t expect to control him or make him work for him; leaving a good impression early was more than enough. So he stuck firmly to his word.
Ji Chenxi’s actions were almost too straightforward, and Lu Jianhan gradually went from wary and suspicious to finally letting his guard drop a little.
This Third Prince was just… different.
Lu Jianhan’s first impression of him was that he was dangerous in a subtle way. A young man with sharp, beautiful features and a constant smile, looking easy to talk to on the surface, yet leaving no room for refusal—his presence carried a quiet threat.
At first, Lu Jianhan thought the guy was trying to kill two birds with one stone: grab the points and snag an extra fighting force, squeezing every bit of value out of him. Who would’ve guessed that the very first thing Ji Chenxi did with him was go catch fish, then light a fire and boil water to make fish soup?
Watching Ji Chenxi calmly tell him to scrape off the scales, clean out the insides, then gather a bunch of things to get rid of the fishy smell, Lu Jianhan’s expression slowly went from disbelief to complete numbness.
Maybe the Third Prince just wanted someone obedient to listen to him and do the dirty work.
With Ji Chenxi giving directions and Lu Jianhan putting in the effort, the fish soup was finally done.
Ji Chenxi’s theory was solid. To make sure he could eat decently during the trial, he’d studied cooking until his knowledge was practically master-level. The problem was that vague phrase “add an appropriate amount.” He could never quite get it right. The grilled meat and mushroom soup he’d made before always tasted strange—not awful, but definitely not good either.
Since his own cooking was only so-so, he didn’t expect Lu Jianhan, working under his guidance, to do any better. But after taking the first sip, Ji Chenxi’s gaze toward Lu Jianhan changed on the spot—not exactly burning with excitement, but a whole lot warmer than his earlier indifference.
“Student Lu, you’ve really got talent in the kitchen,” Ji Chenxi said with a grin, not holding back his praise.
Lu Jianhan: “…”
Or maybe I just already know how to cook.
In the end, Lu Jianhan didn’t say what he was really thinking. Instead, he quietly handed over the mushrooms he’d just finished grilling, suffering in silence like a proper workhorse.
Ji Chenxi’s look toward him grew even more approving. He felt this guy really knew how to read the room.
After feeding a certain noble prince, Lu Jianhan—injured and all—followed Ji Chenxi as they went on a wild spree, robbing others of their points.
Snatching points was fast work. In just a few days, they’d piled up a ridiculous amount. One of them had spiritual power that could mess with people’s minds, while the other’s was straight-up lethal. Add in their sharp reflexes and quick hands, and most people wandering alone had no choice but to hand over their points the moment they ran into them.
As they robbed more people, their teamwork got smoother too. Sometimes, Ji Chenxi and Lu Jianhan even went after small groups traveling together.
