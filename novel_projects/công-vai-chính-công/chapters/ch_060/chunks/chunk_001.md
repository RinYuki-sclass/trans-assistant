Still being kissed, Ji Chenxi was the first to laugh, “What if I say no? What are you going to do then?”
Chi Zhuo looked seriously into Ji Chenxi’s eyes, seeing his own reflection in those blue depths. Up close, the blue wasn’t just like the deep sea—there were layers of color hidden within: purples, blacks, and even hints of yellow. Now, a faint mist hovered in them, making those eyes less like an ocean and more like a starry sky—deep, captivating, impossible to look away from.
“What do you think I should do?” Chi Zhuo asked, tilting Ji Chenxi’s face up and leaving a kiss right on his eye.
As Chi Zhuo leaned in, Ji Chenxi had already instinctively shut that eye, so the kiss landed just on his eyelid.
The intimate gesture made Ji Chenxi blush with embarrassment, almost shy enough not to tease him back. He only let the tail of his words rise playfully, like a little hook: “How should I know what you’re going to do, Brother Chi Zhuo? You’re not exactly honest.”
“I’d say I’ve been pretty honest,” Chi Zhuo replied, “but maybe you should be honest too—tell me where you got that dating guide from.”
“Oh, that~” Ji Chenxi dragged out the word, “that’s a long story.”
“You can make it short if you want,” Chi Zhuo countered.
“Short version? I didn’t really read any dating guide before,” Ji Chenxi confessed with a laugh, laying his cards on the table.
He couldn’t keep pretending anymore—he was afraid that if he kept acting like he had it all figured out, he’d get slapped in the face later. And if Chi Zhuo got interested in this so-called dating guide and went looking for it… that would really be a loss. Ji Chenxi didn’t even want to think about the online love guides for another second.
Chi Zhuo fell into an awkward silence for a long moment after Ji Chenxi’s sudden confession.
He reached up and touched Ji Chenxi’s chin. “Weren’t you acting like you understood everything just now? How come you’re denying it so quickly?”
Being teased, Ji Chenxi didn’t flinch. “Even if I didn’t read some dating guide, I still understand it better than you, Brother Chi Zhuo.”
“Then why don’t you tell me what should or shouldn’t be done when sending someone home?”
“I obviously know that,” Ji Chenxi replied, “but if I just tell you, wouldn’t it seem like I’m telling you what to do?”
A faint, almost unnoticeable smile curved Chi Zhuo’s lips. “That’s true, but now that you’ve said it, won’t it make every future time go smoothly?”
“Well, that’s reasonable.”
Chi Zhuo waited a moment but didn’t get any more from him. He turned to look at Ji Chenxi. “Your Highness?”
“I just said it’s reasonable,” Ji Chenxi said casually, “didn’t say I’d tell you. Compared to laying out the answers so you’d get a smooth result every time, I’d rather have you guess on your own, Brother Chi Zhuo.”
“I understand.”
