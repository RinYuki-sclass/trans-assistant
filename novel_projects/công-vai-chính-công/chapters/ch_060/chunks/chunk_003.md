Chi Zhuo stared after him, watching Ji Chenxi’s figure fade. Soon, someone came to greet him at the estate gates, and Ji Chenxi, who had walked quite a distance, stepped back into his floating car.
Was he trying to walk farther? Of course not. He just wanted to spend a little more time with him.
Ji Chenxi always seemed to do things—half intentionally, half by chance—that made Chi Zhuo’s heart skip.
Throughout their time together, neither of them mentioned the spiritual form still held in Ji Chenxi’s hand, as if the Black Mamba didn’t even exist.
A month passed in the blink of an eye, and suddenly it was time for the trial at the Royal Academy.
At first, Ji Chenxi thought this was just the Royal Academy’s own trial. After all, in the original story, the First Military Academy had gone with a completely different type of trial. The two top universities hadn’t cooperated at all. But perhaps Ji Chenxi had messed with the storyline too much—now it seemed he had to fix it himself. To his surprise, the Royal Academy and the First Academy had decided to hold this freshman trial together.
Among the new students, he spotted Bai Anran, who had made a small effort to tidy up his face. Ji Chenxi wasn’t surprised in the slightest. He just nodded lightly at him, calm and composed.
Seeing a familiar face, Bai Anran paled slightly. He had risked coming to the First Military Academy because almost everyone there was a civilian, and his chosen field made it very unlikely he’d run into anyone he knew. Who could have guessed that the First Academy and Royal Academy would quietly organize a sort of “meet-and-greet”?
Ji Chenxi gave Bai Anran a reassuring glance and then didn’t pay him any more attention.
Thanks to what had happened with Que Qi, a lot of online fans already knew his true identity. Plus, there were several freshmen he could casually chat with, so it wasn’t long before a crowd gathered around him. Many even wanted to team up with him for the upcoming trial.
At first, Ji Chenxi didn’t take the trial seriously at all—just thought it was childish play. But once it actually started, he found it kind of interesting. Most trials usually start by making the candidates cooperate, and only after everyone’s progress has been leveled out do they start competing.
This time, however, the trial kicked off directly with a point-grabbing competition.
Those around Ji Chenxi hesitated at first, but no one expected him to act immediately and decisively, striking first.
While the freshmen fought among themselves, there was also an external threat: the top graduates recruited by the Royal Academy. At first, Ji Chenxi only “counterattacked” one senior and successfully grabbed the two points he was carrying. But that single move caught the attention of that senior, his friends, and their extended circle.
Right now, Ji Chenxi hated people with ridiculously good connections more than anything.
As he ran and dodged, Ji Chenxi was already planning how to take out the whole group in one go. Hidden in the shadows, quietly counting the right moment to strike, he suddenly sensed a familiar presence again.
The only difference this time was that the presence was far away. Ji Chenxi released a faint thread of his spiritual energy in response.
Then he crouched again, waiting for the right moment. When he responded to Chi Zhuo, Chi Zhuo’s spiritual energy directly linked to that faint trace he had sent out.
[How’s it going?]
Ji Chenxi answered honestly: [Surrounded by over a dozen people.]
