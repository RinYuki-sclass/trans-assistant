Death was right at his throat, yet Chi Zhuo didn’t even blink.
In the thick, almost choking smell of blood, his dark eyes stayed locked straight on Ji Chenxi.
Ji Chenxi’s face was flushed in a way that didn’t look normal. His hand brushed against Chi Zhuo’s neck, almost in a teasing, intimate way—his skin burning hot, the dagger in his grip cold and flashing with a sharp gleam. The mix of heat and steel gave off a strange feeling, something both seductive and unsettling.
Ji Chenxi narrowed his eyes slightly as he moved the dagger. It didn’t look like he was trying to scare him—more like he was figuring out where to start. While Chi Zhuo watched him, the dagger finally seemed to find an angle that pleased him. The sharp tip pressed lightly against the skin, ready to make the first cut.
As if realizing something, Chi Zhuo suddenly grabbed Ji Chenxi’s hand, taking the threatening dagger away, and let out a quiet sigh.
“Your Highness, don’t mess around.”
If he still couldn’t tell what Ji Chenxi was trying to do, then all his careful observation would’ve been pointless. The man clearly wanted to carve something into his neck—maybe words, maybe a pattern. His mind flashed back to before, when Ji Chenxi had carved a rose into his skin. But right now… how much of Ji Chenxi’s reason was even left?
“You’re rejecting me?” Ji Chenxi’s tone dropped.
Hearing the displeasure in his voice, Chi Zhuo held down the hand that tried to snatch the dagger back and said, without a hint of fear, “Yeah. If anyone should be upset between us, it should be me. After all, Your Highness still reeks of omega scent. So what—you meant that an alpha being drawn to an omega is just natural?”
Ji Chenxi blinked lightly. “Huh?”
His slow, muddled mind clearly hadn’t expected to be thrown off like that, so he paused for a moment when faced with Chi Zhuo’s question.
His state right now was… strange. He was drifting in a sea of desire—one moment like a drowning man about to sink, the next like a god ruling the ocean.
Reason, desire, calm, frenzy—they twisted together, fighting for control of his body, until what remained was a Ji Chenxi who looked calm on the surface but had long since lost any real control.
“You’re trying to distract me?”
Ji Chenxi asked. Meanwhile, Chi Zhuo had already lifted his hand. His wrist was covered in cuts from the dagger—most of them shallow, no longer bleeding, just layer upon layer of wounds.
The sight made Chi Zhuo’s chest tighten.
Maybe… he shouldn’t have stayed this calm. Calmly setting everything up, then walking in with that same detached composure—like pretending he wasn’t already sinking into this mess.
He lifted that sharply defined wrist and brought it to his lips, pressing down a trail of soft, lingering kisses—gentle to an almost excessive degree.
“Hurting yourself isn’t a good habit.”
Chi Zhuo glanced up at Ji Chenxi, his voice low and rough, like he was holding something back.
