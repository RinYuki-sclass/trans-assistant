Ji Chenxi, of course, had no idea that the guy fighting him was already typing till his fingertips ached, full of disbelief and frustration. To Ji Chenxi, the whole thing was just thrilling. The other person had skill—real skill—and if this were a straight one-on-one, it’d be a tough match. But Ji Chenxi had Bai Han backing him up.
Bai Han, who had clearly decided Ji Chenxi was on her side, quickly jumped in to support him the moment she realized what Ji Chenxi was trying to do.
While Ji Chenxi was happily locked in a cyber duel, Chi Zhuo focused on piloting the mech, steady and precise, heading straight for their destination. With Ji Chenxi clearing the way—and with the right gear—they made it there in less than three minutes.
When the mech came to a stop, Chi Zhuo disengaged the controls. Ji Chenxi, still multitasking like it was nothing, kept up the digital fight while helping Chi Zhuo unlock the door ahead.
As he worked, he glanced over and asked casually, “If you’re really here just for the research data, we might be a little too late.”
They’d spent way more than ten minutes getting here, and ten minutes was plenty of time for the researchers to move their data somewhere else.
Chi Zhuo didn’t seem surprised. He just nodded, like he’d already expected that.
Ji Chenxi caught the look and raised an eyebrow. “So you knew. Then you’re not here for the research data, are you?”
Right as he said that, the stubborn door finally gave way with a heavy clunk.
“I never said I was here for the data,” Chi Zhuo replied lightly. “I came to collect something. If we happen to find the research files too, that’s just a bonus.”
Ji Chenxi paused for a moment, a thoughtful look flickering in his eyes as he caught on to the hint in Chi Zhuo’s words.
Ji Chenxi had zero interest in this kind of human experimentation. So even if the lab held some top-secret research data, he didn’t bother taking a step closer. Instead, he kept himself busy going head-to-head with the people operating behind the scenes. As for Chi Zhuo — the door was already open, and whatever was left in there, he could handle on his own.
Ji Chenxi’s invasion was still going strong. No matter how many firewalls or counterattacks came his way, they only managed to slow him down a little. But just as things were heating up between him and the enemy hacker, a sudden explosion went off in the room Chi Zhuo had just entered.
The loud ‘boom’ almost made Ji Chenxi mistype a command.
He had been completely focused on winning the control battle, but the blast made him pause and glance toward the doorway.
Thick smoke filled the room — too dense to see anything inside. He frowned, raised his voice, and called out, “Chi Zhuo?”
He just needed one response. One word, and he’d go back to tearing through the system. But the room stayed dead quiet. Not a single sound came back.
Ji Chenxi’s expression hardened. He summoned his spiritual form — the snow leopard — which didn’t even need instructions before slipping soundlessly toward the open door.
Because of the distraction, Ji Chenxi started losing ground in the system battle. If he slowed down even a little more, the equipment he was using might just blow itself up in protest.
He split part of his awareness to connect with the snow leopard and continued fighting on both fronts.
