Under those strangely intense eyes, Chi Zhuo still felt the need to explain himself. “No, I think Bai Han saw you in danger and… led you toward me.”
“You mean she took matters into her own hands?”
“Your Highness, you’re a prince of the empire. We can’t just stand by and watch someone die. After all, we are good citizens of the empire, aren’t we?”
Ji Chenxi almost burst out laughing while fleeing. How could this guy say something like that with a straight face? Clearly, he wasn’t the only one with a skewed sense of self.
“Oh, really?” Ji Chenxi said, his voice layered with meaning.
“What do you think it was, then?”
The monsters chasing them had been blocked, and even the road ahead was being cleared. Ji Chenxi didn’t feel as panicked as he did before. He let out a soft laugh. “I don’t really think anything, after all, I’m not Brother Chi Zhuo. How would I know what’s going on in your head?”
Chi Zhuo didn’t respond. Whatever he said now might give Ji Chenxi something to confirm whatever thoughts were running through his mind.
“You’re not normal,” Ji Chenxi added on his own, even though Chi Zhuo didn’t speak.
Chi Zhuo paused for a moment, then acted as if he hadn’t heard it.
Ji Chenxi didn’t continue the small talk either. The lively energy between them from their first meeting vanished completely.
Chi Zhuo glanced at him out of the corner of his eye. Noticing his gaze, Ji Chenxi lifted a corner of his mouth slightly, almost asking, ‘Looking at what?’ Chi Zhuo pulled his eyes away, and Ji Chenxi’s little reaction disappeared instantly.
‘Not normal?’
If anyone was truly “not normal” between them, it was probably Ji Chenxi.
Every time Chi Zhuo thought he understood a bit of him, he’d realize Ji Chenxi was like a deep, bottomless sea. On the surface, beautiful, pure, simple—but in truth, unfathomable.
After teaming up with Chi Zhuo, Ji Chenxi’s luck shot up like a rocket. Just moments ago, he had been down on his luck, and now, with Bai Han’s help, the two of them had stumbled upon a small weapons vault. The outer alloy door opened easily enough, but the inner layer—radiating a semi-transparent blue glow—was something even Bai Han, stationed outside, couldn’t hack.
It was a permission-based lock, not some simple password or facial recognition system. No wonder Bai Han hadn’t forced it open. Clearly, this was the type of door that would self-destruct if anything went wrong.
“Looks like we’ll just have to stare at it and dream,” Ji Chenxi muttered softly.
Chi Zhuo didn’t know much about this kind of tech. Seeing it wouldn’t open, he said, “Let’s try breaking it open with firepower. If that doesn’t work, we should move on to our real goal.”
“What’s the real goal?” Ji Chenxi asked, curious.
