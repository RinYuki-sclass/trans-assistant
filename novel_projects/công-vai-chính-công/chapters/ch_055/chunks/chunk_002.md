“Yeah, you are.”
Su Yu laughed. “Bro, once you’re more familiar with us, don’t call us that! I just can’t handle an alpha calling me like that. But wait… you call Hechuan that—he doesn’t hit you?”
“He does! Before, he really got upset every time I called him that. But now, I guess he’s used to it. And I don’t mean any harm!”
To dare act so freely in front of Gu Hechuan, Lu Tu was definitely the first. Su Yu gradually felt himself warming up to the red-haired youth.
Still, during their outing, Lu Tu’s gaze would now and then drift back to Ji Chenxi. That look—half assessing, half probing—would make most people uncomfortable. Ji Chenxi felt it too, but since the other was a friend he’d known online for years, and they got along well, he simply pretended not to notice the subtle, lingering gaze.
Every time the redhead glanced at Ji Chenxi, Su Yu couldn’t help feeling… complicated. Nervous, even. His friend was already a bit indecisive, and now there was this handsome alpha subtly tempting him? Not a good combination.
Finally, after noticing yet another look Lu Tu shot at Ji Chenxi, Su Yu couldn’t hold back. While dragging him along to buy ice cream, he said, “Stop thinking about it. Ji Chenxi’s not interested in you.”
Lu Tu raised an eyebrow. “I was just glancing at the Third Prince a few times—why do you think I have any funny ideas about him? Was there some alpha before me who openly made a move on the Third Prince?”
Su Yu: “…”
This guy had been dragged along so easily to do all the heavy lifting. Surely he wasn’t trying to fish for information, right?
“Don’t worry about that. You just need to remember one thing: stop thinking nonsense.” Su Yu’s gaze toward the redhead had grown sharp with caution.
“Brother Su, I think you’re misunderstanding. The reason I was looking at the Third Prince is just… he reminds me a lot of a friend I met online.”
Su Yu was half convinced, half skeptical, but Lu Tu went ahead and reassured him, “Relax, I’m not interested in alphas. If I were going to look for one, I’d go for someone I know better—like Brother Gu.”
Su Yu reluctantly believed him. After all, Ji Chenxi’s beauty was undeniable, but personality-wise, he really didn’t have the charm that Gu Hechuan did.
After that, the redhead didn’t pay much attention to Ji Chenxi at all, and Su Yu happily went back to bustling around, keeping everyone entertained.
It was now 7:50.
Ji Chenxi casually glanced at the time, also keeping an eye out for any new messages. There were none. At this hour, Chi Zhuo still hadn’t messaged about when or where to meet at Galaxy Park. That meant Chi Zhuo was completely confident—no matter where he was in the park, he could see him.
Ji Chenxi’s brief sense of anticipation cooled down, logic taking over. If this was going to be a grand, “I can see you no matter where you are in the park” kind of confession, shouldn’t he remind Chi Zhuo to keep it low-key?
Originally, Ji Chenxi just wanted to tell Chi Zhuo that they owed each other a proper confession. If Chi Zhuo wanted to confess, fine; if he didn’t, that was fine too.
At 7:53, Su Yu suggested going on the carousel. The luxurious little horses were always a favorite with kids and couples alike, but Gu Hechuan refused without hesitation.
