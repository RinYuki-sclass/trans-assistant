In truth, it was rather dull.
When he was very, very young—truly a child—Ji Chenxi had been cherished by his father and loved by his mother. He had grown up in a honeyed world, a pampered child, a jewel in his parents’ hands. Even ordinary families wouldn’t dote on an omega so intensely. But all that peace was shattered without his knowledge. His mother had taken him away from the palace, and he had lived with her in that beautiful manor.
Even now, Ji Chenxi didn’t know exactly what had happened between his mother and his father, though he could piece together some clues from the past.
After his mother died, his father’s attitude toward him had been strange—partly loving, partly wrapped in disdain.
He often wondered why the position of imperial crown prince hadn’t gone straight to him, the legitimate son, why his elder brother and second sister had been allowed to stir undercurrents of rivalry. And if there was genuine dislike, why had his father shown that, as long as Ji Chenxi wished it, he could punish his elder brother properly?
It was as if he were being silently encouraged to join the struggle. As long as he wanted to participate, he would have support far stronger than any legion or noble family.
Ji Chenxi frowned in annoyance—he really had no interest in this battle for the throne.
Returning to his studio, pretending to still be absorbed in his work, he found he had completely lost the desire to paint. He had his tools cleaned and then just stared out at the vast swaths of roses outside, lost in thought.
The May roses were in full bloom, and the entire manor was wrapped in their sweet fragrance.
As dusk settled, Ji Chenxi, who had been holed up at home since returning, finally decided to go out for a bit of fun.
Rarely venturing out, he picked a bar he often liked to frequent for a quiet drink. He hadn’t even had more than a few cups before attracting the attention of the ever-energetic Su Yu.
Su Yu, who had long wished for Ji Chenxi to step outside and stop sulking at home, hurriedly waved him over, telling him not to run off. He then led him to a louder, livelier place. Before showing Ji Chenxi the new fun he’d found, Su Yu got pulled into a conversation by a familiar face.
Ji Chenxi wasn’t interested in their chat, so after a quick greeting, he stepped a few paces aside to explore.
In the stairwell, a huge floor-to-ceiling window caught his eye. He paused to admire the view outside, and in that instant, his sharp eyes spotted someone familiar.
It was unmistakably someone he knew.
Seeing that person, weapon in hand and a wary look in their eyes, Ji Chenxi’s frown finally faded. He allowed himself a composed, restrained smile.
