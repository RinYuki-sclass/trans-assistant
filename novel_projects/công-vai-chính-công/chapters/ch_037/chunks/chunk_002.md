Chi Zhuo hadn’t really expected Ji Chenxi to give ‘himself’ away. Just a rose to touch, to smell, to hold—that would’ve been enough. But once again, he was refused.
Chi Zhuo’s eyes darkened a notch, and his hand tightened unconsciously, though his voice stayed as low and cold as ever.
“Does this online friend of yours really exist?”
Ji Chenxi froze for a moment, then fully realized what Chi Zhuo meant.
Was he trying to imply he was being humiliated? That was completely unfair. He really was going to meet his online friend, and the rose was proof. Otherwise, giving Chi Zhuo a rose would have been nothing at all.
“The online friend is real.”
Ji Chenxi tried to tone down his smile, making his expression a little more serious. But the thought that Chi Zhuo might be upset just because he refused to give him the flower made him want to laugh anyway.
Chi Zhuo—wanting a flower?
After all the times they’d met, Chi Zhuo knew Ji Chenxi wouldn’t lie about this. So, he was really going to meet an online friend.
And yet, precisely because of that, Chi Zhuo’s expression looked even worse. Even his question came out heavy and sharp: “What kind of online friend meeting uses a white rose as proof?”
The sudden question almost made Ji Chenxi think Chi Zhuo had some supernatural ability to know he was the Bai Wei from the dark web—but he quickly dismissed that idea.
White roses seemed to symbolize pure love.
They were beautiful and elegant, once seen as a mark of nobility, and at one point had been a favorite among omegas.
So… did Chi Zhuo think the person he was meeting was an omega and got jealous??
Ji Chenxi admitted to himself that the thought was bold, maybe even far-fetched—but also fun. And because it was fun, he ended up spilling a tiny bit of his personal business to Chi Zhuo. “It’s someone I’ve known for many years, and we’re really close online. Brother Chi Zhuo, maybe you should give us a little space?”
Chi Zhuo was… not exactly comfortable with that.
Of course, the person being asked to give space rarely cared about the other’s feelings. Chi Zhuo’s expression darkened, and he moved.
He came from the wall to stand behind Ji Chenxi—less than ten meters, but each step felt like it was pressing on one’s heart, heavy with lethal intent.
Stopping behind the sofa where Ji Chenxi sat, he leaned slightly, lowering his voice. “Give space?”
“Meeting an online friend is dangerous. Better if I go with Your Highness, so no one can trick you. After all… you’re still just a child.”
