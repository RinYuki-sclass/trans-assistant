Truth was, Ji Chenxi wasn’t really that angry. If anything, Chi Zhuo had flustered him a little. He’d honestly thought Chi Zhuo didn’t have any friends—that he was probably the only one who could count as one. Sure, that “friend” now came with the word boyfriend in front of it, but still. It was just a freshman trial, and suddenly Chi Zhuo had someone else he could team up with.
What bothered Ji Chenxi wasn’t only that this new “friend” popped out of nowhere. It was more the feeling of things slipping out of his control—of only finding out after the fact. That subtle sting when his need for control got poked… he had no intention of saying it out loud.
Meanwhile, Chi Zhuo, still tied up, watched Ji Chenxi’s figure fade into the distance. After a long moment, he lowered his head, a helpless yet indulgent smile curling at his lips.
That faint smile wasn’t caught by the recording drone. The leadership at Royal Academy were too busy feeling pleased. As expected of a steady, outstanding graduate—Chi Zhuo handled things with care and balance. Compared to the other side where Lan Mu had basically gone mad, the two were complete opposites.
Just as the school leaders were wondering whether they should send a student to untie Chi Zhuo, a blade formed from condensed spiritual power sliced clean through the ribbon binding his wrists. He rose from the ground, casually rubbing his wrists, then glanced at the recording drone still hovering in the air. The look on his face made it seem like he wanted to crush it in his hand.
The school officials: “…”
This was, without a doubt, the most recording drones they had ever lost in a single trial.
Luckily, Chi Zhuo only looked at it. He didn’t actually make a move.
After leaving Chi Zhuo, Ji Chenxi had a clear goal—go rescue Lu Jianhan and Bai Anran.
From the moment Lu Jianhan entered the underground maze, he had quietly released his spiritual power, spreading it across the entire labyrinth. Keeping that up took almost everything he had, leaving him with barely any strength left to deal with Lan Mu.
His Phantom Butterfly could at best fool Lan Mu for a short while before being exposed. As for Bai Anran, whether it was his spiritual power or his combat skills, he leaned more toward support. He could fight Lan Mu, sure—but the chance of winning was slim. On top of that, Lan Mu’s spiritual power was strange. The mother spider kept spawning countless little spiders. Facing that swarm of densely packed spider spirits, Ji Chenxi couldn’t help but suspect the two of them had already had their points stolen by Lan Mu.
Luckily, Lan Mu loved playing cat and mouse. He enjoyed teasing and wearing down his prey, so he wasn’t in any rush to finish things.
When he saw Ji Chenxi, he lifted a brow slightly. He looked surprised—yet not surprised at all. With a lazy motion, he brushed the messy bangs off his forehead, showing smooth skin beneath. “Looks like Chi failed to catch his cat.”
Lan Mu looked a bit rough around the edges. His clothes hung loose and sloppy, and his hair was a mess. But his face was striking enough that it didn’t come off as careless. Instead, he gave off a laid-back, lazy vibe—carefree in a way that had its own strange charm.
Ji Chenxi narrowed his eyes, though there was still a smile playing on his lips. “Senior Chi was just going easy on me.”
“I don’t think he was going easy,” Lan Mu said lightly. “Teaming up with a Phantom Butterfly gives you a high win rate. Same S-rank, after all. One tiny mistake and you lose. Still…” He tilted his head. “I’ve known Chi for years. I’ve never gotten the better of him. And now he just loses to a little freshman like you? Yeah. That doesn’t sit well with me.”
Ji Chenxi didn’t reply. He simply watched him quietly. Sure enough, right after saying it didn’t sit well, Lan Mu made his move.
Lan Mu was best at long-range attacks—especially assassination. In holographic matches and trials like this, he was always a top-tier killer. Ji Chenxi barely heard any sound before Lan Mu flashed right in front of him. Behind him, his escape routes had already been layered with webs, one after another.
Both were poison types—but spiders were truly annoying creatures.
Not even half as cute as snakes.
