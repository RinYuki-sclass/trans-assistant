Even pinned down and completely under control, Chi Zhuo didn’t look the least bit flustered. Lying flat on the ground, he asked bluntly, “So, Your Highness, were you planning from the start to use the Phantom Butterfly’s hand?”
“I wasn’t, at first.”
“Hm?” Chi Zhuo let out a low sound of confusion, but Ji Chenxi clearly had no intention of explaining.
His face was calm, almost blank. The dagger in his hand glinted with a dangerous light, pressed hard against Chi Zhuo’s chest, inching forward like it meant to go all the way.
“Got anything you want to say?”
“Hm?” Chi Zhuo sounded puzzled again, then let out a soft, quick laugh. “Last words? Or are you asking if I dare to dream after losing?”
The tip of the blade tapped lightly. Just a bit more force and it would slice clean through the fabric over his chest, then slip straight into flesh without any trouble.
Being held at knifepoint never felt good. But Chi Zhuo showed none of the fear or panic that should’ve come with it. His eyes didn’t waver. He wasn’t scared—because he was almost certain Ji Chenxi wouldn’t actually hurt him.
Ji Chenxi toyed with the dagger, already looking a little bored. The hand that had been holding Chi Zhuo down behind his back shifted to his neck instead, moving fast—like an owner grabbing an unruly cat by the scruff while trying not to lose his temper. The dagger, no longer fully pressed to Chi Zhuo’s chest, flicked lightly and picked out the gold coin there. The shining coin was caught in the same hand that held the blade. Then Ji Chenxi tore off a silk ribbon from his own clothes and, with one hand, tied Chi Zhuo’s wrists together.
Ji Chenxi seemed to have a special liking for long, flowing decorations. Almost half of what he wore could be stripped off into ribbon-like strips. Thinking back to the black butterfly bow, Chi Zhuo’s expression softened just a little.
He asked quietly, “Where did I make you unhappy?”
His voice was light, brushing past like a small, soft brush.
They were close—very close. But Chi Zhuo spoke so softly that even at that distance, Ji Chenxi could barely hear him.
Ji Chenxi’s brows dipped slightly. He happened to be standing with his back to the recording drone. Chi Zhuo wasn’t completely blocked from view, still bold as ever, but Ji Chenxi answered in a faint breath of sound, shaping the words more than speaking them: “I’m not angry. What would I even be angry about?”
A double denial. Not that different from admitting it outright.
Chi Zhuo was sharp—when he wanted to be, he noticed every tiny detail. ‘Got anything you want to say?’ That question had been… a little out of place. He quickly caught the key to it.
He’d asked whether Ji Chenxi had planned from the start to use the Phantom Butterfly’s hand. Ji Chenxi said he hadn’t—at first. So what changed his mind?
Countless thoughts raced through Chi Zhuo’s head. He almost caught the fleeting answer several times, but it slipped away each time, just out of reach. Since he couldn’t pin it down, he started ruling things out one by one.
“Is it because I used Lan Mu’s spider to drain your spiritual strength?”
Ji Chenxi’s brows were still drawn down, even the corners of his lips hinting at a faint downturn. Most people would’ve missed those tiny shifts, but in Chi Zhuo’s eyes, they were blown up a hundred times over.
