It looked like anger on the surface—but underneath, there was a trace of hurt. A little bit of grievance. For a moment, Chi Zhuo didn’t know what to say. It felt like a kitten he adored had been wronged—turning its back on him in silence. When he reached out to poke it, the kitten shot him a glare that looked fierce, yet was filled with blame and quiet sadness.
Ji Chenxi wasn’t a kitten. He didn’t look soft and sweet like one either. But people see what they want to see—especially when they’re in love. One tiny expression was enough to set his mind racing.
Ji Chenxi was usually bold and bright in front of him. That made this subtle look hit even harder.
Heartache, guilt, and a dark little urge to take advantage of that soft moment and tease him—all those feelings tangled together, twisting Chi Zhuo’s heart tight.
He wanted to do something. Say something. But he had no idea where to start.
The usually cold man frowned, lowering his voice even more. “Talk to me, little rose. If you don’t say it, how am I supposed to know?”
People always say couples need to talk—be patient, be understanding. But when someone’s upset, do they really want to talk?
Ji Chenxi narrowed his eyes at him. Seeing that Chi Zhuo was genuinely troubled over his small mood, he figured… fine. He wasn’t exactly furious anyway.
He’d already tied the man up. Ji Chenxi’s fingers moved without restraint now, drifting to Chi Zhuo’s Adam’s apple and tapping it lightly. “If I just told you straight out, wouldn’t that make it seem like I’m begging for comfort?”
“No,” Chi Zhuo said softly. “I’m the one who wants your comfort. I made you upset. Seeing you unhappy makes me feel bad. I really want to know what I did wrong. I even want you to kiss me. I’ll secretly steal everyone else’s points and give them all to you—would that make you feel a little better?”
Ji Chenxi spared a second of silence for the “everyone else” in that sentence. His gaze didn’t change much, but his face did. Heat crept up his pale cheeks without him even noticing, staining them a beautiful shade of red.
His lips moved as he muttered with quiet disdain, “Someone sure knows how to sweet-talk.”
Chi Zhuo hadn’t meant it as sweet talk. But words spoken straight from the heart sometimes sounded even sweeter than planned lines.
Ji Chenxi tugged off Chi Zhuo’s tie as well and tightened the binding around his wrists.
“Not sweet talk,” Chi Zhuo murmured.
Ji Chenxi said nothing.
“But right now, I really do want to say something sweet just to make you happy.”
“Behave yourself,” Ji Chenxi finally said.
He stood up and dusted off his hands, admiring his handiwork. By the time he turned to leave, his smile was bright again, lively as ever. “Senior, see you around—if fate allows.”
With that, he vanished in a flash, the recording drone dutifully trailing behind him.
