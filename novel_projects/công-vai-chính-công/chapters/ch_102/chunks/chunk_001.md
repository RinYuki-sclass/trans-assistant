Ji Zhiqing’s eyes widened in anger, opening far more than usual. She was a smart woman—Chen Xing’s actions were more than enough to make her understand that she had only ever been a pawn in his game.
Anger. Unwillingness.
Those intense emotions tangled together, forming something so fierce it felt like her eyes might split apart.
No ambitious person would want something like this to happen right when they were about to succeed, and no one enjoys being betrayed.
Pain kept spreading from her chest. She grabbed tightly onto the hand Chen Xing had driven into her chest, her neatly trimmed nails leaving harsh red marks on that pale skin.
Her blood kept draining away, and the feeling of life slipping through her fingers stirred up a desperate will to survive. But no matter how hard she struggled, she couldn’t break free from Chen Xing’s grip. His hand was like iron—completely unmovable.
“You… got close to me on purpose.”
Chen Xing didn’t answer. Instead, ignoring her weak resistance, he pushed the short blade even deeper into her body by another inch or two. Blood gushed out in large amounts, and even the corner of Ji Zhiqing’s lips began to overflow with blood—tinged with an unnatural black.
The blade was poisoned!
Ji Zhiqing stared at Chen Xing without blinking. She ignored the poison and continued, “All of this, cough, cough…”
As she spoke, another mouthful of blood burst out. The motion forced the dagger in even deeper, but she still managed to finish her sentence, “…was too smooth.”
Chen Xing smiled. With his other hand, he gently wiped the blood from her lips. His movements were careful and soft, like a polite gentleman—if not for the fact that he was the one who had stabbed her.
“Your Highness, what are you trying to say?”
Ji Zhiqing narrowed her eyes slightly, looking at him with a complicated expression. “How long have you been lying in wait by my side?”
They weren’t alone. There were many of her trusted aides nearby. Yet those people who had once loyally supported her now acted as if they hadn’t seen the assassination at all, showing a completely unnatural indifference. It was too strange—almost as if they had all been controlled.
“Second Princess, from the very beginning I knew you wouldn’t be the one I’d support. The ruler I want must be decisive and ruthless, but also upright and open. He can have a few flaws—after all, I have plenty of time to slowly shape him into someone I like.”
The look in Ji Zhiqing’s eyes grew more and more strange. “If I remember correctly, you are… the leader of a dark faction.”
“Ah, yes. Isn’t it interesting for the leader of a dark faction to become the Imperial Tutor and help a new emperor seize the throne?”
“I could have been… a suitable ally.”
“Yes, Second Princess, you perfectly fit my need for decisiveness and ruthlessness. After I helped you, it would’ve been your turn to kill me off once I’d outlived my use. Unfortunately, you were only ever a stepping stone to me. And as a stepping stone, it’s about time you disappeared.”
