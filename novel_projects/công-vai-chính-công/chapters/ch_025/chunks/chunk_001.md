Ji Chenxi walked away, leaving behind a trail of his pheromones. Even though he had just done something that could have made Chi Zhuo hate him to pieces, the lingering pheromones seemed to cling to him, twisting and teasing around him like they had a life of their own.
The normally aggressive, overpowering scent that should have screamed dominance now swirled around him with a hint of grievance—just enough that Ji Chenxi could read a trace of complaint in it.
A while ago, Ji Chenxi had been stingy with his pheromones, barely willing to release even a trace. But this time, he took the initiative, letting a bit of it slip free.
Instantly, the lethargic tequila scent clinging to him perked up, merging with the faint rose aroma, wrapping around him in a tender dance before settling into a familiar, sweet, almost like dessert wine.
Ji Chenxi chuckled quietly. He didn’t rush back. Instead, he lingered outside, letting the scent drift through the air.
He was oddly curious about the way the pheromones had blended. What on earth had Chi Zhuo done to him? Between alphas, pheromones either suppress or repel each other—how could they fuse like this?
If only he had the right tools nearby, he’d have wanted to extract some for research. Just a little, to study it properly.
The scent didn’t fade quickly. By now, Ji Chenxi suspected that it wasn’t just his clothes carrying it—his hair, too, clung to the other person’s aroma.
As his tongue brushed over the teeth marks, Ji Chenxi was certain: the lingering tequila scent on the tips of his teeth was much gentler than when it had first marked him.
Halfway along, the scent on his body showed no sign of fading. If anything, it was growing sweeter, richer.
He slightly regretted not bringing a scent remover. With one spray, he could have gone back and continued sipping his wine in peace.
Or maybe he should’ve taken that unfinished bottle of wine with him. But marking someone with one flavor of liquor and then walking off with another right in front of them? That would’ve been a bit too much, even for him.
The night wind cooled the heat in him, and Ji Chenxi chuckled again.
He casually found a hidden corner, sat on a wooden chair, and pretended to nap. He had planned to wait until the crowd dispersed before leaving, but unexpectedly, he sensed someone passing by.
Ji Chenxi opened his eyes and stepped in front of someone heading in a certain direction.
“Anran,” he greeted with a smile. “What a coincidence!”
It was only then that Bai Anran noticed the silver-haired alpha sitting on the chair. He froze for a moment at the sight of that light, cheerful smile, then replied politely, his tone soft and courteous: “Your Highness, good day.”
Ji Chenxi’s eyes lowered slightly, and he gave a faint, airy acknowledgment.
Bai Anran looked at the silver-haired youth again, who seemed even more striking under the moonlight than many omegas. He was about to excuse himself, but before he could continue walking, Ji Chenxi, who had no intention of starting a conversation, called out to him.
“Anran, don’t go that way.”
