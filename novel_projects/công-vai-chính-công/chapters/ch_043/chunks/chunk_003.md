Clearly, he shouldn’t have agreed to Chi Zhuo’s visit so easily.
When Ji Chenxi checked the messages he hadn’t paid attention to earlier, he found that his father had indeed sent a second one.
[Was it you this time?]
Above it was another message: [I will give you an explanation.]
The two messages were only thirty-six minutes apart.
Ji Chenxi’s expression darkened. He had clearly underestimated his father’s abilities. In just thirty-six minutes, his father had ruled out his two children and their factions and traced it directly back to him.
Ji Chenxi retraced everything in his mind, making sure he hadn’t left a single clue behind. This message might have just been a test from his father, who, showing his usual coldness, sent the two messages anyway.
[Does His Majesty think I have nothing better to do and am looking for trouble?]
[Forget it, whatever His Majesty thinks.]
After sending these, Ji Chenxi cut off the light-screen network entirely.
Good. At least tonight he wouldn’t have to face that old man. Ji Chenxi was very pleased with this outcome. As for how Chi Zhuo would contact him after the network was cut off, that was no longer his concern.
Back at the manor, Ji Chenxi first lay down in the treatment pod to heal his wounds, then took a bath to wash away the bloodstains.
By the time all that was done, it was already three in the afternoon. Ji Chenxi treated himself to some afternoon tea—rose mousse, which tasted really good. Since he had skipped lunch, he ended up having three pieces of rose mousse and finally felt a bit overwhelmed by the sweetness.
Just as he was sipping his floral tea to balance the richness, the butler came to announce that Chi Zhuo from the Chi family had come to visit.
Ji Chenxi told the butler to let him in without hesitation and used the light-panel to order two more desserts—less sweet this time, more popular with most people.
But when Chi Zhuo actually arrived, Ji Chenxi immediately regretted letting him in. Maybe he could have canceled those two desserts he’d just ordered.
Chi Zhuo came, sure, but he also brought an enormous bouquet of champagne-colored roses.
Peach Snow Mountain—a pale, soft-colored rose. Beautiful, no doubt. But who brings roses to visit someone who’s injured?
Under Ji Chenxi’s scrutinizing gaze, Chi Zhuo offered an explanation: “They remind me of the white rose Third Prince gave me back then.”
That was clearly an excuse. Peach Snow Mountain was more champagne-colored, fundamentally different from white roses. Still, the bouquet in Chi Zhuo’s hands was undeniably beautiful, with dewdrops that seemed freshly picked.
