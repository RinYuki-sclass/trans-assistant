“Your little traps won’t stop him.”
“So you’re going in yourself?”
Chi Zhuo didn’t answer. He had already stepped into the darkness, his figure swallowed whole.
“Fine, don’t talk if you don’t want to. Hope your cat hunt goes well.”
Not long after Chi Zhuo left, Lan Mu’s communicator rang. Clearly, the school leaders had seen the explosion just now and were worried the game might be forced to stop early. Lan Mu answered, scratching his messy hair with one hand and smiling as he said, “Director Fang, didn’t we just talk? Don’t tell me you already miss me.”
Unlike Lan Mu, who answered right away, Chi Zhuo simply pressed off the flashing screen and moved silently through the dark.
When Ji Chenxi felt that aura moving closer to him as well, the corner of his lips lifted slightly. With an easy motion, the dagger in his hand pierced straight through a small blue spider.
Along the way, he had no idea how many blue spiders he’d dealt with. He had sliced through countless webs too. One hand played lightly with the dagger, while the other held up the beam of light from his wrist device.
Under the faint white glow, Ji Chenxi dodged several traps. Most of them looked deadly at first glance, but they wouldn’t actually kill you—though whether you’d lose an arm or a leg was another story.
He wasn’t heading straight toward Chi Zhuo the whole time. Once he confirmed that Chi Zhuo was coming for him too, he stopped rushing for a direct clash. Instead, he began searching for the treasure spots marked on the map.
That was exactly why Ji Chenxi came down into the underground maze even though he knew there were traps waiting. If he wanted, he could’ve just set a fire and smoked them out. But where would the fun—or the points—be in that?
After wandering around for a while and easily dodging the smaller traps—ones he could handle much faster on his own—Ji Chenxi finally reached the spot marked on the map. Sure enough, he found a gold coin there. Stamped clearly on its surface was the emblem of the First Military Academy.
The second he picked it up, his wrist device—normally only good for one-way contact and a flashlight—popped up a message about the coin.
A gold coin worth ten thousand points.
Ji Chenxi raised a brow slightly and slipped it away.
With a faint smile, he spoke toward the darkness behind him. “I’m guessing there’s another coin—one with the Imperial Royal Academy’s emblem. I wonder if it’s in your hands, Senior.”
The light from his wrist device barely reached a few meters ahead. He couldn’t see Chi Zhuo at all. And yet he looked calmly toward a patch of shadow, smiling with quiet certainty.
Soft footsteps echoed.
A tall, cold-looking alpha dressed in black stepped out of the darkness and gave Ji Chenxi a slight nod. “You’re lucky. That coin is indeed with me.”
Chi Zhuo casually took out the coin and even tossed it once in his hand. The faint light cast sharp shadows across his face, outlining it in a way that was almost unfairly good-looking. Ji Chenxi admired the view for a second—then stopped wasting time and moved to snatch it.
