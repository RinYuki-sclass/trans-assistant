It was a long story, and it had to start with the fact that their miracle had taken an interest in a female omega.
The more Ji Chenxi read, the darker his expression grew. They had been trying desperately to create a killing machine that hadn’t existed since ancient times. When they finally succeeded, conflicts arose over how to raise him: some wanted to nurture him toward humanity, to teach empathy; others just wanted a pure weapon. The factions clashed, and in the end, the female omega became pregnant with Ares’ child under their guidance.
Amazingly, there was no reproductive barrier between them. This shocking discovery sent all the researchers into a frenzy.
They became even more obsessed with studying their “miracle,” with each faction trying to impose its own methods on Ares. The end result? Their miracle went completely out of control.
The last page of the log was scrawled messily, as if something terrifying was about to strike. A diary that normally recorded only personal thoughts suddenly documented the core of their experiment to create the miracle—but the writing was unfinished, and the page was splattered with blood.
Since the log had been digitized from paper, even the bloodstains were preserved to maintain authenticity.
Ji Chenxi stared at the blood, then at the date and the praise written for Ares.
Tests showed that over the past thousand years, the only SSS-level alpha had appeared here—and that unborn child… if it weren’t for the experiment taking place over thirty years ago, Ji Chenxi might have almost believed that Chi Zhuo was the child of the being created in that experiment.
After all, the most powerful alpha in recent centuries had only reached double-S level, and even that was extremely rare. An SSS-level alpha hadn’t appeared in a millennium—but Chi Zhuo just happened to be that same kind of SSS, a genetic anomaly in the modern world.
It was hard for Ji Chenxi not to connect the dots. He had also seen a few previous heads of the Chi family; their facial features strongly resembled Chi Zhuo’s. The fact that Chi Zhuo was raised within the family and eventually controlled it made it almost impossible for him not to be a Chi. Any traces of genetic modification would have been too obvious to hide.
Ji Chenxi’s fingers lingered on the female omega’s name. If she had attracted Ares, her rank certainly wasn’t low.
He quickly flipped to the earlier part of the log, where this omega was first mentioned:
‘Abriana, our youngest genius researcher—Ares marked her! No… impossible. Abriana did it on purpose. She must have done it on purpose. She’s not experienced enough, yet she tried to skip the assistant stage to become a researcher with a voice in the lab!’
Ji Chenxi considered the words. A genius researcher, huh? He chuckled, flipping through the log again, then deleted it without hesitation, erasing every trace of its existence.
This log was indeed a Pandora’s box. It lured anyone who discovered it into the trap of research—but an incomplete record of such an experiment was, from the start, a path to sin.
This was definitely not the first lab to conduct human experiments. Thirty-plus years had passed—had their research direction shifted at all?
Countless thoughts flashed through Ji Chenxi’s mind, giving him a slight headache.
Just then, that small symbol flickered faintly again.
After several redirects, Ji Chenxi arrived at the black webpage. The top message was from Morningstar, sent just a minute ago.
Morningstar: [Bai Wei, you went to that underground lab, right?]
