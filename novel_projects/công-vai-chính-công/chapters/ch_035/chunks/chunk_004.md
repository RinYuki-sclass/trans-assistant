This time, Ji Chenxi didn’t hesitate to reply.
Bai Wei: [Yes. Yours?]
Morningstar: [Sort of. Not entirely mine, but I did invest. I used to just sit back and collect my share every year. Their lab outwardly claims to do gene research, life-extension stuff. A lot of people research this now, so at first, I didn’t pay much attention. Then random attacks by alphas on the street got me curious. I had one of my men infiltrate the lab… and who knew you’d end up stepping in yourself before he could do anything.]
Bai Wei: [Did you manage to get the lab’s core data?]
Morningstar: [Yeah, we stole it. I took a look—they’ve shifted their main focus toward chip development. Artificial high-level alphas lose control because their brains can’t handle their own physical power. Their spiritual realm collapse, causing that berserk madness we’ve seen. Now they’re transferring the spiritual link of powerful individuals into chips to stabilize and enhance them. Their ultimate goal seems to be a body strong enough to perfectly match that kind of spiritual power.]
Bai Wei: [The data I got was probably scrubbed of the key parts, but they left behind a log—probably bait for the Empire’s official labs to pick up the research.]
Morningstar: [So… you left it?]
Bai Wei: [I deleted the one I had. The one in their system, I didn’t touch.]
This time, Morning Star didn’t type back. He called.
Ji Chenxi waited a moment before answering, and when he finally did, a calm, elegant voice came through—soft, carrying a faint trace of amusement.
“Bai Wei,” he said, “hello. I thought for sure you’d reject the call.”
Ji Chenxi didn’t bother changing his voice with a voice modulator like at the start. He asked directly, “Something you need?”
“After you left, the bug I planted triggered the lab’s system to start self-destructing. The Empire’s people managed to stop it in time, but the important data… like that log… was already destroyed. I didn’t go into detail about the auction before, but what we’re really after isn’t a single item. We wanted to use the piece of material as bait to lure a hidden force out of the shadows — the spider lurking in the dark. It’s unsettling… just knowing it’s there.”
Ji Chenxi acknowledged with a nod. Then Morningstar asked, “Third Prince, when will you enter the race for the throne?”
Pouring himself a glass of water during the call, Ji Chenxi chuckled. “Haven’t I been in the game all along?”
“A fascinating game is about to begin. Why don’t we turn the passive into active?” Morningstar suggested.
“Being too active just makes you a target. A true hunter appears as prey—that’s when people let their guard down. Look at my second sister; now she’s paying more attention to my elder brother instead.”
“Your Highness, directly putting yourself into the game is… dangerously bold. You should first meet with the Night Wanderer and Queqi. In a couple of months, I’ll come to Imperial Star personally.”
“Morningstar, I look forward to your arrival.”
