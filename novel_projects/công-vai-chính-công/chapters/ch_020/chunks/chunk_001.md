Being found wasn’t a surprise to Ji Chenxi; in fact, it was something he had expected.
What caught him off guard, though, was that someone in that group could actually sense his spiritual energy. Suddenly, things had gotten a lot more complicated.
Ji Chenxi released Chi Zhuo, leaning him against the wall, and then moved to the cave entrance. Peering through a crack, he could already make out a shadowy mass approaching.
He glanced back at Chi Zhuo, then at the sky, thinking he might as well draw some fire. If he could fight, he’d fight; if not, he’d run. Either way, it wasn’t like he’d be blamed for leaving someone to die.
Just as he raised his hand to shift the huge rock aside, a cold wind blew in from outside.
So… how was he supposed to fight in this freezing weather?
Before he could step out, the oversized snow leopard cub stirred, making noises to show it was still there.
Ji Chenxi looked at the unconscious Chi Zhuo and said, “You wanna stay with him?”
The snow leopard didn’t move, just stared at him with big blue eyes that made Ji Chenxi feel a little guilty.
The cold outside wasn’t exactly his favorite either. Seeing the snow leopard’s concerned gaze, Ji Chenxi simply held out his hand.
In an instant, the snow leopard wriggled out of Chi Zhuo’s arms and leaped straight at him.
Ji Chenxi retracted the spiritual beast rushing toward him back into his own spiritual realm. It wasn’t that he had a problem with his leopard—it was that he planned to merge with it.
A spiritual beast had another trick: it could fuse with its owner. For example, if it belonged to a sea creature, the owner could swim freely in water once merged. In Ji Chenxi’s case, merging with the snow leopard spirit would let him resist the cold to some extent.
So why hadn’t he done it before, even in the freezing weather? Simple—he didn’t want to grow leopard ears and a tail.
Opening the small hidden chain, he let the fluffy tail slip out. Ji Chenxi felt numb. As a kid, he loved merging with the spirit because the ears and tail were adorable. Now, at eighteen, this kind of cuteness felt painfully embarrassing.
Shaking his head to push the discomfort aside, he looked out again, his vision feeling wider and sharper.
He opened the rock-blocked entrance, then sealed it back, and slipped completely into the night, hidden.
Compared to passively letting these people find the cave, he much preferred taking the initiative.
Hidden and alert, Ji Chenxi observed the large group under the cover of darkness. Surprisingly, the group wasn’t as united as it looked. Wrapped in multiple layers of protective suits, the suspicious Poison Scorpion squinted at the particularly cool young man in the team. “Are you sure they’re over here?”
The young man’s voice carried a teasing smile. “I’m sure.”
