“Classmate Lu doesn’t look to be in great shape.”
Lu Jianhan’s expression didn’t change, but his body tensed even more.
The Phantom Butterfly could confuse people, and the powder on its wings carried a toxin that numbed the nerves. Ji Chenxi wasn’t stupid enough to approach someone with a dangerous spiritual form without any caution.
After moving a few steps closer, he stopped. “I’ve got something on me—picked it up not long ago. Good stuff. Works great for healing. Want to consider teaming up with me? I won’t ask for much. Half of the points you’re holding right now, plus future cooperation between the two of us. Of course, once we’re partners, any points we earn will be split fifty-fifty. In other words, you’re just using half your current points to buy yourself a strong ally like me.”
Whether Ji Chenxi was really that strong, Lu Jianhan didn’t know. But he did know one thing—if he rejected him now, that dangerous spiritual attack would land on him next, and every last point he had would end up in Ji Chenxi’s pocket.
“Third Highness isn’t just trying to mess with me for fun, is he?”
Ji Chenxi pointed to the recording drone hovering not far from them. “It’s still there. If I really wanted to go back on my word, I wouldn’t have brought it along. After all, it’s pretty easy to leave the school’s higher-ups thinking I’m unreliable, isn’t it?”
It wasn’t exactly a completely convincing excuse, but with Lu Jianhan badly injured and already discovered, he didn’t really have a choice.
“I hope Your Highness is someone who keeps his word.”
After a brief pause to think it over, Lu Jianhan answered in a low voice, tossing his points toward Ji Chenxi.
“Good,” Ji Chenxi said without hesitation. After taking half the points, he handed over the herbs he’d found along with the remaining points to Lu Jianhan, simultaneously dissolving the spiritual spike. “Apply the medicine quickly—we still have follow-up actions.”
“What’s Your Highness’s plan?”
“Simple. Grab as many points as we can in the early stage. Later, during the cooperative competitions, points won’t be so easy to take openly. Rather than snatching from freshmen, it’s better to target older students—especially those with some skill who like to give freshmen trouble.”
“And after that?”
“Head to the West District. The school already told us early on there’s a hidden mission there—basically trying to lure us in. Since we want to fight for first place, we naturally have to go. I’m not aiming for instant fame in this trial, but I do want to put someone firmly in second place.”
“Your rival?” Lu Jianhan guessed.
Ji Chenxi tilted his head and let out a light laugh at the question. “You could say that.”
“Did I say something wrong?”
Ji Chenxi’s eyes curved into a faint smile. “No, it’s just… he’s not only a rival, he’s my lifelong enemy.”
The slight lift at the end of his words carried a subtle, almost unnoticeable hint of playfulness.
