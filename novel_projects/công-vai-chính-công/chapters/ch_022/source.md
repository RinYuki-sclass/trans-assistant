The irritation in Ji Chenxi’s eyes eased a little, though a flicker of surprise hid between his brows.

If it had just been a regular message, fine — Chi Zhuo could’ve easily gotten his contact through someone else. But this one came as a private message. Seeing the new name pop up on his light-brain’s friend list, Ji Chenxi didn’t even need to guess who pulled the trick — that quiet, refined-looking female beta.

When Chi Zhuo’s squad regrouped, that beta had practically taken charge of cleaning up the battlefield. She’d only passed by Ji Chenxi once, barely a glance, yet somehow that single brush-by was enough for her to sneak herself onto his friend list.

That little silent move alone told Ji Chenxi she wasn’t just capable — she was sharp. No wonder she could stick by Chi Zhuo’s side like a right-hand aide.

He looked twice at the newly added contact, then finally tapped open the message.

【Thanks for your help at the cave earlier, Your Highness.】

Ji Chenxi arched a brow, lazily tapping a few times before sending a reply.

【Chenxi: Oh? Just a thank-you and that’s it?】

【Then what kind of thank-you gift does Your Highness want?】

Ji Chenxi had only meant to tease, but when the question of a “gift” actually came up, the fun sort of faded.

He hadn’t helped expecting any reward. As a prince of the Empire, whatever he wanted would be handed to him without him lifting a finger — no need to earn favors through a rescue.

His fingers tapped twice on the words ‘thank-you gift’, but instead of brushing him off completely, he typed back a cool:

[Haven’t thought about it.]

Once that message was sent, Ji Chenxi set the light-brain aside. His elder brother had already walked up to him.

The Empire’s eldest prince was named ‘Ji Anze’ — a name that sounded gentle and refined.

But no matter how you looked at him, “refined” was the last word that came to mind. If anything, Ji Chenxi thought the man had picked up quite a few of the classic ‘alpha flaws’.

Top of the list? Arrogance.

Then again, that wasn’t exactly rare among alphas. Even Ji Chenxi himself wasn’t completely free of it.

Still, that didn’t mean he had to like the man standing in front of him, putting on this whole “affectionate elder brother” act. Ji Chenxi had long since seen through Ji Anze’s wolfish ambition, yet the man insisted on keeping up that fake harmony between them.

As expected, the moment Ji Anze arrived, he began his usual round of concern and comfort.

“Chenxi, are you alright? It’s my fault for being late and letting you get scared. These star pirates are getting bolder by the day — they even dared to attack a prince of the Empire. I’ll have to ask Father to let me deal with them properly and put them back in their place.”

When he said it, his eyes flashed with cold fury, like he truly hated the star pirates from the bottom of his heart.

Ji Chenxi hummed softly in response — just enough to count as agreement.

Ji Anze sighed, rubbing his temples with a touch of weariness.

“At least you’re safe, Chenxi. If something had happened to you, I’d have no way to explain it to Father.”

The performance was flawless — to the point where Ji Chenxi almost had to admire it. The man’s acting had improved a lot.

If it weren’t for the fact that there were only a handful of people with the motive and guts to make that earlier move, Ji Chenxi might’ve actually started doubting himself.

His gaze slid toward the middle-aged general who’d followed the crown prince down to inspect the site — a man carrying the rank of major general.

That one was probably the real escort sent to fetch him. But Ji Chenxi, having read that particular “story,” knew the truth: this general secretly answered to Ji Anze.

Otherwise, there was no way the man would’ve allowed the fleet to drag its feet this long before coming to his rescue.

Chances were, there were still loyal old eyes within the squad keeping watch. Ji Anze must’ve known any more delays would be reported back — which was why he’d come in person, to smooth things over.

Not wanting to let his brother’s endless chatter continue, Ji Chenxi spoke up lightly, cutting the act short.

“Big brother, I’m a little tired.”

Ji Anze looked at the beautiful alpha in front of him — those tired eyes half-lidded, his expression quiet and composed — and something like pity flickered across his face.

“Chenxi must’ve had a rough time, getting chased by star pirates all the way to this tiny planet,” he said softly. “But there’s one thing I can’t quite figure out, and I wanted to ask you about it.”

Ji Chenxi lifted his gaze slightly, his smile calm and polite.

“Go ahead, brother.”

“Were you with someone else before we found you?”

Ji Chenxi hadn’t expected the question to be that blunt. His eyelids lifted a little higher.

“Why would you say that, brother?”

Ji Anze didn’t flinch under the deflection. His voice stayed warm, the tone all gentle concern.

“I’m only worried about you, Chenxi. Surely you didn’t take down the Red Scorpion pirate group all by yourself. But right now, we can’t find any trace of whoever was with you. I just don’t want you to be taken advantage of.”

Now that caught Ji Chenxi’s attention. The man was digging a hole — and trying to make him fall right in.

After all, his ability level was officially listed as ‘S-class’.

If he admitted he’d wiped out the Red Scorpions on his own, people would start questioning that rating.

But if he admitted someone else had been there, they’d start asking who that person was. Either way, Ji Anze would find a way to drag Chi Zhuo into it.

Then again, maybe not. Maybe the Red Scorpions hadn’t shared all their intel with his dear big brother.

If that was the case, Ji Anze might not even know Ji Chenxi had been with Chi Zhuo — or that Chi Zhuo had forces moving in the shadows.

So, this was probably a test. Ji Anze was fishing to see if Ji Chenxi had someone powerful backing him.

After a quick bit of mental math, Ji Chenxi let a faint frown crease his brow, a touch of confusion in his voice.

“What do you mean by that, brother?”

Ji Anze’s tone grew even softer, almost coaxing.

“Chenxi, I don’t mean anything by it. I just worry you’re still too young and might be deceived. If that person truly destroyed the Red Scorpion pirates, why didn’t they take you with them? Why leave you alone on this harsh little planet? Don’t think I’m nagging — I just don’t want to see you hurt.”

“I know,” Ji Chenxi replied quietly. “You’ve always cared for me the most. When I was little and ran off from the palace, it was you who found me and carried me back. I remember thinking then that you were the best big brother in the whole world.”

The memory seemed to catch Ji Anze off guard for a moment. He couldn’t help but picture it — a teenage boy trudging through dark alleyways, carrying a tiny, porcelain-faced child on his back, as if holding up his entire world.

But what had come after that fond memory was his mother’s harsh punishment — and her scathing words, full of disappointment and fury.

Remembering it now, Ji Anze’s expression softened for real this time. He reached out and brushed a hand over the young man’s silver hair, his tone light with nostalgia. The youth’s face was too striking, too beautiful — at this age, he still looked more like a boy than a grown man.

“So you still remember that, Chenxi? Back then, you were so tiny and soft. I didn’t even dare to hold you, afraid I might hurt you somehow.”

“I really liked you back then,” Ji Chenxi muttered under his breath.

“We’ve both grown up now.”

Ji Chenxi nodded slightly. They had indeed grown up.

He still remembered — his brother used to be a beta, yet braver than most alphas, gentle like sunlight in the early morning. But after Ji Anze’s secondary differentiation into an alpha, everything changed. That warmth vanished, replaced by pride and pretense — the perfect mask of a classic alpha, polished and false.

That small moment of shared nostalgia, at least, stopped Ji Anze from digging any further.

“If you’re tired, Chenxi, get some rest,” Ji Anze said, standing up. “We can talk later.”

Just as he was about to leave, Ji Chenxi spoke again.

“Brother, I don’t have any interest in that position. Whether it’s you or Second Sister who ends up sitting there — it makes no difference to me.”

Ji Anze froze, surprise flashing in his eyes as he looked back at the younger man, whose lashes were half-lowered in lazy calm.

The beautiful alpha tapped his lips lightly with one finger and added,

“Just this once, brother.”

For a split second, Ji Anze’s carefully maintained gentleness almost slipped. It felt as if Ji Chenxi already knew something — yet his face showed not a single crack.

When the room finally fell quiet again, Ji Chenxi casually lifted his light-brain and glanced at the last message he’d received.

【Then I owe Your Highness a favor.】

‘A favor, huh?’ Ji Chenxi couldn’t quite read Chi Zhuo’s meaning behind those words.

Was he brushing off the whole “biting the gland” incident like it was nothing?

Or did he mean — debts are debts, and grudges are grudges?

Ji Chenxi closed his light-brain and shut his eyes, a flicker of irritation crossing his face.

In the original story, there had been no scene of him being chased by the Red Scorpion pirates. After all, Bai Anran had been his childhood friend, and if he was marked, he would have noticed — there was no way he’d travel through space as if nothing had happened. In that version, he was simply the gentle, caring male supporting character who occasionally checked in on Bai Anran. To him, it was nothing more than awkward concern without any romantic basis, which, in the novel, became the kind of love quietly held in one’s heart but never expressed.

Was this book really prophetic? Or was the world he lived in itself a book?

After Ji Chenxi’s starship took off, the task of gathering information fell to a large team.

They cleaned each corpse, extracting information: identities, causes of death, and so on.

It was a long, tedious process, and many were assigned to it. One person, while sorting through a particularly mangled corpse, fingered a small chip hidden within. The chip was black and lodged in the seam of the corpse’s black glove — unnoticed by anyone around.

That person followed the team, helping move corpses, record data, and collect leftover mecha weapons scattered across the small planet.

Once all the work was done, he finally returned to his quarters. Casually, he inserted the chip into his household robot. The sweet-looking, charming machine shuddered with scrambled signals. Then, surprisingly, the robot began moving on its own, even without commands — motions rigid but fluid, almost human.

She stretched her body casually, feeling her newly acquired form, then muttered in dissatisfaction,

“Ugh, I hate being so weak and useless.”

The man ignored her, his eyes dark.

“Household robots aren’t weak or useless.”

“You’re making me angry,” the sweet robot said, crossing her legs carelessly. Her short maid skirt barely covered her pale thighs.

“Second-in-command, I told you not to get involved with the snake so early. If the snake’s people had cleaned the corpses any better, you’d be dead here on this tiny planet, without anyone ever knowing your name.”

“I wouldn’t say ‘without anyone knowing,’” she replied. “After all, I was the deputy of the Red Scorpion pirate group.”

The man, his face mostly hidden beneath his hood, rubbed at his temples in frustration.

“You really have a death wish, don’t you? Next time you die, even if the boss personally orders me to save you, I won’t lift a finger.”

“Oh, come on, Xiao San, don’t be so heartless!”

He didn’t take the bait, his tone flat.

“Talk. What was it like, making contact with ‘Mamba’?”

The maid-dressed household robot’s lips curved upward into a wide grin.

“SSS-levels really are something else. He took all those forbidden drugs and not only survived, but actually woke up right when they were supposed to send him into total collapse. Can you believe it? When he killed me, he could see again.”

The man scribbled notes while listening, then paused, pen tip still against the page.

“He killed you himself?”

“Sort of — but not exactly.”

“Don’t play games with me, or I’ll make sure you stay trapped in that maid bot forever.”

“Tch, Xiao San, don’t get so grumpy. It wasn’t his hands that did it — it was his spiritual beast. I think that black mamba of his bit into my neck and squeezed the life out of me. Though, to be fair, he was fast — when he grabbed my throat, I thought he was gonna snap my neck, but instead he tossed me straight to his snake. Maybe he realized breaking my neck wouldn’t really kill me?”

The man stopped writing altogether, his eyes narrowing as he studied the robot from head to toe.

“You didn’t leak any information, did you?”

“Of course not.”

“Good. His condition shouldn’t be any better than yours. With that much illegal stimulant in his system, even an SSS-level body shouldn’t have been able to wake up — let alone stabilize his spiritual realm.”

“But he did wake up,” the robot said, her tone almost playful. “He woke up, killed me, and even used his pheromones to suppress me. Maybe your miracle drug wasn’t that strong after all, hmm?”

The man bit down on the end of his pen, his eyes dark with thought.

“No… the drug was fine. No one under Mamba could’ve cracked it that easily. So where did it go wrong? His sight came back, his pheromone control returned, even his spiritual form’s intact… no, something’s off — something’s very off.”

While the man was tearing his hair out in frustration, the people around Chi Zhuo were in just as much chaos.

When their head of the family had returned, everything had seemed fine. But as soon as he boarded the starship, a flood of orders came down — clear, precise objectives. Not only would the Red Scorpion pirates be eliminated for good, but anyone who had secretly aided them would be made to pay.

This mission had always been partly a trap to lure enemies out, and Chi Zhuo held absolute control over it.

Everything had gone smoothly. Their head of the family even tapped and poked at his light-brain, as if chatting with someone, carefully reading each word, weighing every sentence. Even Bai Han couldn’t help but smile.

Though their head wasn’t particularly old, he had no friends around him. This little prince, having shared the hardships of the mission, had earned a bit of rapport. Their head not only helped him but subtly hinted to tamper with his light-brain — a small tweak that, unexpectedly, only added one more name to his friend list.

Bai Han smiled faintly and quickly returned his attention to the tasks Chi Zhuo had assigned.

It was at that moment that Chi Zhuo suddenly fell into unconsciousness again. The wild surge of pheromones almost sent the alphas on the starship into a frenzy.

Bai Han immediately activated the ventilation system while also engaging the scent-neutralizing device. With the air circulating quickly, the alphas barely managed to regain their composure.

After placing Chi Zhuo into the medical pod, Bai Han began a thorough examination. The more she checked, the grimmer her expression became.

“Miss Bai, what should we do?” asked the middle-aged man who had briefly met Ji Chenxi before, bowing respectfully.

Bai Han ignored him and drew two more vials of blood for analysis. By the time the tests were done, her face was as pale as chalk.

“Call Mr. Hua. I have urgent matters to discuss with him. You won’t need to come in later.”

When Mr. Hua, still wearing his bionic mask, entered, Bai Han immediately set up multiple layers of protection, ensuring no one could see what was happening inside.

Mr. Hua’s expression turned grave.

“Xiao Bai, what is it?”

“The master’s pheromones are destabilizing.”

“How is that possible?!”

“I don’t even know what’s really happening right now,” Bai Han said, tension in her voice. “By all logic, it should be slowly stabilizing, but I suspect the master forced his spiritual energy ‘before’ his pheromones had fully settled.”

“Do you have a solution?” Mr. Hua asked.

“Two options. First, remove the glands entirely.”

“That’s definitely not an option,” Mr. Hua said immediately, just hearing it.

“Then there’s the second,” Bai Han continued. “We can re-inject the pheromones extracted from the master’s glands earlier. Those pheromones aren’t highly antagonistic with his own, so they could neutralize the destabilizing ones. There’s about a seventy percent chance it could stabilize the breakdown. But if we force another pheromone in while his own is still weak and unstable, it could create dependency between the two pheromones — plus some side effects I can’t fully predict.”

Mr. Hua’s forehead was already slick with sweat. He wondered why Bai Han, who could make the decision herself, had to drag him in. Was this because of the master’s punishment? If things went wrong, the two of them would die together.

Seeing the look on Mr. Hua’s face, Bai Han shook her head.

“I didn’t call you in to drag you down. It’s just that something this important shouldn’t be decided by me alone. If the master were conscious, I’d ask him directly. But since he’s unconscious, the only person I can consult is you.”

“Neither option is good.”

“True. But they’re the only options I can think of.”

“What if we simply never touch that pheromone again?”

“Then naturally, there’d be no effect.”

Mr. Hua exhaled softly.

“Then option two it is.”

Elsewhere, after circling around, Ji Chenxi returned to his own estate.

The first thing he did was give himself a thorough wash, then he slowly sipped the porridge the butler had brought.

The moment Su Yu got the news that Ji Chenxi had returned, he rushed over in a flurry. Seeing him safe and sound, he couldn’t help but laugh.

“Third Prince, amazing! Single-handedly taking on the star pirates!”

“I told Father someone helped at the time, so it wasn’t really single-handed,” Ji Chenxi replied.

“Who?” Su Yu asked, curious. “It can’t be an omega, right? Otherwise, this would be the start of our Third Prince’s love story.”

“Oh? Does it have to be an omega for it to be love?”

Su Yu chuckled.

“Alright, alright — beta works too. But then, has our prince’s love actually arrived?”

Ji Chenxi swallowed a mouthful of rice porridge before answering slowly.

“He’s an alpha. I don’t know if love has arrived.”

Hearing that, Su Yu immediately realized Ji Chenxi was teasing him.

“If he’s an alpha, then maybe that love shouldn’t arrive at all. But which alpha could actually wipe out the Red Scorpions?”

“Someone I didn’t know. They were wearing a bionic mask,” Ji Chenxi said casually, eyes open, fabricating without hesitation.

Seeing Ji Chenxi’s lack of interest, Su Yu dropped the topic and switched gears.

“It’s barely past three in the afternoon, and Your Highness is already eating seafood porridge?”

“I hadn’t eaten for nearly a full day and night. Naturally, I’m hungry.”

Su Yu, who had meant to tease him, blurted out in surprise,

“Wait, the eldest prince didn’t give you anything to eat?”

“Nutrient pills were available, but do you think I’d eat that stuff?”

Now Su Yu was genuinely impressed. Hungry all day but refusing food he didn’t like — that took some willpower.

“Our Empire’s little rose really can’t be left on his own. Look at him — barely a day outside, and he’s already starving.”

Ji Chenxi raised an eyebrow.

“Say two more sentences like that, and I won’t mind sweeping you right out the door.”

Su Yu hurriedly laughed it off, trying to steer the conversation lightly.

“What about that one from the Chi family? When I first heard you were being chased by the Red Scorpions, I thought he might’ve hired them to kill you.”

“You think he did it?”

“Eh, not exactly. It’s just… well, with what you just said, I can’t help but make the connection.”

“You didn’t tell anyone else, right?” Ji Chenxi had already finished two bowls of seafood porridge. He wasn’t as hungry now and casually set his spoon down.

“Of course not. Who’d go blabbing about this?”

“Hmm, then it’s fine. If he didn’t act, who did isn’t hard to guess — it just depends on whether that person wants to know or not.”

“You mean…”

Ji Chenxi tapped his lips lightly, signaling for him to keep quiet.

The long-haired beauty laughed softly, a vision of elegance.

Su Yu leaned up slightly, a mischievous grin on his face.

“Alright, alright. I won’t say a word.”

After seeing Su Yu off, Ji Chenxi’s light-brain soon showed that his starnotes had been refunded.

Apparently, since he hadn’t rejoined the main fleet, the order was simply canceled.

Ji Chenxi glanced at it casually, about to move on, but seeing the cancellation record reminded him that he’d effectively scammed Chi Zhuo out of one of his latest starships.

He calculated the ship’s approximate value based on his impressions and factored in potential modification costs. After roughly estimating the total, he multiplied it by 1.5 and transferred the amount to Chi Zhuo’s private account.

He had expected Chi Zhuo to at least question him about it, but… nothing.

Ji Chenxi’s lips curved in a faint smile, though he didn’t dwell on it.

As long as he didn’t actively interact with Bai Anran or Chi Zhuo, the book that supposedly chronicled his future was just that — a book.

A few leisurely days passed, and Ji Chenxi soon felt restless again. He had been thinking about whether to invite Su Yu to see the beautiful nebulae of Yunmu Star, when his butler handed him an invitation to a banquet.

Ordinarily, the butler wouldn’t deliver such invitations directly to Ji Chenxi — everyone knew he disliked formal gatherings, let alone his long-serving butler.

So this invitation, placed directly in his hands, was clearly hard to refuse.

Ji Chenxi opened it, and a smile spread across his face.

It was an invitation to his elder brother’s engagement three days from now — the engagement to a noblewoman.

Ji Chenxi had initially made such a scene on purpose. He wanted his brother’s actions to leave traces — a move so reckless it would expose his intent to kill. His extremely capable second sister would never miss such an opportunity.

While his brother was busy handling the fallout, and his sister exploited these cracks to target him, Ji Chenxi could simply enjoy his own life. The only unexpected twist had been his chance encounter with Chi Zhuo.

Now, it seemed his brother planned to lean on the noblewoman’s influence to solve a pressing problem.

Ji Chenxi shook his head lightly — that was a poor move.

Holding an engagement under these circumstances could easily make the noblewoman feel slighted. Her father, a man of considerable status, might also look down on Ji Chenxi’s elder brother.

The butler, respectful as ever, waited for Ji Chenxi’s reply.

“Master, will you attend?”

“I’ll go. After all, it’s my brother’s engagement banquet. If I don’t show, people will start gossiping.”

Three days later, Ji Chenxi arrived at the banquet.

Since it had nothing directly to do with him, he leisurely found a quiet corner and sampled the fine wines.

The engagement hall was stunning, lavishly decorated. Ji Chenxi, taking advantage of the elegant atmosphere, indulged in the wine enough to feel slightly tipsy.

Su Yu, ever mindful of his close friend, didn’t disturb him when he saw Ji Chenxi focused on tasting the wine.

At first, Ji Chenxi hadn’t paid attention to whether Chi Zhuo had come intentionally. When he first noticed a faint hint of tequila in the air, he assumed it was just the aroma from the wine in front of him.

But then the scent seemed to brush against him, teasing him slightly. Ji Chenxi’s eyes clouded with a bit of daze. For a moment, he even wondered if he was drunk — had the drifting aroma of alcohol actually brushed against him?

After the thought sank in, Ji Chenxi almost laughed out loud.

Sure enough, it was the scent of alcohol brushing against him, but not the wine itself — it was the tequila-scented pheromone.

He knew that scent all too well. Pretending not to notice, he drained the last of his glass, refilled it himself, and uncorked another bottle he actually liked.

The richer tequila scent wrapped around him, trying to lure this pure-hearted “big cat” toward a certain poisonous snake.

Ji Chenxi ignored it, but the pheromones — almost human in their urgency — grew insistent, teasing him, flooding his nostrils.

It was that intoxicating mix of seduction and invitation, hot and unrestrained, carrying all the spice of the alcohol itself. The sensation was so strong he felt nearly enveloped in the aroma. Finally, he shifted his feet.

In the original story, his elder brother hadn’t been getting engaged, so Ji Chenxi had no clear idea what Chi Zhuo’s situation was.

Was it another dose of the drugs? That poor snake.

Or… was it the susceptibility period?

But why would the susceptible phase concern him at all?

Ji Chenxi grabbed a random sip from the ancient bottle in his hand, feeling the warmth, and found the wine surprisingly good.

Following the trail of pheromones, he walked out of the banquet hall, down a long corridor, until he reached a quiet, shadowed corner.

There, in the darkness, stood a black-haired man, his breath uneven, a serpent coiled loosely around his body.

Ji Chenxi chuckled lightly.

“Well, isn’t this a coincidence? Every time I see you, you’re a mess.”

He had meant it as a casual tease, but unexpectedly, mixed in with the tequila scent was a faint trace of his rose fragrance.

Startled, Ji Chenxi quickly took another long gulp of wine to steady himself.

Had that first sip really hit him so hard?

A playful smile tugged at his lips.

“Chi Zhuo, what’s the purpose of your pheromones drawing me here? I didn’t bring any inhibitors today, so I can’t solve your urgent problem for you.”

Chi Zhuo had, of course, already noticed him approaching. He made no comment about intentionally using his pheromones to lure Ji Chenxi — just pursed his lips, eyes dark and calm, yet with a subtle, barely perceptible danger lurking within.

“Hmm?” Ji Chenxi took two steps closer, half his body still in the shadows, half in the light.

He tapped Chi Zhuo’s chest lightly with his free hand — the one not holding the bottle.

“Your pheromones seem so desperate for me. You finally draw me here, and yet you act so cold, like you don’t really want to see me. Are your pheromones and your mind not on the same page?”

Chi Zhuo lifted his gaze, meeting a face of striking beauty. His brows curved slightly in a soft smile. The pale, delicate features of this gorgeous man carried a faint blush from the tipsiness, rare and subtle, making him unexpectedly gentle. His almond-shaped eyes tilted slightly upward, lashes like black feathers casting delicate shadows in the dim light.

“I think I’m in my susceptible phase,” Chi Zhuo said, his tone flat, as if stating a simple fact.

“Huh? So suddenly?” Ji Chenxi took another sip, noting again how good the wine tasted.

“Yes,” Chi Zhuo continued, his brows darkening like a void that swallowed all light. “I also smell other alcohol on you, and it irritates me.”

“Huh??” Ji Chenxi’s tone sharpened.

Chi Zhuo felt his heartstring lightly tugged, as if teased by a cat’s paw. Emerging from the shadows, he revealed his full form. Ji Chenxi finally saw him clearly: an alpha, impeccably handsome, radiating aggression. Yet the question he asked was disarmingly gentle:

“Can you release a little pheromone now?”

Chi Zhuo’s slightly husky voice was low and smooth, carrying a magnetic allure that made the heart beat a little faster.

Ji Chenxi had thought himself only slightly tipsy — floating pleasantly in that light, airy state. But hearing those words, he almost questioned his own ears.

“What… did you just say?” he stammered, barely able to believe it.

“I want you to release a little pheromone,” Chi Zhuo said, voice calm but edged with intent.

“But… you already know I’m an alpha. Why do you need my pheromones?”

Chi Zhuo’s gaze bore into him, eyes darkening until they almost glowed red, especially as the other alcohol on Ji Chenxi’s body mingled with his own scent.

“And didn’t you say my pheromones are so desperate for you? If Your Highness won’t give me what I want, you’re asking for it.”

Ji Chenxi’s words died on his lips as Chi Zhuo suddenly closed the distance, pressing him against the wall. The remaining syllables of his protest were trapped by Chi Zhuo’s body.

Feeling the warmth of Chi Zhuo’s lips, the faintly lazy, drunken haze in Ji Chenxi’s expression instantly faded by seven or eight degrees.
