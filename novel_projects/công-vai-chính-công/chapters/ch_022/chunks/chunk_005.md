It was a long, tedious process, and many were assigned to it. One person, while sorting through a particularly mangled corpse, fingered a small chip hidden within. The chip was black and lodged in the seam of the corpse’s black glove — unnoticed by anyone around.
That person followed the team, helping move corpses, record data, and collect leftover mecha weapons scattered across the small planet.
Once all the work was done, he finally returned to his quarters. Casually, he inserted the chip into his household robot. The sweet-looking, charming machine shuddered with scrambled signals. Then, surprisingly, the robot began moving on its own, even without commands — motions rigid but fluid, almost human.
She stretched her body casually, feeling her newly acquired form, then muttered in dissatisfaction,
“Ugh, I hate being so weak and useless.”
The man ignored her, his eyes dark.
“Household robots aren’t weak or useless.”
“You’re making me angry,” the sweet robot said, crossing her legs carelessly. Her short maid skirt barely covered her pale thighs.
“Second-in-command, I told you not to get involved with the snake so early. If the snake’s people had cleaned the corpses any better, you’d be dead here on this tiny planet, without anyone ever knowing your name.”
“I wouldn’t say ‘without anyone knowing,’” she replied. “After all, I was the deputy of the Red Scorpion pirate group.”
The man, his face mostly hidden beneath his hood, rubbed at his temples in frustration.
“You really have a death wish, don’t you? Next time you die, even if the boss personally orders me to save you, I won’t lift a finger.”
“Oh, come on, Xiao San, don’t be so heartless!”
He didn’t take the bait, his tone flat.
“Talk. What was it like, making contact with ‘Mamba’?”
The maid-dressed household robot’s lips curved upward into a wide grin.
“SSS-levels really are something else. He took all those forbidden drugs and not only survived, but actually woke up right when they were supposed to send him into total collapse. Can you believe it? When he killed me, he could see again.”
The man scribbled notes while listening, then paused, pen tip still against the page.
“He killed you himself?”
“Sort of — but not exactly.”
