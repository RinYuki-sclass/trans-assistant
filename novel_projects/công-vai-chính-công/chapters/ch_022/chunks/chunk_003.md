Ji Anze didn’t flinch under the deflection. His voice stayed warm, the tone all gentle concern.
“I’m only worried about you, Chenxi. Surely you didn’t take down the Red Scorpion pirate group all by yourself. But right now, we can’t find any trace of whoever was with you. I just don’t want you to be taken advantage of.”
Now that caught Ji Chenxi’s attention. The man was digging a hole — and trying to make him fall right in.
After all, his ability level was officially listed as ‘S-class’.
If he admitted he’d wiped out the Red Scorpions on his own, people would start questioning that rating.
But if he admitted someone else had been there, they’d start asking who that person was. Either way, Ji Anze would find a way to drag Chi Zhuo into it.
Then again, maybe not. Maybe the Red Scorpions hadn’t shared all their intel with his dear big brother.
If that was the case, Ji Anze might not even know Ji Chenxi had been with Chi Zhuo — or that Chi Zhuo had forces moving in the shadows.
So, this was probably a test. Ji Anze was fishing to see if Ji Chenxi had someone powerful backing him.
After a quick bit of mental math, Ji Chenxi let a faint frown crease his brow, a touch of confusion in his voice.
“What do you mean by that, brother?”
Ji Anze’s tone grew even softer, almost coaxing.
“Chenxi, I don’t mean anything by it. I just worry you’re still too young and might be deceived. If that person truly destroyed the Red Scorpion pirates, why didn’t they take you with them? Why leave you alone on this harsh little planet? Don’t think I’m nagging — I just don’t want to see you hurt.”
“I know,” Ji Chenxi replied quietly. “You’ve always cared for me the most. When I was little and ran off from the palace, it was you who found me and carried me back. I remember thinking then that you were the best big brother in the whole world.”
The memory seemed to catch Ji Anze off guard for a moment. He couldn’t help but picture it — a teenage boy trudging through dark alleyways, carrying a tiny, porcelain-faced child on his back, as if holding up his entire world.
But what had come after that fond memory was his mother’s harsh punishment — and her scathing words, full of disappointment and fury.
Remembering it now, Ji Anze’s expression softened for real this time. He reached out and brushed a hand over the young man’s silver hair, his tone light with nostalgia. The youth’s face was too striking, too beautiful — at this age, he still looked more like a boy than a grown man.
“So you still remember that, Chenxi? Back then, you were so tiny and soft. I didn’t even dare to hold you, afraid I might hurt you somehow.”
“I really liked you back then,” Ji Chenxi muttered under his breath.
“We’ve both grown up now.”
