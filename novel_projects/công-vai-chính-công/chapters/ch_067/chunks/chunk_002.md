Ji Chenxi had only wanted to test it—maybe check if it carried poison too. But since he’d been stopped, he didn’t push his luck. Instead, he pulled out his dagger. He spun it twice in his hand, then threw it out like a boomerang.
The blade, sharp enough to cut through iron like mud, only slowed a little as it met resistance. Soon enough, it curved back and landed neatly in Ji Chenxi’s hand.
He calculated the angles and threw it forward two more times. After that, he boldly led the group two steps ahead.
The cave was so quiet it felt heavy. Even the sound of breathing and footsteps seemed loud. After just two steps, Ji Chenxi stopped again. He took his water bottle and splashed some water forward. The droplets slid along invisible threads, and at last, the people who hadn’t seen anything before finally caught sight of what had blocked nearly ninety percent of the others.
Ji Chenxi was sure he hadn’t missed any leftover threads—or those tiny movements.
The spiders were still spinning silk.
The moment they stepped in here, they had already walked straight into a spider’s trap.
Ji Chenxi spread his spiritual power even more carefully. When he caught over a dozen tiny presences—so small they were almost impossible to notice—he didn’t hesitate. He launched a spiritual attack and pierced straight through them.
On the other side, Lan Mu, who had been waiting to see how his little spiders performed, straightened slightly. The lazy air around him faded a bit. “Not bad. They got through the first round pretty fast.”
“The first round isn’t that hard. Anyone careful enough could pass it,” Chi Zhuo said flatly.
“That’s not quite fair,” Lan Mu replied with a chuckle. “My silk isn’t just tough—it’s almost impossible to see. It’s perfect for killing without anyone noticing. It’s just that this is a freshman trial, and killing isn’t allowed. Otherwise, if I made it a little more hidden, I could’ve taken quite a few lives. Passing the first round is one thing. But finding my little ones and killing them that quickly? Even out there, that’s rare.”
Chi Zhuo didn’t respond.
Lan Mu was the kind of person who could handle being ignored—that was probably why he could stand staying around Chi Zhuo in the first place. Since Chi Zhuo wouldn’t talk, he just kept chatting to himself. “Looks like it was that pretty snow leopard. Ha, now I’m kind of looking forward to this.”
“Him. I want him.” Chi Zhuo’s voice turned cold.
Lan Mu, who had just been talking to himself, shot him a surprised look. That glance carried way too much meaning. But in the end, all he said was, “Fine. Then the other two S-rank can be mine to play with.”
