Yeah—Chi Zhou was definitely playing the soft-touch card.
Ji Chenxi’s lips eased into a faint, barely there smile. “If you’d say something nice—maybe beg a little—I might keep my mouth shut.”
“Then I’ll thank Your Highness for the kindness.” Chi Zhou inclined his head slightly, the sharp lines of his face softening just a bit.
Perfect. The man had completely skipped the part about begging, acting like Ji Chenxi had already promised to keep quiet.
Ji Chenxi almost laughed out loud from how shameless it was, but he let it slide.
Once Chi Zhou’s team left—quiet and orderly, not a trace remaining—Ji Chenxi pulled his spiritual form back into himself. He climbed up a nearby tree, perched there against the cold wind, and waited for his pickup.
The luxury starship—fast, powerful, and annoyingly slow to arrive—finally descended near him just as dawn broke.
When it landed, a group of people disembarked. Leading them was a tall, striking young man, sword at his waist, noble attire sharp and immaculate.
Ji Chenxi’s phoenix-shaped eyes narrowed slightly as he studied him. After a few seconds, recognition flashed, and a smile curved his lips. “Big brother.”
The handsome man’s expression softened into a warm smile as he approached, but Ji Chenxi’s next words made his steps falter.
“Didn’t think you’d find the time to come pick me up yourself.”
The man paused, his tone dropping low and serious. “Chenxi, don’t say that. What could possibly be more important than your safety?”
Ji Chenxi only smiled, saying nothing. While the two of them talked, the others had already begun scanning the area. The situation was pretty obvious anyway—everyone from the Red Scorpion pirate crew was dead. Before his brother could ask him anything more, a group of medics had already surrounded him, checking his injuries and starting treatment.
He turned to one familiar-looking staff member and casually mentioned, “There are two people who came here with me. They’re still on this planet. Don’t forget to pick them up before we leave.”
The two mercenaries—code-named ‘Nether’ and ‘Shade’—had already been interrogated before Ji Chenxi saw them again. From the looks of it, they hadn’t spilled a word about Chi Zhou. Ji Chenxi didn’t bother speaking to them anyway; he just closed his eyes, resting quietly as he waited for his brother to finish his inspection and start questioning him.
Just like Chi Zhou had cleaned up all traces before leaving, his brother was doing the same thing—personally overseeing the site cleanup, probably worried that the Red Scorpion crew might’ve left some clue behind.
Ji Chenxi found the whole thing rather amusing.
When the starship finally took off, his terminal synced back to the network, and immediately, his inbox exploded.
Nearly eighty percent of the messages were from Su Yu. Having lost contact with Ji Chenxi for so long, Su Yu had apparently decided Chi Zhou must’ve killed him—sending not only message after message but also nonstop call and video requests.
Of course, none of that had reached Ji Chenxi while he’d been off-grid.
