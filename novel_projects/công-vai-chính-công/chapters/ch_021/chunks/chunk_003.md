He smiled lazily, eyes half-lidded, brushing the topic away as if it were nothing. Even when facing those probing black eyes, his expression didn’t waver in the slightest.
Waiting for pickup didn’t take long—just as Ji Chenxi had expected. Barely half an hour later, someone finally arrived to retrieve them.
The funny part? The first to show up weren’t the ones coming for ‘him’, the imperial prince, but Chi Zhou’s own people.
Chi Zhou’s subordinates gathered fast, so efficiently that Ji Chenxi couldn’t help wondering if Chi Zhou had purposely avoided meeting up with his team earlier—just to come find ‘him’, the unlucky one stuck in this mess. Then again, even if Chi Zhou had met up with Bo Han and the others first, it probably wouldn’t have made much difference.
The team was sharp and well-trained. They worked like ghosts, scrubbing away every trace of Chi Zhou’s presence—erasing pheromone residue, cleaning up the bodies of those strangled and bitten by the black mamba, even dismantling and destroying Chi Zhou’s own starship.
By the time they were done, it was like Chi Zhou had never been there at all. Not a single trace left behind.
Truthfully, the one thing they ‘should’ve’ been dealing with was Ji Chenxi himself—the only living witness who knew everything. But with Chi Zhou staying silent, none of them dared to make a move. Most of them even wore bionic masks to hide their real faces from the prince.
Ji Chenxi found it all rather amusing. Watching them hesitate—wanting to spray him with scent neutralizer but not daring to—made him want to laugh.
And while he was watching Chi Zhou with that half-smile of his, Chi Zhou stepped forward, holding a bottle of scent eraser.
“Your Highness,” he said quietly, “forgive the offense.”
The word offense instantly made Ji Chenxi’s scalp prickle. He reached out his hand quickly. “Give it here. I’ll do it myself.”
Under Chi Zhou’s shadowed gaze, Ji Chenxi sprayed himself thoroughly—almost like he was disinfecting.
His lips curved slightly, that familiar teasing smile playing at the corners, carrying both amusement and something harder to read.
In the end, Ji Chenxi was the one who broke the silence. “Aren’t you gonna say something to me?”
Chi Zhou’s eyes flicked to him. “What does Your Highness think I should say?”
Ji Chenxi twirled the scent eraser between his fingers, the slim bottle catching the light as his long, jade-like fingertips tapped idly against it. “A threat? A request? You’ve got to say something, right? After all, no matter how clean you’ve wiped this place, one word from me and everyone will know your secret.”
Chi Zhou’s tone stayed calm. “Would Your Highness really say it?”
Ji Chenxi tilted his head, pretending to think it over. After a long pause, he shot the question back, “Do you want me to tell them… or keep quiet?”
“I’d rather you didn’t,” Chi Zhou said evenly, “but if you must, I won’t stop you.”
Ji Chenxi’s tapping stopped. Depending on how you looked at it, that line almost sounded like he refused to threaten him at all.
