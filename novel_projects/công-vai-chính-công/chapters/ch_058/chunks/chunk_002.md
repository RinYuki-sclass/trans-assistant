“That’s why this bugs me even more.” Lu Tu grinned. “From the way you described him before, I thought this prince you admire so much would be some harmless little kitten. Turns out I was wrong. I feel like he’s way deeper than that. Compared to a kitten, he’s more like one of those big cats that can actually eat people.”
He was smiling wide, like he’d suddenly remembered something, when he said to Gu Hechuan, “Oh right, almost forgot to tell you—Morningstar is coming to the Imperial Star.”
“What’s he coming here for?”
Lu Tu shrugged. “Who knows? Maybe for experiment materials. Didn’t the Night Wanderer and Queqi help him fish for data before? Oh, and there’s Bai Wei too. At this point, it feels like only you and I still don’t know what Bai Wei’s real identity is.”
He’d meant it as a casual remark, but something about his own words seemed to spark a thought. Lu Tu suddenly pulled up an old record and found a picture the Night Wanderer had once sent him—blurry to the point of being almost useless.
He frowned at the image, then looked up several times at Ji Chenxi walking ahead of them. Suddenly, he laughed.
“I think I know why he looks so familiar now.”
Lu Tu looked like he’d just stumbled onto something incredibly interesting. Instead of telling Gu Hechuan outright, he asked with a grin, “So when you’re in front of Ji Chenxi, are you always using the image you showed before?”
“Yeah. Didn’t want to scare them.”
“Ohhh, I see.” Lu Tu’s smile turned especially loaded, full of unspoken meaning.
While Lu Tu chose to see through it without saying a word, Su Yu grabbed Ji Chenxi and hurried forward a few steps, acting like he couldn’t wait to get out of the haunted house. Yet even as he moved, he quietly set up several layers of protection between them, tight and thorough.
“You wanted to ask me something?” Ji Chenxi said.
“Mm-hmm. Aren’t you about to start school?” Su Yu said. “I heard your class has a month-long trial starting in August. The Royal Academy is inviting outstanding graduates to come guide you new students. How about it—want me and Gu Hechuan to come together?”
Hearing that, Ji Chenxi suddenly remembered that Chi Zhuo seemed to have studied at the Royal Academy before. His thoughts stirred a bit as he asked, “Do you guys have time? Those newbie trials are pretty boring. If you’re really free, you can come play around. But you didn’t go out of your way to set up anti-eavesdropping barriers just to tell me this, did you?”
“You just saw Chi Zhuo, didn’t you?” Su Yu said bluntly.
“Ran into him by chance. Said hello.”
Su Yu went quiet for a long time before finally blurting out in disbelief, “Do you really think I’m that easy to fool?”
Ji Chenxi lowered his gaze and laughed softly. “I never said that.”
Su Yu felt a headache coming on. If Ji Chenxi wanted to mess around, fine, but did he really have to be this obvious about it? He even felt like that red-haired kid had already noticed something. “So how far have you and Chi Zhuo gone?” he asked. “Don’t lie to me. At least let me prepare myself mentally.”
“How far, huh—” Ji Chenxi dragged out the words lazily. “I don’t really know. Probably about as far as you’re thinking.”
