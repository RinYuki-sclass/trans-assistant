For the Red Scorpion Pirates, this scene was nothing short of terrifying. Their starship suddenly went completely out of control, as if some invisible hand had taken over the system. Shouts of panic echoed through the corridors.
Even the deputy commander on the left, the one with the gold-rimmed glasses, was facing the same nightmare.
The ship’s system had gone haywire—spiraling downward while a twenty-second self-destruct countdown flashed across the main console.
Amis froze for half a second, then panicked. She scrambled to override the system, fingers flying across the controls, desperate to take back command. But every line of code he entered came back with the same mocking message—‘error, error, error’.
Seeing the chaos, the deputy commander shoved Amis aside and took over himself. His hands moved so fast they blurred, and at the very last two seconds of the countdown, the self-destruct was finally canceled. The ship stopped its deadly fall just in time, steadying itself once more in the void.
He had barely regained control when the comm unit crackled to life, a cold, commanding voice cutting through the static:
“What’s going on?”
“Our system has been hacked,” the deputy replied, voice tight. “The ship has entered into a crash-and-detonate sequence.”
He glanced around, and it all clicked—the reason behind the fury in their leader’s tone. Several of their ships had already been shot down earlier. Now, after this system breach, there were barely five pirate ships left flying. Three of those had arrived later as reinforcements. Of the ones that had been chasing Ji Chenxi’s crew, only his own ship and another—manned by the tech team—were still intact.
Scorpion’s grin was twisted and cold. All this loss just to kill a barely grown “kitten”? No wonder that man was hell-bent on wiping him out. If that kid ever got the chance to grow, he’d be unstoppable.
“Send more men,” Scorpion said, his tone sharp as a blade. “We have to finish this before their backup gets here. Leave nothing behind.”
The deputy immediately relayed the order. His crew scattered to carry it out. But before he could end the transmission, the voice on the other end spoke again.
“Zuo Xian,” the leader hissed, “our system gets hacked, and you didn’t even notice?”
Cold sweat trickled down Zuo Xian’s back. He knew what was coming next.
“Can’t even handle something this small,” the voice said, icily calm. “Tell me, what use do I have for you?”
Zuo Xian’s glasses slipped down his nose, but he didn’t dare adjust them. He swallowed hard and said, “Rest assured, sir. I’ll clean this up completely.”
“Two hours,” the man replied flatly. “If it’s not done by then, I’ll handle it myself. But when I’m done, there won’t be a deputy named Zuo Xian left in the Red Scorpions.”
The line went dead.
Zuo Xian wiped the sweat from his temple, barking orders to his men as they prepared to descend onto a small, unexplored planet nearby.
Meanwhile, with Ji Chenxi’s crew—
