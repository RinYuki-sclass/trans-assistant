“I get the logic. But he really does seem close to you.” After circling through all the heavy talk, Ji Chenxi steered the topic back to the very start.
“We are pretty close. I’ve known him for eleven years. In those eleven years, he hasn’t beaten me once.”
Every single time, he’d lost. That kind of “close” was definitely not something Lan Mu would want. Chi Zhuo was making it clear—at his core, he and Lan Mu weren’t that close at all.
“Actually, I don’t mind you having friends.”
“Reverse talk?”
Ji Chenxi hadn’t expected that his rare moment of generosity would get that kind of reply. For a second, he didn’t know whether to laugh or get annoyed. “I’m really starting to wonder if you see me as some sharp-tongued villain.”
“Nothing like that.”
“Oh? Reverse talk?” Ji Chenxi smiled without quite smiling, neatly tossing the same words back at him.
Chi Zhuo was blocked for a moment, speechless, but his brows eased a little. On his usually cold face, a faint hint of a smile appeared. “Maybe. Your Highness, want to guess whether that was the truth or reverse talk?”
Ji Chenxi shook his head, clearly not interested in playing guessing games. He pulled the topic back again. “Seriously, on a rational level, I don’t mind you having friends. I’ve got close friends myself.”
“On a rational level?” Chi Zhuo caught the key phrase right away. “So Your Highness also has irrational moments?”
“Of course. Sometimes feelings just don’t listen to reason.”
“And how do your feelings misbehave?” Chi Zhuo’s voice softened just a little.
It was the kind of moment people look forward to. In a normal young couple’s talk, no matter what they truly think, the usual line here would be something like, ‘My feelings don’t want you to have friends. I don’t want to see you that close to anyone else.’ Whether the other person takes it seriously or not, it’s still a kind of flirting. People always get pulled into that push-and-pull tension. Even Chi Zhuo wasn’t completely immune.
When Ji Chenxi heard the question, he actually looked pleased—like he had dug a little trap and the other person had obediently stepped into it. It was almost a satisfied smile.
He leaned closer with that smile, moving right up to Chi Zhuo’s ear, and whispered, “Of course it wants to lock you up. Make it so you can’t see anyone but me. Leave you in the dark for so long that the only things you hear are your own breathing, your own heartbeat… and my voice. You’d slowly start to rely on me. You wouldn’t be able to leave me…”
Chi Zhuo’s breathing grew a little heavier, though it was hard to tell whether it was because of Ji Chenxi’s closeness—or the threat woven into those words.
