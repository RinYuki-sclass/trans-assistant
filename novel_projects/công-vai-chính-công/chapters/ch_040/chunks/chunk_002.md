“It’s true I wanted to get the mineral rose. But buying flowers for myself is fine. Accepting flowers from someone else… doesn’t feel right,” Ji Chenxi explained calmly, his reasoning clear.
“You not only refuse to give me flowers, but even the ones I give you are rejected. Seems like Third Prince really wants to distance himself from me.”
Ji Chenxi tilted his head slightly, smiling. “Saying it like that makes me sound cold and heartless. It’s just… I can’t accept gifts for no reason. Taking something from someone without cause would make me feel guilty.”
“Feel guilty?”
They were very close. As Ji Chenxi smiled, Chi Zhuo reached out, brushing the tail of his eyebrow, then tracing down to gently caress the small mole under his right eye. After saying that, Chi Zhuo added on his own, “Then Third Prince should just do something that won’t make himself feel guilty.”
“For example?” Ji Chenxi asked, smiling at him again.
This smile had nothing to do with elegance. It had a faint, strange wickedness, like a charming little demon whispering mischief into your mind.
“For example… just stand still and let me kiss you.”
“Oh?” Ji Chenxi’s voice dragged slightly at the end. “So my kiss is worth that much?”
“Your Highness, priceless as gold and jade. Naturally, your kiss is valuable too.”
Ji Chenxi chuckled. If that were really true, Chi Zhuo probably already owed him a fortune.
Chi Zhuo’s words “priceless as gold and jade” weren’t wrong. But just a few light strokes of his calloused fingers across Ji Chenxi’s skin brought a faint flush to his pale face. That small bloom of color made Chi Zhuo’s breath unconsciously hitch.
“I wonder… could I call Your Highness something else?”
“For example?” Ji Chenxi repeated, smiling, though this smile had a slightly different tone than before.
Ji Chenxi paused for a moment. He’d heard plenty of intimate nicknames—his mother called him “Little Rose” or “Baby,” his father “Xi’er,” even his step-siblings sometimes called him “Chenxi” or “Third Brother” as a sign of closeness. But “Rose” was a first. And the way Chi Zhuo said it felt almost like saying “Darling.”
Ji Chenxi laughed and declined gently. “I’m not really fond of people calling me by my pheromones directly.”
Chi Zhuo looked a little disappointed. “Could it be you prefer ‘Little Rose’ instead?”
“You could also just call me by my name,” Ji Chenxi suggested seriously.
“Never mind… none of that matters.”
Chi Zhuo dropped the topic of nicknames. He moved closer until he was just within reach, but instead of leaning in for a kiss, his hand slid behind Ji Chenxi.
