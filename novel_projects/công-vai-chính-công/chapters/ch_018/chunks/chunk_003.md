Ji Chenxi frowned tightly. With a gentle motion, the hand indeed slid off his arm.
He reached out to touch Chi Zhuo’s forehead, feeling the astonishing heat. He shook his head and sighed, wondering if he had altered the original story too much—was that why Chi Zhuo was about to die now?
Before Ji Chenxi could pull back his hand, Chi Zhuo, clearly still weak, opened his eyes again and asked, “Your Highness… didn’t you want to kill me before?”
“You’re really not afraid of me losing my temper by saying that outright.”
“You’re holy and gentle, Your Highness. I figured you wouldn’t.”
“Cut the mystical talk,” Ji Chenxi muttered, slightly annoyed, though he didn’t act on impulse this time.
“Then… tell me, why the sudden change of heart? Not only did you not kill me, but you want to save me?”
“The night is long. If you die, it’ll just be boring for me alone,” Ji Chenxi replied in his usual casual tone.
“Looks like that isn’t the real answer.”
“Oh? Then maybe I’m just a man of my word. There aren’t many contract partners like me.”
“Any other answers?”
“I’m in a good mood today. Enjoy it—I don’t help people for fun very often.”
With that, Ji Chenxi ignored Chi Zhuo and reached for the silk strip again. But Chi Zhuo tugged at his arm once more. “Your Highness, the water’s hot. You can wait for it to cool a little before wringing it out.”
Ji Chenxi nodded, grabbed a small stick, and lifted the silk strip. The soft dripping of water rang out.
While it cooled, Ji Chenxi kept an eye on Chi Zhuo from the corner of his eye. People said he was gentle, but Chi Zhuo’s actions—so careful, so considerate—were true, unfiltered gentleness. Was the man being burned silly? He was at that rare stage where someone on the verge of death speaks kindly. Maybe even a faint glimmer of recovery, because moments ago, he had been dangerously close to fainting, yet now, with half-closed eyes, he was still talking to Ji Chenxi at length.
After wringing out the silk strip, Ji Chenxi placed it back on Chi Zhuo’s forehead. He then picked up the water in the lid. The night was bitterly cold; the boiling water had cooled slightly, now just hotter than drinkable.
He brought the lid to Chi Zhuo’s lips. “Drink.”
Chi Zhuo drank all the water along the path of Ji Chenxi’s hand. He looked at the figure bathed in the golden glow of the firelight and spoke slowly, his voice drawn out, “You really don’t need to trouble yourself for me.”
“Who said I’m a kind person? Seeing you half-dead like this, I just want to lend a hand,” Ji Chenxi said expressionlessly, speaking words entirely unlike himself.
Sure enough, Chi Zhuo let out a low, muffled laugh at Ji Chenxi’s words.
