Crow: [I don’t think I misjudged him.]
In that moment, the layer of the hot-headed online persona peeled away. The one typing wasn’t the rough, loud-mouthed Crow—it was the gentle, noble friend who, before joining the group, had said to him when they first spoke about the heir to the throne: ‘If you want it, I’ll help you. If you don’t, I’ll do my best to protect you.’
Ji Chenxi let out a quiet sigh. He wasn’t quite as perfect as his friend thought he was.
Manye Zhe: [You guys are making me really embarrassed. I even complained before that the Third Prince was just a pretty face, nothing else.]
Manye Zhe’s despair broke the slightly serious mood that had settled over the group.
Morning Star peeked in on Ji Chenxi privately again.
[Your friend really did infiltrate our group to recruit people for you! Who would’ve thought you joined five years before him?]
The little group was a bit exclusive. Back then, everyone was fairly steady, so Crow’s joining was acceptable—partly because he had a personal connection with Traveler, and partly because Bai Wei and Morning Star approved.
[He’s not recruiting. He just wants to keep me alive if my big brother or second sister tries to grab power.]
[Your Highness, you really don’t seem interested in that position at all?]
Ji Chenxi knew what Morning Star really meant: ‘You’re just pretending to be disinterested outwardly, aren’t you?’
The truth was slightly different. At first, Ji Chenxi really wasn’t interested in that position. Some people crave power, and some despise it—but sadly, the position he was in meant he couldn’t just sit back and ignore the struggle. He had to compete, whether he wanted to or not.
Nightfall Auction House.
An auction house that occasionally brought in something fresh and unusual.
In remote star systems, the people themselves were the highlight of the auctions—modified humans, robots, and more. On the Imperial Star, such things would never be displayed openly, but subtle, hidden methods were plentiful.
Before leaving, Ji Chenxi flipped through that book once more, scanning the pages full of romantic drama to find the hidden threads of the story.
This auction was mainly about Bai Anran changing his mind about Chi Zhuo. From the overflowing hints of flirtation, Ji Chenxi finally caught a glimpse of the undercurrents in the auction.
The person who almost harmed Bai Anran was probably the prey they had been trying to lure. And the fact that Chi Zhuo saved Bai Anran just in time—rather than a simple hero-rescues-beauty scenario—wasn’t just about the pheromone attraction. It was more like someone was cutting in on their plans.
This time, Ji Chenxi deliberately didn’t invite Bai Anran, but as he entered the auction house, he still saw him—speaking with Chi Zhuo.
One of them was talking, the other listening with their head slightly bowed.
