Two hours, thirty-eight minutes, and fifty-six seconds.
Chi Zhuo froze, stunned by the precision of the number.
If a person wasn’t counting every second, how could they possibly know?
A little over two hours ago—that was exactly when the other person had replied to his message.
Chi Zhuo’s heart, which had been holding onto some semblance of calm, could no longer maintain its usual composure. Now it pounded wildly against his chest, reckless and violent, like a drum being beaten, and the thought he had not long ago—about giving someone a proper lesson—vanished silently in an instant.
Ji Chenxi stepped closer, each move radiating force. His pheromones, intensified by the sensitive period, hit Chi Zhuo like a strong, overbearing wave. Any other alpha would probably feel nauseous or even want to retch under such nearly tangible pheromones. Yet Chi Zhuo was different. Somehow, beneath that forceful scent, he sensed a hint of grievance.
Grievance? A word so unsuitable for an alpha, especially one as fierce and violent as this.
The tense, blade-sharp standoff between them had shifted entirely.
Under Ji Chenxi’s ferocious gaze, Chi Zhuo said, “I never said I’d definitely come.”
Ji Chenxi let out a cold snort, clearly dismissive. Yet his forward stride paused, then stopped entirely.
A sliver of remaining reason—worn thin by the torment of the sensitive period—reawakened in Ji Chenxi. Slowly, he remembered something: he had just said something like “come find me if you dare” to Chi Zhuo. Chi Zhuo hadn’t responded, hadn’t even indicated he would come. If anything, the fact that he showed up at all was a surprise.
But that bit of logic was quickly tossed aside. An alpha in the sensitive period wasn’t one for reasoning—otherwise countless omegas and betas wouldn’t suffer so much trying to handle their sensitive partners.
The result? Ji Chenxi wasn’t calmed down at all. On the contrary, he only grew more restless and irritated.
When he didn’t see the person, he grew uneasy because his alpha hadn’t shown up—fragile to the point of breaking. Once the person arrived, half of that unease melted away, leaving only the dark thoughts that anxiety had stirred.
“So you mean… the fact that you could come now is already good enough?”
Before Chi Zhuo could reply, Ji Chenxi added, “If that’s what you think, then you can leave right now. I won’t stop you.”
His deep-sea-blue eyes narrowed slightly, and he pointed to the window Chi Zhuo had just climbed in through. The message was clear—he wanted him gone.
But if he really wanted him to leave, why would he feel hurt when Chi Zhuo hadn’t come?
Kids who knew how to act spoiled got candy. And yet, a kid who was used to being spoiled suddenly refused to be coddled.
Chi Zhuo felt a twinge of discomfort, his eyes dark as midnight, darker even than the night sky outside.
