Meanwhile, the man with the golden-rimmed glasses had already arrived in his own ship, his expression growing darker and darker. The piloting on the other side was so sharp, so practiced, that he had the sinking feeling they’d soon be unable to keep up.
“We have to stop them from jumping. Increase firepower!”
“But Deputy Commander, if we go too hard, won’t we risk drawing the military’s attention?” Amis muttered nervously.
“Idiot. Why do you think we’ve been doing so well up to now?”
Amis fell silent and obediently ramped up the firepower.
Just then, the man with the golden-rimmed glasses received a communication—and his face instantly changed.
Amis, firing frantically at the ships ahead, simultaneously relayed her deputy commander’s orders. Out of the corner of her eye, she noticed the deputy’s grim expression and tried to reassure him. “Deputy Commander, we’ve already called in our best squads. With so many units surrounding them, they won’t hold out long.”
“He’s here.”
“Hm?” Amis’s hand froze for a moment, a clear look of fear spreading across her face.
“He came personally.” The man with the golden-rimmed glasses removed his spectacles to wipe them, his partially closed eyes mirroring the same fear as Amis.
“The one” referred to their captain. Most had only heard of the Red Scorpions’ fearsome reputation, trembling at the name itself, without realizing that the captain was the real nightmare for every villain in the Red Scorpions.
“Amis, chase him!”
The frozen Amis snapped back to full alert, pushing the ship harder and turning the firepower up even more than before.
The news that their captain was arriving sent the Red Scorpions into total frenzy. They began attacking recklessly, without any regard for consequences. Chi Zhuo and Ji Chenxi, the ones being pursued, immediately noticed the change.
“Brother Chi Zhuo, how long until your people get here to save us?” Ji Chenxi asked.
“If it were earlier, they’d probably make it before you die. Now… it’s hard to say.”
“Hm? So that’s why they’re so reckless?” Ji Chenxi probed lightly. He was even starting to regret that the book had been written from Bai Anran’s perspective, only showing him a tiny glimpse of Chi Zhuo’s iceberg-like persona.
“Have you never heard of the Red Scorpions before?” Chi Zhuo asked.
“A bit, in current affairs class,” Ji Chenxi admitted. He wasn’t much interested in military matters, so he’d never studied the structure of pirate fleets in detail.
Chi Zhuo didn’t comment further, probably realizing that Third Prince had only just turned eighteen and hadn’t even started university yet.
