Maybe a child’s affection for their parents is something they’re born with. Or maybe it’s just human nature—wanting more of what you can’t get. Ji Chenxi had a near-obsessive need for his mother’s approval. Chi Zhuo couldn’t fully relate, but he still stood by Ji Chenxi’s side. At the very least, he showed up less in front of that woman, and when he did, he tried his best to keep himself from looking too threatening.
For a predator, hiding their wild nature was never easy. Yet no matter what he did, it always felt like Ji Chenxi noticed. The kid would get excited because of his restraint… but after that excitement faded, there was always a hint of sadness left behind.
The little bundle of a child stroked the black little snake. “Little snake, I think I made you suffer.”
The little snake licked Ji Chenxi’s finger, as if saying: *good, you know it.*
But honestly, whether it was suffering or not didn’t really matter. This kind of pretending wasn’t anything serious. What mattered was that the child could be a little happier because of it.
Chi Zhuo originally thought the kid would praise his considerate spirit beast. Instead, Ji Chenxi pulled the snake tightly into his arms.
“You don’t need to suffer just for me,” he said softly. “You’re mine. I should be the one protecting you, not making you step back for me. I just complained a little… did you really understand? Or is it because you’re a spirit beast, so you just follow my will?”
If that were true, then his closest companion would be nothing more than a lie.
Not a companion. Not comfort for the mind. Just another form of self-deception.
Chi Zhuo had only wanted to make the child happier—but what he saw in Ji Chenxi’s eyes was something heavier instead: sadness.
The beautiful child had clear, bright eyes, but now they were misted over with a faint layer of tears.
It looked like he hadn’t comforted Ji Chenxi at all. In fact… he might have made him even sadder.
Chi Zhuo felt a rare sense of panic, which showed in the black snake clumsily trying to comfort him.
But Ji Chenxi had already sunk into his own world. He seemed to be looking at it… and yet not really seeing it at all.
The black snake moved around aimlessly, like someone pacing back and forth. Then suddenly, without hesitation, it slithered out the window.
The night stalker returned quickly. In its mouth was a bright, fiery red rose.
A black snake, a red rose—black and red, a color pairing artists loved most. Their existence alone was already beauty.
Ji Chenxi had shifted position, now sitting under the moonlight, eyes lowered as he thought about something.
At this moment, he didn’t even fully count as a teenager yet. He was young, soft, still someone who should be guided and protected by adults—but there wasn’t a trace of childish innocence in him.
Chi Zhuo had brought back something that wasn’t precious at all—a single beautiful rose.
