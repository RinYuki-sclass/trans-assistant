“Your Highness must be joking.”
Chi Zhuo’s reply came a beat slower than usual—but that pause was just right.
And that tiny delay alone was enough to please Ji Chenxi. A smile tugged at his lips. “Yeah, I was joking. Senior, don’t take it seriously.”
Ji Chenxi said goodbye without the slightest sign of hesitation. Just like he’d said before, he first sent out his spiritual power to check the situation outside. The area beyond the underground maze didn’t look much different from before—still packed with people. The only change was that they weren’t here for Chi Zhuo and the poisonous spider anymore.
They were here for him.
Ji Chenxi took out two gold coins, each stamped with the emblems of the Royal Academy and the First Military Academy. He tossed them lightly into the air, catching them with lazy ease. Now that Chi Zhuo didn’t need to take part in this game anymore, his hunger for those huge points had faded. He even found himself wondering if he shouldn’t have used Lu Jianhan’s hand earlier. If he hadn’t, maybe he could’ve fought a little longer. But when you’ve got a ready-made card in hand and don’t use it, that doesn’t make much sense either.
After a quick scan of the outside, Ji Chenxi turned and headed back into the cave.
Lu Jianhan fell silent for a brief moment when he saw Ji Chenxi return so soon. Then he asked, “You’re not back because you’re eyeing the points in my hand, are you?”
Ji Chenxi raised a brow. “I’m already the undisputed first place—so long as these two gold coins in my hand don’t get snatched away.”
“If Your Highness wanted to take our points, you could’ve done it earlier. No need to wait until now.” Bai Anran knew exactly how many points he had. Among the other freshmen, his score would count as high—but compared to Ji Chenxi’s, it wasn’t even a drop in the ocean. “So you came back because you want us to guard your points?”
Ji Chenxi nodded in approval. “Even though I could handle it alone—those people probably couldn’t take my points anyway—there are still several days left. I’ll need to rest at some point.”
There were only three S-rank freshmen in total. If two of them stood on his side, then the rest of the assessment would feel like playing house with kids.
Lu Jianhan and Bai Anran were still suffering from the poison debuff. No one had shown up with an antidote, so for now, they had no choice but to rely on Ji Chenxi. Lu Jianhan had already been forced into cooperating with him once under pressure, so he didn’t care if it happened again.
As for the others, even though a few of them were A+ rank, they couldn’t possibly handle being hunted down by all the freshmen from two top academies. On the surface, it looked like Ji Chenxi had coldly abandoned them to avoid being dragged down. In truth, though, he had given them a chance—to blend into the other freshmen and stop living like fugitives.
After that, the next few days turned into a full-on manhunt. The other freshmen went crazy trying to chase Ji Chenxi down, all hoping to snatch the points from his hands. The problem was simple—no one could beat any of the three of them. Even Bai Anran, who looked like the weakest link, was a solid S-rank. In some ways, he could totally crush an A-rank without breaking a sweat.
And since most of the freshmen had zero real combat experience, even the way they tried to hunt them down felt clumsy—almost cute. Ji Chenxi didn’t take them seriously at all.
The grand freshman trial was practically turning into a camping trip thanks to him. Lu Jianhan handled catching fish and hunting prey. Bai Anran gathered wild vegetables and mushrooms. Lu Jianhan cooked and made soup too. As for Ji Chenxi? He just ate, drank, and occasionally bullied a few freshmen for fun.
Lu Jianhan, who had to find prey, prep the ingredients, and cook every meal: “…”
He seriously suspected Ji Chenxi brought him along just to get a free cook.
With such an overwhelming gap in strength, the final stretch of the trial felt even easier than when the senior students were around. At least among the older students, there had been quite a few who could actually fight. But most of the freshmen? They didn’t even know how to properly use their spiritual power or apply real combat skills.
