“…You really understood, didn’t you?” The Association President asked, his voice full of doubt. His son had always been obedient. But today, his compliant attitude was strangely irritating.

“Yes.” Cheon Sang-hyun replied calmly. No emotion was discernible in his clear, ash-gray eyes. This was the Cheon Sang-hyun the Association President knew. Still, something didn’t sit right with him, but it seemed there was nothing more he could pry out.

“Alright. You may go.” “Yes.” Cheon Sang-hyun bowed politely, then turned and left without a sound. The Association President didn’t take his eyes off Cheon Sang-hyun until he closed the door and disappeared. As soon as his presence was completely gone, the Association President immediately called his secretary.

[Yes, Mr. President.] “Assign more people to my son. I want a thorough report on what he’s doing, who he’s meeting, from text records to call logs.” [Understood.]

After the brief conversation, the Association President looked down at the few documents still scattered on the floor. Strangely, there was no report containing photos of Won Hwi-do. That wasn’t an issue.

The problem was his son. There was definitely something he didn’t know. It was the intuition of the Association President, who had risen to his position through all sorts of schemes and stratagems. His intuition was rarely wrong. So, he had to set things straight now.

His son had to remain a loyal dog, no more, no less. If he tried to step outside the fence his father had built, then it wouldn’t be overlooked as it was now.

The Association President took out his phone from his pocket. Soon, he made a call somewhere. Before long, a voice could be heard from the other end of the phone.

[Hello, Mr. President! What can I do for you?] The moment he heard the Vice President’s voice, thick with flattery, the Association President’s lips curled into a silent smile. Never had the voice of this hyena-like man, who treated him like a puppet, been so welcome.

“Come see me for a moment.”

Won Hwi-do’s red eyes glanced at his phone with dissatisfaction. But the phone was silent. No one had contacted him. There was no need to pay it much mind. He wasn’t the type to dwell on such things.

Or so he thought.
