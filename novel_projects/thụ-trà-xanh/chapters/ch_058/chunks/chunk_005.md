Both of them knew that even if they reported it, the Federation was unlikely to intervene.
District Seven was far too chaotic. With its tangled web of various industries, no leader was willing to deal with such a mess.
They didn't have the kind of power that could reach the heavens; they could only do their best to help.
The four of them rented a car and first circled the city to scout the terrain.
The photography and paperwork were handled by the secretaries; as the bosses, Gu Suizhou and Shen Feizhi’s job was to observe.
"Actually, when I buy weapons, I sometimes go through the District Seven Central Bank's accounts too," Gu Suizhou whispered into Shen Feizhi’s ear in the backseat. "But that bank you told me about—I’ve never even heard of it."
"It seems your influence isn't quite 'black' enough yet," Shen Feizhi remarked. "You should have looked at that bank earlier. It’s obscure, but the volume of transactions is enormous. Its nature is already self-evident."
Gu Suizhou nodded in agreement. "Where do we… start?"
"Look over there." Shen Feizhi tilted his chin toward the window. "The bank's IP location."
He was pointing at a standalone, dilapidated ATM embedded in the entrance of a derelict building. The building was a guesthouse, and judging by the pile of trash at the main entrance, it wasn't a clean place. Indeed, the flow of people going in and out was pitifully small.
One look was enough for the two of them to tell that there were certainly some more clandestine dealings going on inside that building.
Shen Feizhi glanced at Gu Suizhou, who understood instantly. Gu Suizhou pulled a mechanical mouse from his pocket and tossed it out through the crack in the car window.
Shen Feizhi covered his face in distaste. "Your surveillance is getting more and more 'creative.'"

They changed course to find a hotel where they could stay.
