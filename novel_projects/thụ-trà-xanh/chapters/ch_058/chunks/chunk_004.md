“Not necessarily. Who’s to say District Seven’s banking system isn’t the best on Blue Star?” Gu Suizhou said in a joking tone. “After all, the more chaotic it is, the easier it is to find cover.”
“True.” Shen Feizhi nodded in approval. “We’ll know for sure once we arrive.”
Even though they had prepared themselves for a massive wealth gap, the group remained speechless for a long time when they finally laid eyes on District Seven—it was even worse than they had imagined.
Even the two secretaries were stunned by the sight before them as they stepped out of the car.
The heavy-industrial city was choked with dust and smog, the air thick and oppressive. The desolate roads were filled with dark masses of people hurrying along; street vendors shouted their wares, their cries occasionally punctuated by accented arguments. Piles of trash cluttered the alley entrances, stained with blood and discarded clothes, and even muggers went about their business in broad daylight.
While District Eight and the Ninth District were poor, at least their public security was decent.
District Seven was the very definition of chaotic squalor.
Shen Feizhi lowered his eyes and said flatly, "Keep an eye on your smartphone. Don't let it get stolen."
Gu Suizhou felt deeply uncomfortable. "It’s been a long time since I was last here. It wasn't this bad before."
"You’ve been here before?" Shen Feizhi asked. "What for?"
Gu Suizhou had just bought a few masks on the street. He answered as he helped the other man put one on. "I… have some industries here."
The kind of industries that couldn't see the light of day.
District Seven was a city of vice, inextricably linked to underground trades.
"Then you should be able to guess why this place is getting worse." Shen Feizhi handed masks to the two secretaries. "Only this kind of environment is suitable for doing that dirty work."
Environments like this were the result of the wealthy's indulgence; it was even highly probable that those people were secretly pushing to keep things this way on purpose.
