Inside lay a broken wooden carving of a small fox.
The carving was no larger than half a palm, made from the cheapest, most common wood. The carving itself was crude, the kind no one would buy even from a roadside stall.
The wooden carving was coated in a protective oil, and even the wood itself had undergone advanced anti-termite treatment. It was clear how much its owner cherished it.

The little fox lay quietly on the ground, sleeping. Two sharp canines peeked out from its slightly parted mouth, making it look both dangerous and adorable.
And cradled in its arms was…
A tiny crucifix.
Tragically, both the little fox and the crucifix had been snapped in half.
Gu Suizhou stroked the broken edges with heartache, even bending down to gently kiss the fractured join.
The carving had actually been broken long ago, but he'd had it carefully repaired, the restored piece looking as good as new.
This was the only thing he had left connected to that person .
He had been forced to leave in such a rush last time that he hadn't had time to bring it. He never imagined Lin Sui would find it—and deliberately smash it.
At this thought, the usually tender and flirtatious glint in his fox-like eyes turned cold and ruthless, his lashes fluttering rapidly.
He'd been too lenient with Lin Sui today. Next time they met, he'd break at least one of his arms.
Satisfied that the carving was merely cracked and otherwise undamaged, Gu Suizhou sighed in relief. He carefully rewrapped it and tucked it into the breast pocket of his shirt, close to his heart.
"Uncle Zhong," he murmured, "I think I had another episode the other night."
"Could you get me two more doses of the medicine?"
Gu Suizhou suffered from a rare form of mental illness, a secret known to very few.
Not even Leapton was aware of it.
