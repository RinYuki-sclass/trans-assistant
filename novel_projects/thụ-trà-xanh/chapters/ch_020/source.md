Chapter 20

After his initial attempts to weasel out of it, Gu Suizhou had worried Shen Feizhi might impulsively force himself on him.

But now he truly understood: his concerns had been utterly unfounded.

Shen Feizhi paid no heed to Gu Suizhou's evasive tactics, acting like an ascetic monk, detached from worldly desires and untainted by earthly concerns.

"What business is it of yours how I use you? Are you defying your benefactor now? Even if I ordered you to empty a toilet, could you refuse?"

Gu Suizhou: "……"

Fine, you win.

Once Shen Feizhi had finally finished giving his instructions, Gu Suizhou immediately sent location messages to Xu Zhong and Leapton.

"Then go take a shower first. I'll get a couple of helpers to come cook noodles for you. How's that?"

"Helpers? Is this some kind of forced-purchase scheme?"

"I'll cover their expenses myself—no extra charge."

"That works." Shen Feizhi glanced at his watch. "But I'm not showering now. I need to study."

It was study time.

Knowledge was the only true currency that allowed a person to stand tall anywhere!

Gu Suizhou's gaze gradually shifted from numb indifference to utter horror.

How could anyone be so obsessed with studying?

He stared blankly as Shen Feizhi steadily rose, calmly walked into the study, and calmly picked up a book from the desk…

[ Bringing You into the Gray Zone Between Dreams and Reality ] .

Gu Suizhou's Adam's apple bobbed. He ultimately didn't dare push his luck too far.

He respectfully closed the door behind him.

Sorry about that.

The creature inside wasn't of this world—he'd probably transcended dimensions. I shouldn't have disturbed him.

Through the closed door, Gu Suizhou quipped, "Hey Brother Shen, study hard! Try for top of the class!"

Shen Feizhi's muffled voice replied, "Mm."

Gu Suizhou's expression froze.

He stalked over to the sofa, face stiff, and suddenly threw himself down, slamming his face into the soft cushions twice.

Holy shit!

Shen Feizhi is so damn obedient!

He rolled around on the sofa twice, then sat cross-legged in the spot Shen Feizhi had just occupied, lost in thought. A stray glance caught the living room mirror.

His tongue pressed against the roof of his mouth, and his beautiful fox-like eyes narrowed.

This angle…

Shen Feizhi, you sneaky bastard! How could you be playing these underhanded games too?!

Obedient my ass!

Not obedient at all!

He leaped up in a fury and repositioned the mirror.

He shifted it from an angle where Shen Feizhi could see his bedroom to one where the dressing room was visible from any angle.

There. Now it's fair.

No one's getting the drop on anyone!

Xu Zhong and Leapton rushed to the address only to find the kitchen filled with smoke.

The two men exchanged glances, speechless.

"Gu Suizhou, are you trying to kill my Mr. Shen?" Leapton asked, leaning against the kitchen doorway with a complex expression as he watched Gu Suizhou dump another pot of sludge-like batter into the trash—his third attempt.

"If you really can't cook, just have the robotic maid make him something. There's no need to torture yourself like this."

"You think I haven't tried that?" Gu Suizhou snapped, his face darkening. "That old grump won't eat it!"

He had initially instructed the AI to prepare the meal, but Shen Feizhi hadn't even put a noodle in his mouth before disdainfully setting the plate on the nightstand. "I pay you to operate machines, not have them do everything for you!"

Gu Suizhou had nearly smeared the noodles across his face in fury.

Fortunately, his rationality prevailed. He knew he was to blame for Shen Feizhi's drunken state tonight and had to swallow his anger, retreating sheepishly to the kitchen.

"Perfect timing," he exhaled heavily, shoving the apron into Leapton's hands. "Your Mr. Shen is holed up inside, half-dead from his academic pursuits, and hasn't eaten dinner yet. Here's your chance to impress him."

Gu Suizhou had already briefed them on the situation via his smartphone, and Leapton immediately understood.

He rolled up his sleeves. "Don't worry! Leave it to me! Tonight, I'll make Mr. Shen fall head over heels for my apron!"

Gu Suizhou wanted to wish him luck, but the words caught in his throat. For some reason, he swallowed them back.

"Oh," he replied, and returned to the living room with Xu Zhong.

"Uncle Zhong," he said, handing over an electronic file, "here's the detailed layout of the Shen family's old mansion and all the surveillance feeds I could access."

"Understood, Young Master," Xu Zhong replied, his face still covered by a thick mask and black-framed glasses even indoors, leaving only a pair of murky, small eyes visible. "I'll have a detailed analysis map for you within three days."

"Thank you for your hard work," Gu Suizhou said, pinching the bridge of his nose wearily.

Neither of them spoke again.

They sat on opposite ends of the sofa, the silence between them unnervingly heavy.

After a long while, Gu Suizhou suddenly picked up the black suit he'd worn earlier that evening. He carefully pulled out a handkerchief-wrapped object from the inner pocket and unwrapped it.

Inside lay a broken wooden carving of a small fox.

The carving was no larger than half a palm, made from the cheapest, most common wood. The carving itself was crude, the kind no one would buy even from a roadside stall.

The wooden carving was coated in a protective oil, and even the wood itself had undergone advanced anti-termite treatment. It was clear how much its owner cherished it.

The little fox lay quietly on the ground, sleeping. Two sharp canines peeked out from its slightly parted mouth, making it look both dangerous and adorable.

And cradled in its arms was…

A tiny crucifix.

Tragically, both the little fox and the crucifix had been snapped in half.

Gu Suizhou stroked the broken edges with heartache, even bending down to gently kiss the fractured join.

The carving had actually been broken long ago, but he'd had it carefully repaired, the restored piece looking as good as new.

This was the only thing he had left connected to that person .

He had been forced to leave in such a rush last time that he hadn't had time to bring it. He never imagined Lin Sui would find it—and deliberately smash it.

At this thought, the usually tender and flirtatious glint in his fox-like eyes turned cold and ruthless, his lashes fluttering rapidly.

He'd been too lenient with Lin Sui today. Next time they met, he'd break at least one of his arms.

Satisfied that the carving was merely cracked and otherwise undamaged, Gu Suizhou sighed in relief. He carefully rewrapped it and tucked it into the breast pocket of his shirt, close to his heart.

"Uncle Zhong," he murmured, "I think I had another episode the other night."

"Could you get me two more doses of the medicine?"

Gu Suizhou suffered from a rare form of mental illness, a secret known to very few.

Not even Leapton was aware of it.

And afterward, he remembered nothing.
