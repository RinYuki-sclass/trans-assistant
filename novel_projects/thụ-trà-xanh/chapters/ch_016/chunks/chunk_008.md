Lin Sui didn't notice their little exchange. His smile remained unchanged, though the stem of the wine glass nearly snapped in his grip.
"I see. Xiao Zhou is quite immature. Thank you, Second Young Master Shen, for looking after him these past few days," he said, nodding gratefully to Shen Feizhi. Then he pulled a small, broken wooden carving from his pocket. "Oh, Xiao Zhou, I was cleaning the house the other day and accidentally—"
"Lin Sui."
Gu Suizhou suddenly spoke, his voice barely concealing his rage. The oppressive atmosphere around him drew frequent sidelong glances from onlookers.
"Are you courting death?"
Shen Feizhi, genuinely surprised, widened his eyes, trying to get a better look at the broken wooden carving. What precious artifact could possibly provoke Gu Suizhou to drop his facade like this?
Before he could examine it properly, Gu Suizhou snatched the carving away, carefully smoothed it with his fingers, and wrapped it in a handkerchief like a priceless treasure before tucking it into his pocket.
Clenching his jaw, Gu Suizhou spoke each word deliberately: "We… need to talk elsewhere."
"Xiao Zhou, I didn't mean to," Lin Sui said, flustered, especially as more people turned to stare. He quickly stepped forward, reaching for Gu Suizhou's hand. "Don't be angry."
Gu Suizhou ruthlessly dodged his touch.
Lin Sui's eyes reddened slightly.
"In public, especially in front of… Give me some face, brother," he said, glancing awkwardly at Shen Feizhi, his meaning clear.
But Shen Feizhi acted as if he hadn't heard a word, giving Gu Suizhou's nape an ambiguous squeeze and murmuring in a low, nonchalant voice, "Control your emotions. Don't give anyone ammunition."
Gu Suizhou felt an itch at the back of his neck. Even through the glove, he could almost feel the person's slightly cool, rough fingertips.
