Chapter 59

Gu Suizhou didn’t like hospitals, so Shen Feizhi didn’t linger. After contacting the director to collect the hospital's data, he led him away.

The two didn't choose to call a cab for the return trip, instead walking aimlessly through the streets wearing masks.

One reason was to let the hospital smell cling to their clothes dissipate; the other was because Gu Suizhou was in a bad mood, and Shen Feizhi wanted to accompany him on a walk to clear his head.

Gu Suizhou wanted to take his hand, but Shen Feizhi discreetly shoved his hands into his pockets. Left with no choice but to settle for the next best thing, Gu Suizhou grabbed the corner of the other man's coat. "We won't be able to wear these clothes anymore once we get back."

The smell was too mixed and unpleasant.

Shen Feizhi looked pointedly at the hem of his coat. "You seem to be quite fond of them, though."

Gu Suizhou smiled without saying a word, but his hand didn't loosen its grip in the slightest.

Shen Feizhi didn't bother saying more.

As they passed a small stall selling candied fruits, Shen Feizhi suddenly stopped and asked, "Want some tanghulu?"

Gu Suizhou paused, startled.

Shen Feizhi was patient and asked again, "Do you want a stick?"

Gu Suizhou pursed his lips. "I haven't eaten them in a long time."

Not since the year his parents died, when his tanghulu fell to the ground.

He had never eaten them again.

Shen Feizhi hummed in affirmation and said to the vendor, "I’ll take two."

Gu Suizhou was about to ask if he liked this kind of thing too, only to see both sticks of hawthorn candy placed in front of him.

Shen Feizhi said, "Eat. They’re both yours."

In that instant, Gu Suizhou’s eyes suddenly stung.

When he didn't take them, Shen Feizhi personally brought one to his lips. "Open up."

Gu Suizhou lowered his long, thick lashes, masking whatever was behind them, and took a bite of the first hawthorn.

The sugar coating was crisp, followed by a faint tartness that surfaced after the initial sweetness, perfectly cutting through the cloying richness.

There was a reason people had been eating these for so long, and why they still sold so well even now, amidst a staggering variety of modern delicacies.

Gu Suizhou was usually very good at playing weak in front of Shen Feizhi, but this time was an exception; he didn't want to be sad.

Taking the sticks, he waited a moment before softly saying, "Thank you."

Shen Feizhi helped him tuck a stray strand of hair behind his ear. "Let’s go."

They didn't head toward the hotel, instead wandering aimlessly for a couple of laps.

Gu Suizhou couldn't finish two sticks of candied hawthorns by himself, of course, so in the end, Shen Feizhi helped him finish half.

He brandished the empty bamboo skewer in his hand. "How much longer do you think they’re planning to tail us?"

"My bet is two more blocks before they lose their patience." Gu Suizhou took the skewer from him and meticulously wiped his hands with a wet wipe.

Shen Feizhi raised an eyebrow and gave an approving nod.

They had realized they were being followed the moment they stepped out of the hospital. It was surprising how much restraint the tail was showing; a full hour had passed, and they still hadn't made a move.

"They aren't sure of our objective yet, so of course they won't risk tipping us off," Gu Suizhou said. "It’s a good thing we had the company’s market research to use as a cov—"

He broke off mid-sentence, looking at Shen Feizhi with a sudden realization. "You did this on purpose?"

It wasn't that he’d decided to check the banks in District Seven because they were here on business. Rather, he had planned to investigate the banks from the very start, using the trip as a convenient way to draw the snakes out of their holes.

No wonder Shen Feizhi had agreed so readily when he asked him to accompany him to the meeting. At the time, Gu Suizhou's entire focus had been on Shen Feizhi, so he hadn't noticed that the other man had actually been controlling the rhythm of the entire meeting!

"You're only realizing this now?" Shen Feizhi chided. "What exactly were you doing during that meeting?"

“I was looking at you…” A wave of lingering fear washed over Gu Suizhou. “Thank goodness you’re on my side.”

Otherwise, he wouldn’t even know how he died.

Shen Feizhi laughed out of sheer exasperation. “Looking at me? Are you lovestruck or something?”

Gu Suizhou’s eyes lit up. “Does that mean we’re dating?!”

Shen Feizhi: “…”

He shouldn’t have bothered talking to him.

Gu Suizhou knew when to stop. He pulled back his smile and reflected on his actions. “My fault. I’ll make sure to pay attention next time and not let it get in the way of business.”

“It’s not a big deal. You did quite well,” Shen Feizhi’s assessment was, as always, fair and impartial. “When I was about your age, I doubt I would have fared much better.”

“That’s the age gap according to our Blue Star ID cards,” Shen Feizhi said. “In reality, I’ve probably lived thousands of years longer than you.”

Belatedly, Gu Suizhou recalled the things Shen Feizhi had told him before.

The corners of his mouth sank.

Some might envy Shen Feizhi’s long lifespan, but all Gu Suizhou could think about was how much pain Shen Feizhi must have endured—to have met so many people over such a vast stretch of time, only to end up all alone in the end.

Shen Feizhi paid no mind to his sudden bout of gloom. "Heads up. They’re losing patience."

The two intentionally steered their path toward the street with the ATM. As expected, the presence of those tailing them grew markedly more palpable.

Gu Suizhou snapped out of it. "Wipe 'em out?"

Shen Feizhi: "…"

He shot him a sharp glare.

Gu Suizhou offered a placating grin. "Just a joke. Should I knock them out and dump them back where they came from, or what?"

"If you have to ask me for everything, what use are you?" Shen Feizhi retorted testily.

"So I'm making the call?" Gu Suizhou rubbed his chin. "Honestly, I think they're useless for anything except wasting our time."

"Oh?" Shen Feizhi’s interest piqued. "And your reasoning?"

"The fact that they're only moving now shows they haven't a clue what we’re up to. We've been circling for ages without a peep from them, yet they suddenly radiate clear murderous intent the moment we hit this spot. If they were key players, they'd have to be remarkably brainless." Shen Feizhi chuckled, offered a casual "not bad," and continued walking toward the bank.

Gu Suizhou followed him, a bit puzzled. "Are we making our move today?"

"Huh?" Gu Suizhou, who habitually filtered out everyone except Shen Feizhi, looked up. "…Who's that?"

"While you were packing back at the hotel, I checked the surveillance and saw her head to an ATM," Shen Feizhi said composedly. "So I had your little mice gnaw her tires flat. It's perfect timing—she's just about to head home."

In District Seven, everyone was busy just trying to stay alive; people were cold, and it was rare for anyone to proactively offer a hand.

The woman turned around in surprise. “You guys are…”

Gu Suizhou immediately asked, "Then would you like some help?"

"If it wouldn't be an inconvenience, then yes, please!" the woman said expectantly.

"It's no trouble." Gu Suizhou confidently rolled up his sleeves. "Please, have a seat over there and wait a moment."

"Alright! I’ll go buy you some water." The woman gave a grateful bow and ran toward the mall before he could refuse.

Once she was gone, Shen Feizhi crossed his arms and leaned lazily against the car with one hand. "Looking quite the gentleman, aren't you?" he remarked with amusement.

"You taught me well," Gu Suizhou replied bashfully.

He brushed back the stray hairs at his forehead and tied them into a tiny ponytail, then stood there with hands on his hips, looking dashingly at the spare tire.

Shen Feizhi was incredibly grateful that for the sake of easy movement today, he had worn cargo pants and boots, with a casual jacket over it that was easy to take off.

He briskly shrugged off the jacket, tossed it into Gu Suizhou’s arms, and pulled on a pair of hemp gloves he’d bought by the roadside. "Hold this."

Gu Suizhou caught it steadily, but before he could get a word of cheekiness out, he looked up and was so stunned by the sight before him that he nearly bit his tongue.

He had packed the luggage himself, intentionally leaving out the dress shirts and instead packing clothes that Shen Feizhi rarely wore—which meant the man was currently wearing a sleeveless tank top.

He was the quintessential type who looked lean in clothes but possessed a solid physique underneath. The muscles on his arms weren't overly bulky; they were tight, lean, and well-defined—the kind of aesthetic beauty that would make medical and art students crave to carve them off to use as a reference model.

The hem of his tank top was tucked into his waistband. Whether he was bending over or exerting force, every movement was a tantalizing pull that left one flushed with heat.

Broad shoulders, a narrow waist, and a firm, upturned rear—aside from himself, Gu Suizhou had never seen anyone embody those three descriptions so perfectly.

"You mean the right one? I did. They didn't look like the hands of someone who does manual labor."

The woman's hands weren't rough at all, but they moved with a certain stiffness, and her knuckles were quite thick. If they hadn't misseen, she even had a medicinal patch on her right wrist.

These were common ailments for people who spent years tapping away at light screens and keyboards: arthritis and tenosynovitis.

Gu Suizhou was left completely bewildered by the pat.

The double room had twin beds, much to Gu Suizhou's disappointment.

Seeing that Shen Feizhi had quickly finished washing up and was already lying in bed, Gu Suizhou considerately refrained from teasing him any further. He washed up himself and turned in for the night.

However, it was a miserable night's sleep; he was jolted awake in the middle of the night by the stifling heat.

Gu Suizhou was particularly sensitive to heat. While Shen Feizhi could wear button-down shirts even in the height of summer, Gu Suizhou was different—he started wearing short sleeves and tank tops as early as spring or autumn.

Because of this, he woke up before Shen Feizhi even stirred, becoming the first to discover that the entirety of District Seven had suffered a power outage.

The temperature in District Seven remained high. As the air grew stifling, flies and insects squeezed through the cracks in the window, buzzing incessantly and biting.

It was utterly wretched; it wouldn't even let a person get a decent night's sleep.

Gu Suizhou felt a surge of irritability.

He cursed silently under his breath, then quietly moved a chair to sit by Shen Feizhi's bedside. Propping his head with one hand, he picked up a book provided by the hotel with the other and began to fan him gently.

Shen Feizhi was a light sleeper. To avoid waking him, Gu Suizhou kept his wrist moving at a constant rhythm. By the time the power finally returned, the sky outside was nearly dawn.

Only then did a drowsy Gu Suizhou set down the book, give his wrist a careless rub, and stumble back to his own bed to catch up on sleep.
