Gu Suizhou spent an entire day handling Shen Feizhi's legal dispute and compensation negotiations with Yang Rui.
Shen Feizhi lay in his hospital bed, completely detached from the outside world, having entrusted the entire matter to Gu Suizhou.
When they finally met again, Gu Suizhou delivered the news: Yang Rui had been sentenced to five years in prison.
He touched the surgical scar on his abdomen. "I thought he'd get at least ten years."
"Originally, it was twelve," Gu Suizhou said, peeling an apple and handing it to him. "But the Minor Protection Law reduced his sentence, and his parents spent a lot of money, so it ended up being just five years."
"What a pity," Shen Feizhi said, glancing disdainfully at the apple. "A fruit platter with nothing but apples? Is that reasonable?"
Gu Suizhou looked embarrassed. "The other fruits have gotten so expensive lately. I can't afford them."
Shen Feizhi: "……"
Knowing this kid was no simpleton, he had kept his guard up, not yet revealing that he'd made some money investing in stocks.
But as the hospital meals grew worse each day—the apples dwindling from two to just one—he could no longer hold back.
"Didn't that… uh, sheep give us compensation money?"
Gu Suizhou sighed. "The compensation has to go through the court's approval process. It probably won't arrive until next month."
That single sentence cast a pall over Shen Feizhi's mood, darkening his expression until he was discharged from the hospital on Sunday evening.

Gu Suizhou watched Shen Feizhi ahead of him, now bundled in a down jacket, and jogged to catch up, walking shoulder-to-shoulder with him. "Brother Shen, your wound is pretty deep. Shouldn't you stay a few more days?"
"Resting at home is the same."
Shen Feizhi couldn't stand the acrid stench of hospital disinfectant. Besides, he had work the next day. A mere five-centimeter-deep cut was nothing to him.
Gu Suizhou wanted to press the matter further, but Shen Feizhi preempted him by changing the subject. "Oh, by the way, I canceled the lease on that old place and rented a new apartment in District Six. Any objections?"
District Six was the closest to the main station leading to the Underground City, and the High School bus also stopped there. At most, it was an hour's commute—perfectly convenient for work and school.
Gu Suizhou smiled. "None at all. Whatever Brother Shen decides is fine with me."
