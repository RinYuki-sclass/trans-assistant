Gu Suizhou spent an entire day handling Shen Feizhi's legal dispute and compensation negotiations with Yang Rui.

Shen Feizhi lay in his hospital bed, completely detached from the outside world, having entrusted the entire matter to Gu Suizhou.

When they finally met again, Gu Suizhou delivered the news: Yang Rui had been sentenced to five years in prison.

He touched the surgical scar on his abdomen. "I thought he'd get at least ten years."

"Originally, it was twelve," Gu Suizhou said, peeling an apple and handing it to him. "But the Minor Protection Law reduced his sentence, and his parents spent a lot of money, so it ended up being just five years."

"What a pity," Shen Feizhi said, glancing disdainfully at the apple. "A fruit platter with nothing but apples? Is that reasonable?"

Gu Suizhou looked embarrassed. "The other fruits have gotten so expensive lately. I can't afford them."

Shen Feizhi: "……"

Knowing this kid was no simpleton, he had kept his guard up, not yet revealing that he'd made some money investing in stocks.

But as the hospital meals grew worse each day—the apples dwindling from two to just one—he could no longer hold back.

"Didn't that… uh, sheep give us compensation money?"

Gu Suizhou sighed. "The compensation has to go through the court's approval process. It probably won't arrive until next month."

That single sentence cast a pall over Shen Feizhi's mood, darkening his expression until he was discharged from the hospital on Sunday evening.

Gu Suizhou watched Shen Feizhi ahead of him, now bundled in a down jacket, and jogged to catch up, walking shoulder-to-shoulder with him. "Brother Shen, your wound is pretty deep. Shouldn't you stay a few more days?"

"Resting at home is the same."

Shen Feizhi couldn't stand the acrid stench of hospital disinfectant. Besides, he had work the next day. A mere five-centimeter-deep cut was nothing to him.

Gu Suizhou wanted to press the matter further, but Shen Feizhi preempted him by changing the subject. "Oh, by the way, I canceled the lease on that old place and rented a new apartment in District Six. Any objections?"

District Six was the closest to the main station leading to the Underground City, and the High School bus also stopped there. At most, it was an hour's commute—perfectly convenient for work and school.

Gu Suizhou smiled. "None at all. Whatever Brother Shen decides is fine with me."

What's the point of asking me now that you've already canceled the lease?

Shen Feizhi glanced sideways at him, then casually turned his gaze forward again.

"It's been two days. Don't you have anything you want to explain to me?"

Gu Suizhou looked genuinely puzzled. "Explain? About what?"

"Do I have to drag the answers out of you?" Shen Feizhi didn't mince words, holding up a hand and ticking off the points on his fingers. "Who is Yang Rui? Why did you fight? Where did you get the money for a lawyer? …And why didn't you wait for me after school? Why go straight back to the apartment?"

Gu Suizhou's eyes curved into crescents, his lips lifting in a mischievous grin that revealed two small fangs.

"Is that all, Brother Shen? If you want to know, of course I'll tell you everything!"

Shen Feizhi remained skeptical. "You'd better."

Gu Suizhou seemed oblivious to the sarcasm. "The Yang family used to be suppliers for the Gu family. After my parents died, Yang Rui got cocky and started picking fights with me."

In poverty, even kin turn away; in wealth, strangers gather.

The harsh realities of human nature must have sunk deep into Gu Suizhou's young mind.

Shen Feizhi offered no empty platitudes, his gaze urging him to continue.

Gu Suizhou continued, "You can probably guess why we fought, Brother Shen. He just didn't like me, so he started throwing punches."

Shen Feizhi pressed him, his tone ambiguous. "Just because he didn't like you?"

"Yup!" Gu Suizhou replied firmly. "I didn't do anything to provoke him."

"I just teased him a little bit. Rounded down, that's practically nothing, right?"

Shen Feizhi: "Alright, carry on."

"Don't worry about the lawyers, Brother Shen. I have an uncle who's an old friend of my dad's; he'll definitely help with something this small."

Shen Feizhi had no intention of delving into the truth of that statement.

Gu Suizhou wanted to stay by his side, most likely because of the original owner's identity—that is, his connection to the Shen family. It had nothing to do with Shen Feizhi himself. As long as it posed no threat to him personally, he couldn't be bothered to ask further.

"Then, the last one…"

"The last one," Gu Suizhou cut in, recklessly grabbing Shen Feizhi's sleeve, "Brother Shen, I just wanted you to come pick me up."

Shen Feizhi's eyebrow twitched violently.

He felt he should shut that little brat up, but when he tried to yank his sleeve free, it wouldn't budge.

So Gu Suizhou's words spilled out unchecked:

"I thought… you didn't want me anymore."

Shen Feizhi scoffed lightly. "You flatter yourself too much—"

Gu Suizhou: "This is the first time anyone's ever come to pick me up from school."

Shen Feizhi swallowed the rest of his sentence.

After a long pause, he finally blurted out four words:

"Abandonment is illegal."

During his two-day hospital stay, Shen Feizhi had already secured a new residence in District Six through the Starnet. For half the rent of his previous place, he'd leased a 180-square-meter penthouse apartment in a high-end residential complex—an absolute steal.

As he surveyed the apartment's interior, Shen Feizhi thrust the newly purchased disinfectant into Gu Suizhou's hands. "Let's get to work."

Gu Suizhou's lips twitched.

He knew Shen Feizhi was a neat freak, but he hadn't realized just how extreme it was.

Watching Shen Feizhi spray the disinfectant without the slightest hesitation, Gu Suizhou resigned himself to joining the cleanup crew.

Three hours later, the pair collapsed onto the sofa.

Shen Feizhi hadn't done a thorough cleaning in ages. After this top-to-bottom overhaul, he felt like every muscle and bone in his body had been revitalized.

He lazily crossed one leg over the other, swiping through his smartphone with one hand. "What time do you have school tomorrow?"

Gu Suizhou froze, only a quarter of his face visible from where he was buried in a throw pillow. "Eight o'clock."

"Oh," Shen Feizhi replied flatly.

Then… silence.

Gu Suizhou waited, but no follow-up came.

His heart pounded with anxiety until he finally couldn't hold back any longer. "Brother Shen, are you going to take me to school?"

Please don't! Tomorrow I have to…

"I was planning to," Shen Feizhi said.

Gu Suizhou anticipated the catch. "But?"

"But eight o'clock is too early. I can't get up that early."

"……" Gu Suizhou quietly exhaled in relief.

I almost gave you too much credit.

The next morning, Shen Feizhi was awakened by a relentless clanging and crashing coming from outside his room.

His eyelid twitched as his gaze swept to the hovering alarm clock beside him.

"Gu Suizhou."

The next moment, a murderous call drifted heavily from the room.

In the kitchen, Gu Suizhou's hand trembled mid-struggle, and he reflexively tossed the broken egg and pile of garbage into the trash bin.

"Brother Shen, breakfast is on the table! I'm heading to school now, see you Friday!"

With lightning reflexes, he grabbed his bag and bolted for the door.

But Shen Feizhi moved even faster.

Just as Gu Suizhou reached for the doorknob, Shen Feizhi's fingers clamped down, yanking him back by his backpack strap.

"Shut up." Shen Feizhi closed his eyes briefly, forcibly suppressing the urge to beat him to death, then opened them again. "I wasn't finished talking. Why are you running away?"

Gu Suizhou blurted out a lie without hesitation, "Aren't I going to be late?"

Shen Feizhi hurled the clock at him. "Two hours isn't enough to get to school?"

Gu Suizhou brazenly retorted, "I wanted to go early for morning study."

Shen Feizhi's smile was half amusement, half mockery. "So you're such a diligent student now?"

"Then remember to show me your report card at the end of the semester."

Gu Suizhou nearly lost his composure, his smile threatening to crack.

Fortunately, Shen Feizhi was merely speaking offhandedly. He tapped his fingers on the wooden table, deftly shifting the topic.

"I'll pick you up right on time Friday."

Gu Suizhou blinked in surprise.

Shen Feizhi released his grip on Gu Suizhou's shoulder strap and gave him a gentle shove toward the door. "Also, at school, you can throw punches, but you can't get hit. I don't care what the reason is—don't let me see that stupid stunt from yesterday again."

Gu Suizhou's knuckles involuntarily curled for a moment.

Snapping back to attention, he quickly curved his eyes into a wide smile. "Sure thing, Brother Shen! I'll be waiting for you~"

"Cut the cutesy stuff," Shen Feizhi said, unimpressed. Leaning against the doorframe, he pulled out his smartphone. "Got a QR code? Scan me."

Gu Suizhou raised an eyebrow slightly.

He swiftly pulled out his smartphone and opened the QR code scanner.

"Brother Shen, you're actually adding me as a contact~"

Shen Feizhi extinguished the screen that had just confirmed their friend request. "I'm not senile yet. I know what I'm doing. You don't need to repeat yourself."

The sight of that brat grinning always rubbed him the wrong way. It felt like he was about to spout some cockamamie nonsense any second…

Gu Suizhou: "You love me so much~"

Shen Feizhi: "…"

The door crashed shut in Gu Suizhou's face.

Standing on the balcony, Shen Feizhi watched Gu Suizhou descend the stairs and wait for his ride.

Only after the hovercar carrying him disappeared from sight did he leisurely withdraw his gaze, retrieve his smartphone, tap a few commands, and carelessly tuck it back into his pocket.

The fried egg on the table still emitted an acrid stench. With a flick of his slender, pale fingers, he flipped it over.

The underside was scorched black.

He clenched his jaw, strode to the kitchen, and kicked the trash can out from under the cabinet.

Inside lay over a dozen eggshell halves, scattered like fallen leaves.

He opened the refrigerator. The two dozen eggs he'd stocked up on with last night's takeout were now less than a tenth of their original number.

Shen Feizhi nearly bit through his cheek.

That little bastard.

What a goddamn spendthrift!

His good mood had been disintegrating since he'd laid eyes on Gu Suizhou.

Clutching his employee ID, Shen Feizhi descended into the Underground City, his mood as dark as the tunnels themselves.

The guard on duty today was the same one from yesterday. Noticing Shen Feizhi absentmindedly twirling his wooden stick and poking at the floor-cleaning robot, he couldn't help but remind him, "Hey, straighten up! The Institute's top brass is coming for inspection today!"

"Top brass?" Shen Feizhi instantly lost interest in Gu Suizhou, his full attention now focused on the Research Institute. "Professor-level figures, you mean?"

"Not exactly." The guard lowered his voice, checking his surroundings before whispering conspiratorially: "You probably don't know this, but the Institute's largest shareholder isn't actually the Federation—it's an individual."

Shen Feizhi was genuinely surprised. "The Federation trusts them with that?"

The most authoritative and capable research institute on the entire planet was under the control of a single person. He couldn't help but wonder what the Federation's high-ranking officials were thinking. How could they be so confident?

The guard sighed. "What choice do they have? This institute was practically founded because of that family. The two founders died suddenly before the Federation could gradually buy back the shares, so everything fell into the hands of their descendants."

"Can't they just keep reclaiming the shares?" Shen Feizhi asked, a vague suspicion already forming in his mind.

Sure enough, the answer confirmed his hunch.

"No way. Their legal heir is still a minor, and the Federation strictly enforces its Minor Protection Law. Snatching shares from a kid would be a PR disaster. So we're just waiting for the boy to turn eighteen."

"But today's inspector is his older brother." The guard lowered his voice even further. "You know how these big families are—full of tangled relationships. Everyone knows what's going on. The older brother's probably already scheming to seize his younger brother's shares."

Shen Feizhi had no interest in such gossip.

After extracting the useful information, he cut the guard's chatter short and herded the little cleaning robots into the warehouse.

He hadn't come here to be a janitor.

He needed to find a chance to contact the Research Institute's core personnel. Only then could he hope to find a way to reach the Main Star.

Surveillance cameras were everywhere in the Research Institute. As Shen Feizhi stirred up a ruckus with his cleaning stick, poking at the cleaning robots, his eyes darted around, scanning his surroundings.

Back when he was exploring and colonizing new worlds, Shen Feizhi had always favored warehouses. Sometimes, messy places yielded more valuable discoveries than libraries or research labs.

This belief proved true once again.

Soon, in a corner pinned down by a crate, he discovered a duty roster.

The janitorial robot, kicked halfway around, advanced into the corner and swept the duty roster out.

It was the duty roster for Core Group A from the previous year.

After only a quick glance, Shen Feizhi tucked the roster into the robot's storage compartment and prodded it toward the exit with his stick.

I'll sneak a proper look during lunch break.

As he reached the warehouse door, ready to leave, a familiar voice suddenly drifted from outside.

"…I understand the Director is in a difficult position, and I sincerely apologize, but I currently lack sufficient funds to invest in this research. I regret to say I cannot support it."

The more Shen Feizhi listened, the tighter his brow furrowed.

The voice was unmistakably familiar.

Where have I heard it before?

He cautiously cracked the door open a sliver, glimpsing a figure in a white lab coat.

Then the door slammed shut.

Shen Feizhi stood wooden-faced, slowly exhaling a long, heavy breath.

How is it Lin Sui?!

Meanwhile, Gu Suizhou had disembarked from the school bus halfway through the journey.

He put on a mask and waited by the roadside for a while. Finally, under the curious gazes of passersby, he boarded a private hovercar that reeked of money.

The driver, also wearing sunglasses and a mask, had his face completely covered.

Once the rear door closed, he respectfully asked, "Young Master, where shall we go?"

Gu Suizhou stared blankly out the window, as if he hadn't even heard the question. "Uncle Zhong, arrange for Yang Rui to have a… less comfortable time inside."

After all, Shen Feizhi had gotten into trouble for his sake.

Even a five-year sentence felt inadequate.

Xu Zhong asked no further questions. "Understood."

The car fell silent.

After a long silence, Gu Suizhou finally shifted his gaze from the window.

"Take me to the Underground City."
