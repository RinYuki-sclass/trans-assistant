Song Zhe rubbed his nose, too embarrassed to speak.
To be honest, ever since he'd known Gu Suizhou, the guy had never had a single stroke of good luck.
Every time he crossed the street, the light turned red. Every time he copied homework, the teacher caught him. Even at the middle school graduation event, with a 99.9% prize-winning rate, he'd managed to be the sole member of the 0.1%.
In a twisted way, it was a kind of legendary luck.
So, the fact that they'd run into Yang Rui at the school gate at midnight didn't even seem that surprising to Song Zhe.
He tried to be diplomatic. "Brother Zhou, it's not that you have bad luck. You're just saving up good karma for your next life."
Gu Suizhou's lips twitched into a sneer.
What's the damn point of saving karma for the next life if I can't use it in this one?
He closed his eyes. "…Just go home."
Song Zhe refused. "Brother, I have to protect you!"
"I'd be grateful if you just stayed out of my way," Gu Suizhou said. "If you don't leave now, your dad's going to come looking for you."
At the mention of his father, Song Zhe instinctively shivered. "Then… then take care of yourself, Brother! Call me if anything happens!"
Gu Suizhou waved his hand dismissively, signaling for him to scram.

After the Gu family's downfall, nearly all the powerful and wealthy families had cut ties, terrified of getting dragged into the mess.
Only Song Zhe, simple-minded as he was, secretly hung out with him every day behind his father's back.
Yang Rui had already sauntered over. Watching Song Zhe's retreating figure, he sneered, "Squatting here at midnight. How pitiful, little stray dog."
Gu Suizhou glared at the cigarette in Yang Rui's hand with disgust. "Stay away from me. You reek."
"Fuck you! Can't you say anything decent, you little bitch? You're in this state, and you still act so high and mighty?" Yang Rui, thinking it was a personal insult, spat several times near Gu Suizhou's hand in fury.
Gu Suizhou easily dodged it and scoffed. Just as he was about to stand up and fight back, he caught sight of a police officer at the intersection out of the corner of his eye.
