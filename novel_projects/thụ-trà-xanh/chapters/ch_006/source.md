In the interstellar future, where high technology advanced at breakneck speed, menial jobs like cleaning had long been fully automated, handled by machines and lifelike androids.

But no matter how sophisticated artificial intelligence became, it lacked human agency. To prevent machines from malfunctioning and damaging important assets, many companies and research institutes hired human "Cleaning Supervisors" to ensure the AIs operated correctly.

"Mr. Shen Zhi, congratulations on successfully joining W.N. We look forward to working with you."

"Thank you." Shen Feizhi accepted his Cleaning Supervisor ID badge from the HR Department and nodded politely. "So, I can start work next Monday?"

"That's right, Mr. Shen. The cleaning shift at our institute is from 9 AM to 8 PM, with weekends off. You'll need to clock in and out on time. There's a bonus for perfect attendance each month… Just come in on time next Monday."

"Understood."

Shen Feizhi nodded again. "See you next week."

On his way here, he had hacked into Blue Star's Personnel Data Collection Network System and fabricated a fake identity for himself: "Shen Zhi," an orphan who had barely attended a few days of school.

Research institutes with strict confidentiality protocols loved hiring menial workers with such simple backgrounds. Since they were illiterate and couldn't read, there was no risk of them understanding any sensitive documents they might see.

The HR Department would undoubtedly conduct a more thorough background check, but Shen Feizhi wasn't worried. The network technology training he'd received was far more advanced than anything on Blue Star.

Swinging his ID badge, he sauntered away under the guard's disgusted gaze, as if he'd just witnessed someone eat something foul.

Having shed his unemployed status and secured a month's salary in advance, he was in an excellent mood.

The Underground City was too far from the First District. After a moment's thought, Shen Feizhi decided to pay his rent and find a place to live nearby.

Of course, a month's base salary wouldn't cover all that.

His gaze flickered between the Underground City's casinos and internet cafes. After some hesitation, he finally stepped into an internet cafe.

Gambling was a quick way to make money, but he was a law-abiding citizen. He'd just play the stock market for some quick cash.

Having spent years constantly learning and absorbing information, Shen Feizhi had long since mastered this world's stock market system. Luckily, it had a T+0 trading system, allowing for same-day settlement—short, thrilling, and high-risk.

He didn't plan to play the market long-term. Drawing too much attention from the financial world would be detrimental to his current situation, so the shorter the time, the better.

Shen Feizhi rented a private booth and took out a large loan on his newly opened credit card. His fingers flew across the keyboard.

Soon, the screen filled with a chaotic dance of red and green curves.

Time flies when you're focused.

By the time Shen Feizhi rubbed his sore eyes and cleared his positions, it was already 23:21. The sky outside was completely dark.

He admired the hundred-thousand-plus balance on his card, then rose with a deadpan expression.

He paced around the room.

I'm. Rich!

Even after paying off the loan and rent, he'd still have more than half left. He carefully tucked away his wallet and credit card, then casually opened the booth door.

He'd barely taken a step before nearly colliding with a student carrying a backpack.

"Sorry! I'm so sorry, sir!" the student stammered, bowing deeply in apology.

Since there'd been no actual impact, Shen Feizhi magnanimously shook his head. "It's fine."

As he watched the student hurry away, still muttering apologies, he noticed the internet cafe had become bustling.

When he'd arrived that morning, it had been deserted. Now, it was packed with students in school uniforms, all carrying backpacks.

…That's not right.

He knew many students loved gaming, but this was the Underground City, a place dominated by adults. Why were there so many students here?

Shen Feizhi tapped on the front desk. "An instant coffee, hot."

The handsome blond young man at the counter had been sneaking glances at him all along. Now that Shen Feizhi was speaking to him, he flashed a seductive smile. "Of course! Just a moment."

"No rush." Shen Feizhi leaned casually against the counter. While the young man prepared the coffee, he asked, "Are there always this many students here?"

Blondie shook his head. "Only on days like today."

Shen Feizhi raised an eyebrow. "Days like today? What's so special about today?"

"It's a special day for students, of course." Blondie smiled, placing the coffee on the counter. "It's Friday. The kids from the vocational boarding school next door finally made it to the weekend. Naturally, they're out to have some fun."

Shen Feizhi's hand trembled as he reached for the coffee, knocking it against the card reader.

"Whoa, careful!" Blondie winced sympathetically as Shen Feizhi's wrist bone turned red. "Does it hurt? Do you need me to get you some medicine—"

"It's Friday?" Shen Feizhi ignored him, pulling out his smart device in disbelief. The word "FRIDAY" blazed on the screen, glaringly conspicuous.

…No wonder I felt like I'd forgotten something all day.

That little brat, Gu Suizhou, was out of school!

This was his first time looking after a kid, and he'd completely forgotten he had a dead weight to drag around. Had the boy gone back to the rental apartment by himself when he didn't show up?

He hadn't even thought to ask for his phone number.

For the first time, Shen Feizhi felt a pang of guilt. He paid for the coffee with forced composure and bolted toward the station.

"Hey! Sir, you forgot your coffee!"

Shen Feizhi had no time to worry about the coffee.

Pressed for time, he couldn't even afford to save money by buying a bus ticket. Instead, he booked the next available shuttle, arriving back in the First District in just over an hour.

As he ran, he frantically tried to concoct an excuse for when he finally saw Gu Suizhou.

But as he reached an intersection, he abruptly stopped, his expression a mixture of awkwardness and blank confusion.

Gu Suizhou…

Where the hell does he even go to school?

Meanwhile, outside High Tier High School.

Gu Suizhou watched the time creep toward midnight, his face growing darker by the second.

That bastard Shen Feizhi… Did he really just ditch me?

He'd made such a big deal about it before. Now, after only five days apart, the man had completely abandoned him? Was his heart made of diamond?!

"Brother… Brother Zhou, maybe we should just head home?"

"Home my ass." Gu Suizhou glared at his childhood friend, who had trailed him like a shadow since they were kids. "You want me to go back and let Lin Sui beat me to death?"

Song Zhe flinched, muttering under his breath, "It's not like you can't beat him…"

"I can beat him." Gu Suizhou pressed his throbbing temples. "But now isn't the time."

Until he secured full control of the assets that rightfully belonged to him, he had to flawlessly perform the role of a "helpless waste" for Lin Sui.

Before Gu Lang passed away, he had left all his personal assets to Gu Suizhou. Unfortunately, since Gu Suizhou was still a minor, the court had granted guardianship of the entire estate to his only legal relative—Lin Sui.

If Gu Suizhou played the part of a useless fool, Lin Sui wouldn't see him as a threat. Once he came of age, Lin Sui would likely hand over the inheritance as if bestowing charity. But if Gu Suizhou proved himself more capable, things would get complicated.

With the Shen family's backing, Lin Sui had already made a name for himself. It wouldn't be difficult for him to tamper with the inheritance.

Gu Suizhou didn't actually care about the money Gu Lang had left him. What mattered was…

He cut the thought short and, without turning his head, kicked Song Zhe. "Don't smoke in front of me."

"Huh? I wasn't smoking," Song Zhe protested, shoving the unlit cigarette back into his pocket, looking utterly innocent.

Gu Suizhou opened his eyes, his patience wearing thin. "Then where's that smoke smell coming from?"
