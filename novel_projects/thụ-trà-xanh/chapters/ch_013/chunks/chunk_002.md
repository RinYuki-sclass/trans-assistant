"Fine," Gu Suizhou said. "So, you'll be busy these next few days?"
"Mm-hmm." Shen Feizhi stood up and hailed a nearby shared hovercar. "Don't bother me too much in the meantime."
"Relax, I won't," Gu Suizhou replied with a mirthless smile, his eyes curving into crescents. "I'm heading out tomorrow."
"To the Underground City?" Shen Feizhi thought of the blond-haired kid. "Looking for Leapton?"
"You remember him clearly enough," Gu Suizhou scoffed. "But you'll be disappointed—I'm not going to see him. You're so all-knowing and omniscient—how could you possibly not know what I'm doing tomorrow?"
Shen Feizhi's hand froze mid-motion as he reached for another cigarette.
He hadn't thought of it earlier, but now that the brat mentioned it, a faint memory surfaced.
Tomorrow, it seemed, marked the tenth anniversary of Lin Zizhuo and Gu Lang's suicides.
He recalled a passage from the original documentation describing the circumstances of Lin Sui's parents—no, Gu Suizhou's parents' suicides.
Lin Sui later told the original owner about it himself, after their relationship had deepened.

Lin Sui boarded at school and rarely needed to return home. To make it easier for Gu Suizhou to commute, Lin Zizhuo and Gu Lang bought a new house near the school.
The new house was very close by. One day, Lin Sui finished school early and picked up his younger brother on the way home. They walked hand-in-hand beneath streetlights that glowed as bright as daylight. Lin Sui even bought Gu Suizhou a string of candied hawthorns, intending to share them with their parents when they got home.
But they never got to eat the candied hawthorns.
As soon as they opened the front door, they saw two bodies hanging from the rafters.
One was their father, the other their mother.
Just then, it started to rain, and a flash of lightning illuminated Lin Zizhuo and Gu Lang's deathly pale faces.
They were very quiet, just looking a little sad.
Shen Feizhi closed his eyes briefly, his thumb gently rubbing the lighter's button as he lit a cigarette. "I'm sorry," he said.
Before entering this world, he had needed to grasp these basic facts to find its critical junctures. He hadn't intentionally pried into anyone's private affairs.
