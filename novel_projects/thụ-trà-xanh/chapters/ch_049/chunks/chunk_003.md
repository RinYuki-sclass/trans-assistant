The spot he pointed to was occupied by the very subject of their conversation.
Gu Suizhou walked in shoulder-to-shoulder with a young man, and they took seats opposite each other in a corner.
Shen Feizhi recognized the guy—it was Number 9.
Narrowing his eyes, he scrutinized the pair without a shred of guilt.
The man was acting the perfect gentleman, cutting a steak for Gu Suizhou, thoughtfully wiping his utensils, and pouring his wine.
Gu Suizhou, meanwhile, sat there like a pampered lord, propping up his head lazily without moving a muscle. He accepted the other’s service as if it were his birthright, occasionally offering a gentle smile.
C2 watched him with great interest. "How does it feel? Like a cabbage you raised is being rooted up by a pig?"

"Hardly," Shen Feizhi said nonchalantly. "Though I do think that guy is beneath Gu Suizhou."
"Fair point, and accurate." C2 nodded in agreement. "Then why are you playing matchmaker?"
"Whether he’s good enough isn't for me to decide. The parties involved have to try it out to know. It’s better than having no one to help him when he differentiates—he'd suffocate otherwise." Shen Feizhi took a sip of his cocoa. "The cocoa here is terrible."
"Huh?" C2 looked at his cup. "You finished the whole thing before realizing that?"
“The more I drink it, the worse it tastes,” Shen Feizhi said. “Let’s go. I don’t have a habit of voyeurism.”
“Ah…” Deprived of his gossip, C2’s face fell in disappointment. “Come on, stay and eat a few more bites with me!”
Shen Feizhi ruthlessly saw through his excuse. “You’ve eaten a whole table’s worth. You’re still not full?”
C2 tugged at his waistband. “Fine. If it were Gu Suizhou, would you be this heartless about making him leave too?”
Shen Feizhi thought for a moment, a trace of a smile appearing at the corners of his mouth. “No. I’d order another table’s worth and force him to eat it all. If he couldn’t finish, he wouldn’t be going back.”
Thanks, but that was the kind of special treatment he could do without.
The two of them left openly through the front door, not caring whether they were spotted or not.
