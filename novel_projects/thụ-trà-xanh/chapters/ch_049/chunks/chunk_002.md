“Want to hear my advice?” C2 asked.

C2 said, “Lin Zizhuo and Gu Lang likely burned down the Research Institute because of that person. Now that the Institute is developing normally again, the data that person is interested in might reappear; they’re bound to get restless sooner or later. If you ask me, you should just go about your own business for now and wait for the fish to bite the hook.”
C2’s suggestion wasn’t without merit; Shen Feizhi had thought as much himself.
C2 noticed his hesitation. “You think something’s off?”
“No, everything you said is correct.” Shen Feizhi set down his knife and fork, using a sip of hot cocoa to mask the fleeting look of daze on his face.
It was just that he didn’t know what his “own business” was.
Shen Feizhi was a man of very clear objectives, someone who could sacrifice everything and use any means necessary to reach a “key node.” But once a goal was achieved, he had never considered what came after.
He was suddenly struck by a sense of confusion: aside from these things, what else could he do? What was the purpose of his existence?
