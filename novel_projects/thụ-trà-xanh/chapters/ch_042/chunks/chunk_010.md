The next second, in the night sky directly above the newly opened chocolate factory, a magnificent, iridescent cloud suddenly burst into existence.
Gu Suizhou’s pupils constricted.
He recognized this sight. Gu Lang had told him once that it took at least a military-grade shuttle exploding in space to create such beauty—a sight almost impossible to encounter.
The cost was too high, the operator’s chance of survival one in ten; no one ever tried it. At most, people just watched it in movies and left it at that.
But now, he was seeing it.
And it had appeared because of him.
Because Shen Feizhi had spoken to him with a rare hint of a smile in his voice.
“Gu Suizhou, you’re seventeen. Happy birthday.”
The soundproofing in this building was quite good, but with the windows open, the voices of others managed to catch the wind.
Gu Suizhou’s ears were filled with a constant ringing; it took a long while before a few stray voices finally squeezed through.

“Ahhhh! Quick, take a picture! Take a picture of me!”
“My god, who set that off? We’re actually seeing something this rare?!”
“Should we make a wish? Whoever sees this is lucky for life!”
…Lucky for life?
The corners of Gu Suizhou’s mouth quirked.
This was clearly a gift that man had given him, a gift that could make a person lucky for life—so why was he so sad instead?
So sad that every breath he took sent a twitch of pain through his heart.
He thought blankly for a long time before the realization finally hit him.
It was because Shen Feizhi didn’t want him anymore.
