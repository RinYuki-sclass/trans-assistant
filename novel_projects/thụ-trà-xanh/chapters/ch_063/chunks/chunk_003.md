“How could you call this courting death?” Ritter asked, seemingly reverting back to that gentle professor who doted on his juniors. “Come down, Gu Suizhou. I’ll take you back to where we belong!”
Gu Suizhou let out a soft sneer.
Ever since he was young, Ritter had been pretentious. He was a genius—a conceited, contemptuous genius.
He had established a strict social circle for himself, surrounding himself only with the elite of the elite, like Lin Zizhuo and Gu Lang. Everyone else, in his eyes, was mere trash that lowered his quality of life.
He had always felt that he didn’t belong here. Someone like him clearly belonged in a more advanced world; there were too many idiots in this world, to the point where even breathing felt like an ordeal.
As for Gu Suizhou, a junior with immense talent, Ritter felt a sense of pity. He thought it was a shame that such a perfect physical vessel was trapped by a depraved and decadent soul.

He admired Gu Suizhou’s talent.
But he despised Gu Suizhou’s character.
Ritter viewed his love for Lin Zizhuo and Gu Lang, as well as his insistence on certain moral standards, as utterly superfluous.
To be honest, Ritter actually preferred Shen Feizhi, but for his own sake, Shen Feizhi had to die.
"It’s a pity," he said regretfully. "Are you really not coming with me?"
"Suit yourself," Gu Suizhou rejected him without a shred of mercy.
"Very well." Ritter shrugged. "Then goodbye, child."
Gu Suizhou didn't say a word. As Ritter turned to board the shuttle, Gu Suizhou put on a pair of full-coverage protective goggles and pulled an ion launcher from his waist.
Shen Feizhi’s heart skipped a beat at the sight. "Are you a hundred percent sure?"
What he really wanted to say was: This is the "arrangement" you mentioned? How is anyone supposed to feel at ease?
Gu Suizhou hummed in affirmation. "Don't worry, Big Brother. Trust me."
"I’d love to trust you," Shen Feizhi said, his expression complicated. "But you don't exactly look reliable right now."
Gu Suizhou clicked his tongue.
