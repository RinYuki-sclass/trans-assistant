Chapter 24

Shen Feizhi had originally planned to depart for Pami-10 on Sunday, the sixth planet of the Federation's star system, the region with the highest concentration of medicinal herb cultivation.

One of W.N.'s most crucial ingredients, the Bai Shi Herb, grew abundantly there.

Blue Star's most expensive life-saving medicine utilized this herb, its efficacy and rarity self-evident.

However, its toxicity was equally potent; improper use could prove fatal.

This time, Shen Feizhi intended to locate W.N.'s supplier from a decade ago and review the Research Institute's purchase records from that time.

If the quantities didn't match the amount of medicine W.N. had produced, it would prove someone had tampered with the supply chain.

His plan was meticulously crafted, but reality rarely follows the script. Sunday came and went, and he still hadn't left.

The reason? Gu Suizhou, that rascal, had his final exams the following week.

But that wasn't the worst of it.

Even worse, Jiang Che, as the visiting student's supervisor for the final month, insisted Shen Feizhi take the exams as well.

Jiang Che's exact words were: "Since you've studied the material, you might as well test yourself. I've seen your records—you never finished high school, right? If you pass these exams, regardless of your score, I can help you apply for a diploma through the Registrar's Office."

Jiang Che was a good teacher, and his intentions were sincere. Shen Feizhi wasn't ungrateful; though he didn't particularly want to take the exams, he accepted Jiang Che's offer.

He was the type of person who either did nothing at all or went all in, striving for perfection. The idea of "just taking the exams casually" simply didn't exist for him.

Even if Jiang Che said the grades didn't matter, Shen Feizhi couldn't bear the thought of his academic record being filled with failing marks.

So, for nearly a week, he forced Gu Suizhou to hole up at home with him, cramming for the exams.

Math and science posed little challenge for either of them. Their real struggle lay with history and geography—together, they couldn't even scrape up double-digit scores.

Shen Feizhi's difficulty stemmed from his unfamiliarity with this world's setting, while Gu Suizhou simply refused to study. The moment he opened a textbook, he'd fall asleep—a feat more difficult than killing him.

Only those who endured that week of agony truly understood its torment.

Judging by the dark circles under their eyes on exam day, Shen Feizhi had likely studied even harder than Gu Suizhou.

Shen Feizhi had never felt such a headache in his life.

He already had some knowledge of politics and law, so catching up in those subjects wasn't too bad. Language arts wasn't particularly challenging either. But trying to cram twelve years of history into seven days left him feeling like he wanted to die.

The only consolation was that when the exam papers were handed out, he knew the answers to most of the questions.

At High School, students rotated exam rooms for each subject, with seating arranged according to grades. As an auditor taking the exam for the first time, he was naturally assigned to the last exam room.

Coincidentally, Gu Suizhou, who consistently ranked dead last in the history department, sat right next to him.

Watching Shen Feizhi furiously scribbling away beside him, Gu Suizhou was inwardly astonished.

"Gu Suizhou!"
