He suddenly felt this scene was very familiar.
In his daze, a memory from a long time ago flickered back to him.
“It hurts so much, Big Brother.”
Curled into a ball and covered in wounds, the little boy clutched the teenager’s hand with a death grip. The older boy was too weak to squeeze back; he could only curl his fingers around the child’s hand, murmuring gently, “Don’t be afraid.”
The boy’s eyes were hollow and vacant—the unmistakable look of the blind.
He tried desperately to burrow into the older boy’s embrace. “I’m hurting so much, ugh… I don’t want to live anymore!”
To undergo Differentiation in this kind of place and at this kind of time was, without a doubt, a case of adding frost to snow.
“Don’t talk nonsense,” the teenager scolded in a very soft voice. “Don’t be afraid. It’ll be over soon.”
If the boy could see, he would have been horrified.
