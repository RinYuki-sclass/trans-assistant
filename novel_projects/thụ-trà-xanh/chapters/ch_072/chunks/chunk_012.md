Gu Suizhou: "?"
"Why?!" he blurted out, losing his voice.
"You tell me," Shen Feizhi sneered. "What kind of trouble were you stirring up behind my back recently? You think it’s all going to be brushed aside that easily?"
"Easily?" Gu Suizhou was incredulous. "You weren't satisfied?"
Shen Feizhi remained perfectly calm. "One thing has nothing to do with the other."
Now that his desires had been vented, the influence the rut had over him was minimal. Once he put his clothes back on, he returned to being that stoic, unreadable "Old Immortal."
Seeing him stand up, Gu Suizhou quickly rolled over and pinned him back down: "Don't go!"
"I'm thirsty." Shen Feizhi hadn’t even finished buttoning his shirt; it was draped loosely over him, his chest pressing against the other man’s burning skin without any barrier.
He knit his brows. "You're covered in filth. Go take a shower."
“What are you getting all worked up about? It belongs to you,” Gu Suizhou said, his hands starting to wander again. He buried his head in the crook of the other man's neck, his voice muffled.
“I owed an elder a favor from before. To pay it back, I have to give him a hand in business.”
“One of his nephews has a company facing a serious financial crisis. I’ve been busy lately with due diligence and figuring out how to maximize the profit from the situation. Aside from that, I’m really not hiding anything else from you.”
Having said that, fearing Shen Feizhi wouldn't believe him, he had a mechanical claw bring over his smartphone and unlocked it right in front of him. “If you don’t believe me, check it. Check whatever you want! The password is your birthday.”
Shen Feizhi had no intention of checking. He stopped the hand that was trying to reach further inside his clothes and asked, puzzled, “If it’s just that, why hide it from me?”
Gu Suizhou stammered, “Well, that company… how should I put it…”
As he was weighing his words, the doorbell suddenly rang downstairs.
Shen Feizhi pushed him aside and stood up. “The delivery must be here.”
In the past three days, they hadn't had any time to cook at all. They had mostly been living on instant food or takeout—eating when they got hungry from the exertion, then continuing their indulgence once they finished. It was, quite literally, a total lapse into decadence.
Gu Suizhou, his train of thought interrupted, lost the words he had painstakingly organized. He gave a dry “Oh.” “Then I…”
"Go take a shower first." Shen Feizhi got out of bed, his germaphobia returning as he demanded fastidiously, "Wash yourself thoroughly."
