“Died on Saturday,
Buried on Sunday.”
The voice, humming the nursery rhyme a mother would sing to a child at bedtime, without rise or fall, sounded like a countdown to death. The man nervously rolled the ring in his hand as he listened to the song. It was a magic-resistance stone ring, one his usual gang member had boasted about all night while drinking, claiming he'd gone through great lengths to acquire it.

When the heads of the men waiting outside exploded like fountains during the unknown mage's attack, the man, amidst the chaos, snatched the ring from the corpse of one of the victims and fled. He desperately prayed for the ring to save him from the demon, huddling his body. But God did not answer the prayers of a man who had lived his entire life as a villain.

“And that was the end of Solomon Grundy.”
As the voice finished the song, the ring in his hand shattered into pieces. The man, whose hand was instantly covered in blood from the ring fragments, involuntarily shrieked in pain, then belatedly came to his senses and looked forward. Turning his gaze, he met the eyes of a man with a pale face, not a single drop of blood on him, standing in a pool of blood. As their eyes met, the man smiled, his eyes curving. His appearance was so grotesque that an unbearable terror rose within him.

The man slowly approached the box, paying no mind to the villain's frantic attempts to retreat further into the cramped space, oozing various bodily fluids as he tried to escape. He merely spoke.

“Doesn't it seem like a good life? To live and die according to the natural order.”

“Ugh, ah, sa-save... Save me...”

At the villain's plea, the man expressed regret with a smiling face.

“That won't do. I can't make any more mistakes.”

The villain couldn't close his mouth, tears streaming down his face. The man pushed open the box door, bent down, and lightly tapped the villain's cheek with his gloved hand.

“You must regret that you've done nothing wrong to me. But don't feel too wronged. You haven't exactly lived a life that would make your death unfair, have you?”

Just as he had when slaughtering his comrades moments ago, the man slowly extended his hand, and the villain stammered his plea.

“I have a wife! A son! Please, please...!”

At the man's desperate plea, the other man blinked once, stared at him for a moment, then smiled.

“Oh? I'll keep that in mind for next time.”
