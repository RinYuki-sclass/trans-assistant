Ascending the unobstructed stairs and entering the wide-open house on the fifth floor, I found Liam Carter, caught by my subordinates and kneeling with a bewildered expression.

There was no need to go this far, but perhaps because he was the one who had turned the mansion upside down since morning, my subordinates, unaware of the situation, seemed to think he was some kind of criminal.

Seeing his gleaming face, blinking at me, the anger I had suppressed on the way flared up again. I slowly drew my sword and placed it against his neck. I pressed the blade close enough to be a threat, though not to cut him, and asked him.

“Liam Carter. Explain yourself.”

“Well… In the morning… I was just too surprised…”

“I shouldn’t have humored a drunkard.”

At his pathetic answer, I clicked my tongue in exasperation. He, in turn, flared up and retorted.

“I didn’t confess because of the alcohol!”

“Oh? So, should I take that to mean you’re inherently a faithless person?”

“Ah, no, that’s not it…”

It was absurd how deflated he became with every word I uttered. If this was how it was going to be, why on earth did he cause such a commotion since morning?

I saw my subordinates’ eyes gleam as they realized our conversation wasn't about apprehending a criminal, but a lover’s quarrel with their superior. I gave a light glare to the men watching Liam and me with keen interest, then lowered my gaze back to Liam, only to widen my eyes in surprise.

Liam was, by then, sniffling, his eyes brimming with tears, his lips pressed tightly together. In the midst of it, he glared up at me, determined not to let a single tear fall, which was absurd and, well, a little cute.

In my opinion, Liam Carter had an exceptional talent for escaping difficult situations. Otherwise, why would he consistently do things that made me soften towards him?

I stared blankly at Liam’s tear-streaked face, then, feeling self-reproach, I sighed softly and sheathed my sword. Making a boy much younger than myself cry, and then finding it cute—what a commendable thing to do, Caden Amir.

As I sheathed my sword, Liam, perhaps surprised, blinked repeatedly, and tears streamed down his cheeks with the movement of his eyelashes. Just like yesterday, when he cried like that, I wanted to grant him anything, which was a huge problem.

After putting my sword back in its scabbard, I knelt on one knee to meet his gaze. Just as I had last night, I wiped the tears from his cheeks and comforted him in the softest voice I could manage.

“Don’t cry. When you cry, I want to grant you anything.”

But Liam Carter, after hearing my words, stared blankly at me, his mouth agape, and then burst into loud sobs instead. I had meant to make him stop crying, but I had only made him cry harder, which was perplexing. Perhaps it was because of our twelve-year age gap, but it was difficult to follow his psychology.

In the end, after trying to comfort him a little longer, I realized we couldn't stay like this. So, I hoisted the boy, who showed no sign of stopping his sniffling, onto my shoulder and left the house.
