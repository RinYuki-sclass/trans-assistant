If “I don’t want to go back” referred to time?

I was looking out at the scenery when the thought suddenly struck me, and I let out a hollow laugh. It made no sense.

Amazing things often happened in the world, but there was still such a thing as common sense. This was no exception, even in the mysteries of magic. People often attributed countless incomprehensible phenomena to magic, but this was a way of thinking based on common prejudice and misunderstanding. No matter how many astonishing feats a mage might perform, they ultimately only transformed existing things. Creation and time, realms that fundamentally altered the world, were not the purview of mages, but rather the domain of what was commonly referred to as the gods—the realm of mythical beings now only told to children at bedtime.

Though not a mage myself, I knew enough about the study of magic to understand how preposterous my recent thought was. Yet, to simply dismiss time as an absurd option felt somewhat unsettling. Especially when I reconsidered Liam’s past actions. His constant composure, even when things went awry. His uncanny ability to know where and when the organization we were chasing would move, before anyone else. Watching him smoothly resolve matters, as if he had already seen the future, I initially assumed he had a competent informant. Or perhaps he used magic to spy on the organizations. When I asked which it was, Liam would only roll his eyes and say he couldn’t answer, so I had only planned to interrogate him later if our work together ever went awry. But if his “going back” referred to time…

My thoughts spiraled uncontrollably, and I frowned. But it’s impossible.

As I tried to recall several reasons why the hypothesis that had just occurred to me was impossible, I remembered something Baron Trevor, the former Chief Royal Investigator who had trained me when I first took on the role of Royal Investigator, had said.

“Investigator Caden, do you know what the most dangerous trap investigators easily fall into when conducting an investigation is?”

“Being swayed by emotions?”

“That is also something to be careful about. But what you must be most careful of is prejudice stemming from common sense.”

The Baron’s words made sense, but it was such a fundamental, obvious answer that I just stared at him. He smiled and gave me an example.

“In the past, the perfume extracted from Blam clams often caused significant disruptions to investigations. Do you know why?”

“Because at certain temperatures, Blam clam perfume was difficult to distinguish from the poison Hess. Before the invention of a reagent that could definitively differentiate Blam and Hess, confusing the two to obstruct investigations was a popular method, as I recall.”

“Yes, that’s right. It happened so often that before the reagent appeared, if Blam clam perfume was found at a crime scene, investigators would first focus on whether Hess had been used in the case. However, sometimes this was used in reverse.”
