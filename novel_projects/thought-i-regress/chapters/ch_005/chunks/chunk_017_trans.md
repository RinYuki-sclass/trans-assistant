Caden, who had stopped reviewing documents, quietly rubbed his forehead. According to his current flimsy hypothesis, Liam was unable to 'go back' because of the influence Caden had on him. Something felt almost within his grasp, yet just out of reach.

Caden rested his chin in his hand, pretending not to notice his subordinate's displeased gaze, narrowed at his superior who had once again stopped working. If he exerted an even greater influence on Liam, would Liam become even more entangled by his side?

As he pondered, a knight carefully knocked on his office door. When the knight asked what to do, saying Liam Carter wished to see the Duke, Caden, thinking it was perfect timing, permitted Liam to be brought in.

However, he instructed them to bring Liam while keeping a close watch to ensure he couldn't escape.

Watching the knight turn and leave, Caden lightly ran a hand through his hair and spoke to his aide.

“Jackson, bring the marriage documents. We have spares at the mansion, don't we?”

“Pardon? Well, yes, we do, but why the marriage documents...?”

“Do marriage documents have any other purpose?”

“Your Grace!”

At his superior's words, Jackson, with a horrified expression, worried about the future of the young mage caught by the fox-like superior before him.

“Marriage just because he ran away once? Isn't that too harsh? He's still young.”

“Jackson, I won't say it twice.”

At his superior's firm command, the aide, with a regretful expression, retrieved a marriage document from a drawer containing all the paperwork used in the royal palace.

A look of regret was etched on the aide's face as he handed it to Caden. Caden snatched the document from his impertinent subordinate's hand and slowly began to fill in the blanks.

Few things allow one person to exert such great influence over another's life as marriage. They even say, in vulgar terms, that marriage is a binding of one's life. Therefore, there would be few methods as effective as this to greatly influence Liam.

But at the same time, throughout filling out the document, a cry echoed in a corner of Caden's mind: *Is this truly the right thing to do?* Was it right to decide on something as important as marriage in such a way?

To keep a mage he'd been dating for only a day? Moreover, the hypothesis that he wouldn't go back if Caden influenced him wasn't even certain. What if marriage turned out to be unexpectedly ineffective?

Caden let out a groan and stopped his hand. His long-held rationality tried to dissuade him, calling his current actions madness, but as he considered stopping, the image of the boy-like man who had shed tears at their first meeting flickered in his mind.

*Right, there was nothing bad about marrying Liam.* He had no intention of marrying anyway and wasn't even engaged, so giving his side to Liam would only slightly alter his life plans.

Caden began to rationalize his actions, deliberately downplaying the biggest problem. And besides, even if he proposed, couldn't Liam just refuse if he didn't want to? Of course, if he refused, Caden would probably be a little annoyed.
