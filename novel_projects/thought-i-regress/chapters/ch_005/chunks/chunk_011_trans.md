In the carriage, Caden, annoyed by the persistent discomfort below, cracked his knuckles one after another. He folded his arms, leaning back awkwardly against the seat, and quietly closed his eyes. Though he was heading to Liam Carter's house in a fit of pique, he knew it was time to calm down.

Liam Carter had a tendency to be overly sensitive to Caden's moods. If Caden showed any sign of displeasure, there was no telling what Liam Carter might do to appease him, so it was important to appear calm for now.

*If he's going to be so sensitive, why do things that upset me in the first place?* Caden thought, but brushed it aside. In any case, he needed to calm himself down a little.

Caden slowly took a deep breath and opened his eyes. As his mind settled, facts he hadn't been able to recall that morning, when his anger had been at its peak, slowly resurfaced.

Liam Carter had said, "I don't want to go back," last night. For someone who didn't want to go back, he had certainly left for his own home this morning, but Caden still needed to consider what those words meant. People often let slip important things when they were drunk.

To go back is usually used in the context of returning to a certain place or state...

It didn't make sense. If "going back" referred to a place, it didn't quite fit. Liam had said he would go back tomorrow, but hadn't he already returned home on his own this morning?

Ah, perhaps he was under a spell and forced to return? But there aren't many ways to compel a mage of Liam Carter's caliber.

Caden decided to consider another hypothesis, given the lack of clarity in his current deduction. Could it have meant a state? If it referred to a state, it might mean the cold, detached personality described in the reports. Was it that he didn't want to return to that state?

But this, too, was strange. While there were people whose personalities changed after waking up, Liam Carter hadn't shown any such signs over the past few months.

Furthermore, far from not wanting to return to it, that state described in the reports would, in some ways, make life easier for Liam Carter. Caden often worried, whenever he saw Liam Carter's gentle nature, how someone so bright and innocent would survive in this harsh world.

In that sense, if he lived with the personality from the reports, Caden wouldn't have to worry about him being swindled somewhere, at least. Though he wouldn't be as cute as he was now.

Caden frowned, reining in his wandering thoughts. None of his hypotheses were clear. First, he would meet Liam and see for himself; then, something would surely become clear.

Caden felt the carriage come to a stop, signaling their arrival at the destination, and he uncrossed his arms. It was time to hear the scoundrel's excuses.

It wasn't difficult to find Liam Carter at the entrance of the apartment building, which lacked any security measures. The fellow seemed to be about to jump from a fifth-story window, with one foot already on the sill. The sight was utterly absurd. *What in the world is he doing?*

"D-Duke."

The fellow, having spotted Caden, called out in surprise, his mouth agape. Last night, he had been perfectly fine calling Caden by his given name, but it seemed he had come to his senses now.

Seeing him with his foot on the window and his body leaning out, as if about to jump, was simply preposterous. A mage wouldn't die from falling five stories. He would instinctively use magic to land safely once he was halfway down.

Still, Caden didn't want to see him get hurt for no reason, so he made a hand gesture signaling that he would kill him if he jumped. But seeing Liam just gape and tilt his head, it didn't seem like he understood.

First, Caden gestured for his subordinates to go up ahead, then slowly ascended to the fifth floor himself. Residents, peeking out their doors due to the sudden commotion, quickly recoiled and hid behind their doors like mimosa plants touched by a hand when their eyes met his.
