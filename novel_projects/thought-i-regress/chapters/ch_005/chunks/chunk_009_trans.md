Seeing Liam babbling things his partner couldn't understand, it seemed he didn't even know what he was saying under the influence of alcohol. At his tear-streaked face, saying, "I'm going back," Caden squeezed his eyes shut and opened them again. *Damn it, that face again.*

Whenever Liam got teary-eyed like that, Caden somehow couldn't refuse his requests. As Caden resigned himself and gave in, Liam didn't hesitate to cling to him again.

But the tears, once started, showed no sign of stopping. Although the sight of Liam's face, tear-streaked as he thrust into him, was pleasing, Caden couldn't just let him keep crying. He gently cupped Liam's face and lightly brushed his thumb over his eyelids, wiping away the tears.

Feeling that touch, Liam stopped moving. He clutched the warm hand on his face, unable to stop his flowing tears, and murmured,

"I don't want to go back..."

"Then don't go back. Do you think I can't take responsibility for just one person like you?"

"No. No, Caden. But, I..."

At Caden's words, Liam bit his lip, his face filled with sorrow. He couldn't finish his sentence, only murmuring repeatedly that he didn't want to go back. Faced with the drunkard's inexplicable sadness, Caden could only wonder and try to soothe his young lover.

And then, the next day. Waking later than usual after the passionate lovemaking that had begun the previous afternoon, Caden felt his head spin at Liam's absence, unable to find him anywhere in the house.

"They say even monkeys fall from trees, and eagles fail in their hunt. It seems such a day has befallen Your Grace as well."

"Silence, Jackson."

Caden glared at his aide, his eyes narrowed menacingly. At his superior's sharp rebuke, Jackson, Caden's long-time aide, merely shrugged.

Jackson's indifferent attitude always had a remarkable knack for provoking a tsunami of anger in anyone who witnessed it. Caden ground his teeth, toying with the inkwell on his desk.

He slowly took a deep breath, calming his anger, to prevent himself from throwing the inkwell and cracking the head of his competent but irritating aide.

Ignoring his superior's patience with an unconcerned gaze, Jackson politely handed Caden the documents in his hand. Caden asked, slowly enunciating his words as if chewing them.

"What is it?"

"Documents Your Grace postponed while enjoying himself yesterday."

"...As I always say, Jackson, you should be grateful for your abilities."

"Thank you for the advice, Your Grace."

At the aide's insolent behavior, placing a hand politely over his left chest and offering empty thanks for the advice, Caden felt as if he truly might throw the ink bottle. He pushed the ink bottle far away and snatched the documents with an enraged gesture. They were papers concerning the territory's taxes for the last quarter.
