The Baron smiled quietly as he recounted an old story.

“At a murder scene, Blam clam perfume was found, and everyone was desperate to detect Hess, but no trace of Hess was found, so there was no decisive evidence to incriminate the prime suspect. As a new investigator re-examined the scene, focusing on the unusually yellow tint of the corpse, he discovered that Mestamin, not Hess, had been used. Mestamin breaks down in the body over time, leaving no trace, so the method was to use Blam clams to delay the investigation.”

The Baron said this, subtly boasting that he was that new investigator. Caden remembered smiling faintly at his advice and saying he would keep it in mind for his future career as an investigator.

However, the Baron’s words were merely a warning to be wary of tricks that exploited common sense, not a principle to be applied to something that defied common sense like this.

His head began to ache from trying to ponder something so absurd, and he was frowning when a worried voice suddenly came from across the room. Startled, he forgot all his thoughts and turned his head.

“Your Grace, are you feeling unwell?”

“What?”

At his sudden question, Caden blinked, wondering what he meant. Then, following Liam’s gaze, he realized Liam was asking about his sitting posture, and he involuntarily scowled.

This brat, seriously. Someone here is racking their brain, even coming up with absurd hypotheses because of him. No, isn’t the reason his posture is crooked in the first place because of this guy?

As this thought crossed his mind, he found himself involuntarily glaring at Liam with wide eyes. Only then did Liam seem to realize his mistake, bowing his head and cautiously trying to gauge Caden’s mood.

Caden glared for a while at the man who was nervously scratching his chair with his finger and trying to read his expression, then looked out the window again. He felt as if he was on the verge of grasping something, but Liam’s sudden interruption had broken his train of thought. He swallowed a sigh and continued to think.

It seemed difficult to find out more about what Liam meant by “I don’t want to go back” here. Logically, a place or a specific state was the most likely, but his instinct as an investigator, honed over years, warned him not to dismiss the option of time lightly.

Since no clear answer emerged, he decided to defer judgment on this for now. Instead, he decided to consider why Liam “couldn’t go back.”

It might be his hasty conclusion, but observing Liam, the current Liam seemed no different from yesterday. In this case, two possibilities existed: he went back, but Caden didn’t notice, or he didn’t go back.

However, for him to have gone back without Caden noticing, Liam was far too cheerful and happy now, especially considering how much he had cried yesterday about not wanting to go back.

Observing Liam, who was still trying to gauge Caden’s mood because of his slip of the tongue, Caden decided to assume that he hadn’t gone back for now. After all, if he had gone back, there was nothing Caden could do anyway.

Yesterday, he cried and said he didn’t want to go back, so going back wouldn’t be his own will. But he must have known he would definitely go back the next day, which is why he reacted that way.

However, judging by his confused and tearful reaction this morning, things didn’t seem to have played out as he expected yesterday. If so, why?

The most plausible explanation was that something unexpected happened, preventing him from going back. What could have happened yesterday that was different from his expectations? Liam’s sudden confession came to mind.

Liam, who had always been relaxed as if he knew the future, seemed surprised and taken aback when Caden accepted his confession yesterday, as if he had encountered something unexpected. Caden felt he needed to focus on this point.
